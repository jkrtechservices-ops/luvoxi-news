Is Naperville, IL a Good Place to Live? Pros and Cons

Naperville, IL blends suburban comfort with easy access to Chicago. With a 2020‑2024 median household income of $155,105 and a strong school system, it attracts families and professionals alike. The city offers extensive parks, commuter rail, and a historic downtown, but higher home values and rents can be a hurdle. Overall, it ranks among the nation’s top places to live, though personal priorities will determine if the pros outweigh the cons.

Population Size and State Ranking

The 2020 census recorded 149,540 residents, and the most recent American Community Survey (2020‑2024 5‑year) estimates a modest increase to 150,692, confirming that Naperville’s growth remains steady after the explosive expansion of the 1960s and 1970s. With a median age of 40.4 years, the population blends young families, mid‑career professionals, and a sizable retiree segment, creating a balanced community that feels both vibrant and stable. This size places Naperville as the fourth‑largest city in Illinois, trailing only Chicago, Aurora, and Rockford, yet it retains a small‑city atmosphere—walkable downtown streets, neighborhood parks, and a strong sense of local identity.

Economic indicators reinforce the city’s appeal. The median household income tops $155,000, well above state and national averages, reflecting the high‑skill jobs anchored by major employers such as Edward Hospital (≈4,500 staff) and a growing tech corridor. Housing data show that roughly three‑quarters of occupied units are owner‑occupied, with a median home value of $540,200 and a median gross rent of $1,885, underscoring both investment potential and a relatively affluent resident base.

Geographically, Naperville straddles DuPage and Will counties, sitting 28 miles west of Chicago along the DuPage River. Its strategic location is bolstered by Interstate 88 and Metra’s BNSF commuter line, providing easy access to the Chicago metropolitan job market while preserving the city’s own robust amenities—extensive parks, cultural landmarks like the Millennium Carillon, and top‑rated public schools (districts 203 and 204). This combination of size, prosperity, and connectivity makes Naperville a distinctive mid‑sized hub within the state.

Economic Drivers and Major Employers

Naperville’s economy is buoyed by a mix of health‑care, technology, and professional services that benefit from the city’s strategic location and affluent resident base. The single largest employer, Edward Hospital, provides roughly 4,500 jobs in health care, research, and ancillary support, anchoring the city’s medical corridor and attracting related firms such as biotech startups and medical‑device manufacturers.

The city’s position 28 miles west of Chicago along Interstate 88 and the Metra BNSF commuter line makes it a prime hub for companies that need easy access to the Chicago market while enjoying lower operating costs. Corporate campuses, financial‑services firms, and consulting agencies have clustered near the I‑88 corridor and the Naperville Metra station (105 E. Fourth Ave.), leveraging the hourly rail service that links downtown Chicago to the suburbs.

A median household income of $155,105 (2020‑2024 ACS) reflects the purchasing power of residents, sustaining a vibrant downtown filled with boutique retailers, upscale restaurants, and professional service firms. This wealth also underpins a robust real‑estate market—about 75 % of housing units are owner‑occupied, with a median home value of $540,200 and median gross rent of $1,885—providing a stable tax base for municipal infrastructure and public amenities.

Together, these factors create a diversified economic ecosystem that not only offers plentiful employment opportunities but also reinforces Naperville’s reputation as a desirable place to live and work.

Transportation Options for Commuters

Commuters to Chicago have multiple choices. Metra’s BNSF Line runs hourly from Chicago Union Station to Naperville, offering a direct rail link for workers and students. Amtrak also stops at the downtown station on 105 E. Fourth Ave., providing longer‑distance travel. For drivers, Interstate 88 cuts across the northern part of the city, delivering quick east‑west highway access. Together, these options make daily trips to the city feasible without relying solely on a car.

Recreational Amenities and Community Events

Naperville boasts an extensive parks and forest preserve network, highlighted by Centennial Beach, a historic swimming venue. The downtown area features the Moser Tower and the Millennium Carillon, one of the world’s four largest carillons, adding cultural flair. Throughout the year, the city hosts festivals, farmers markets, and concerts that draw residents together. These amenities contribute to a high quality of life and provide plenty of options for outdoor activities and community engagement.

Schools and Education Districts

Education in Naperville is served by two public school districts, 203 and 204, which cover most of the city’s neighborhoods. Both districts operate a range of elementary, middle, and high schools, offering families choices close to home. While specific test scores are not listed, the presence of two dedicated districts and the city’s overall affluence suggest well‑funded schools and a strong emphasis on education. Many families cite the school system as a key factor in choosing to live here.

Frequently asked questions

What is the population of Naperville and how does it rank among Illinois cities?

Naperville had 149,540 residents in the 2020 census, making it the fourth‑most populous city in Illinois. Updated estimates place the population at 150,692.

What are the major employers and economic drivers in Naperville?

Edward Hospital is the largest employer with 4,500 staff. The city’s location along Interstate 88 and Metra’s BNSF Line also attracts businesses that benefit from easy Chicago access.

What transportation options are available for commuting to and from Chicago?

Metra’s BNSF Line provides hourly commuter rail service, and Amtrak stops at the downtown station. Interstate 88 offers direct highway access for drivers.

How do schools and education districts in Naperville compare to other suburbs?

Naperville is served by two public districts, 203 and 204, covering a full range of K‑12 schools. The city’s higher median income and strong community support suggest well‑funded schools compared with many other suburbs.

The bottom line

Naperville, IL offers strong earnings, quality schools, solid transit links, and abundant recreation, making it a compelling choice for many families and professionals. Weigh the higher housing costs against these benefits to decide if it fits your lifestyle.
