Frisco vs Houston: Which City Is Right for You?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 29, 2026 · 4 min read

Frisco and Houston offer very different living experiences. Frisco is a smaller, affluent suburb of Dallas with high incomes and home values, while Houston is a massive, diverse metropolis with lower costs and a broader job market. Understanding the numbers helps you match a city to your budget, family needs, and career goals.

Key takeaways
  • Frisco’s median income is more than double Houston’s.
  • Houston’s rent is about 25% of household income versus 16% in Frisco.
  • Home values in Frisco are $364,300 higher than in Houston.
  • Houston is ten times larger in population and land area.

Population and Size: How Big Is Each City?

Houston is the fourth‑most populous city in the United States with an estimated 2,328,253 residents, while Frisco’s population stands at 219,304. In plain numbers, Houston is 2,108,949 people larger, roughly a 962% difference. The land footprint reflects that gap: Houston covers 640.4 square miles, ranking as the ninth‑largest city by area, whereas Frisco occupies 62.4 square miles. The scale of Houston means more neighborhoods, a wider transit network, and a bustling downtown, while Frisco feels more like a tight‑knit community within the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex.

Income Levels: Who Earns More?

The median household income in Frisco is $150,212, which is $85,399 higher than Houston’s median of $64,813. This translates to Frisco earning about 132% more than the average Houston household. Higher earnings in Frisco often stem from its proximity to corporate headquarters and professional services in the Dallas area. Houston’s income reflects a broader mix of industries, including energy, healthcare, and aerospace, but on average it remains lower than Frisco’s affluent profile.

Rent Costs: What Will You Pay Monthly?

Rent in Frisco carries a premium. The median gross rent is $2,014 per month, compared with $1,361 in Houston. That $653 difference means Frisco rent is about 48% higher. However, when you compare rent to income, Frisco renters spend roughly 16% of their median household income on rent, while Houston renters allocate about 25%. For renters on a tighter budget, Houston’s lower rent-to‑income ratio may feel more manageable.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Buying a home in Frisco is a significant investment, but one that aligns with the city’s high‑earning, owner‑focused community. The median value of owner‑occupied homes sits at $642,100—roughly $364,300 (131 %) more than Houston’s median of $277,800. This price premium reflects Frisco’s median household income of $150,212, more than double Houston’s $64,813, and a population of 219,304 that is younger (median age 38.3) yet affluent enough to sustain higher property values. Approximately 66 % of Frisco’s occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating a strong preference for homeownership among residents.

In contrast, Houston’s massive population of 2,328,253 (about 962 % larger than Frisco) creates a broader, more varied housing market. With a median home value of $277,800, first‑time buyers can find entry‑level options that are out of reach in Frisco. However, only 42 % of Houston’s occupied units are owner‑occupied, suggesting a larger rental sector. Median gross rent in Houston is $1,361 per month—about 25 % of the city’s median household income—versus Frisco’s $2,014 rent, which represents roughly 16 % of its higher income. These figures illustrate that Frisco’s market is geared toward wealthier families seeking long‑term equity, while Houston offers more affordable pathways to homeownership and a sizable rental pool for those not ready to buy.

Affordability: Which City Fits Your Budget?

Affordability isn’t just about raw numbers; it’s about how those numbers relate to income. In Frisco, rent is 16% of median income, suggesting a comfortable margin for renters with higher earnings. Houston’s rent at 25% of median income indicates a tighter budget for many households. Home buyers in Frisco face higher purchase prices but also benefit from higher incomes and a larger share of owners. Houston provides lower entry costs but also lower average earnings, making it attractive for those prioritizing cost over income potential.

Lifestyle and Who Each City Suits Best

Frisco is known for its suburban feel, Tree City USA designation, and amenities like Stonebriar Centre and The Star, the Dallas Cowboys headquarters. Its median age of 38.3 years points to a mature, family‑oriented population. Professionals seeking high salaries, newer housing, and a smaller‑city vibe often gravitate to Frisco.

Houston, by contrast, is famous for its cultural diversity, energy sector, and expansive metropolitan amenities. With a median age of 34.4 years, the city leans slightly younger. Its vast size, lower housing costs, and broader job market appeal young professionals, artists, and families looking for more affordable options and a big‑city experience.

Frequently asked questions

Is Frisco or Houston cheaper to live in?

Houston is generally cheaper. Median rent is $1,361 versus $2,014 in Frisco, and home values are $277,800 compared with $642,100. However, Houston’s lower median income means a higher rent‑to‑income ratio.

Which has higher incomes, Frisco or Houston?

Frisco’s median household income is $150,212, which is $85,399 higher than Houston’s median of $64,813.

Is rent higher in Frisco or Houston?

Rent is higher in Frisco. The median gross rent is $2,014 per month, while Houston’s median is $1,361.

Which is better for families, Frisco or Houston?

Frisco often suits families seeking higher incomes, newer homes, and a suburban environment with a 66% owner‑occupancy rate. Houston offers more affordable housing and a wider range of cultural and recreational options for families on a tighter budget.

Is Frisco or Houston bigger?

Houston is much larger. It has a population of 2,328,253 compared with Frisco’s 219,304, making Houston about 962% larger in population and covering 640.4 square miles versus Frisco’s 62.4.

The bottom line

Frisco delivers high incomes, newer homes and a suburban feel at a premium price, while Houston offers scale, diversity and lower costs but with a lower average income. Match these trade‑offs to your financial situation and lifestyle preferences to choose the right city.
