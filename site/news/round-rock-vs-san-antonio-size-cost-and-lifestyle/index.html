Round Rock vs San Antonio: Size, Cost, and Lifestyle

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Round Rock and San Antonio offer very different living experiences. Round Rock is a midsize city with high incomes and higher housing costs, while San Antonio is a large metropolis with lower prices and a broader cultural scene. Your decision will hinge on how you weigh size, affordability, and what kind of community fits your lifestyle.

Key takeaways
  • Round Rock is much smaller but wealthier.
  • San Antonio costs less for rent and homes.
  • Both cities have similar homeownership rates.
  • Round Rock leans toward tech and families; San Antonio offers diverse culture.

Population and Physical Size

Round Rock’s population stands at 127,786, making it a compact community within the Greater Austin area. By contrast, San Antonio is home to 1,479,835 residents, a ten‑fold increase that places it among the nation’s largest cities. The sheer scale of San Antonio means more neighborhoods, a wider range of services, and a larger metropolitan footprint of 465.4 square miles. Round Rock’s smaller size often translates to shorter commutes and a tighter‑knit feel, while San Antonio’s size supports a bustling urban environment with many distinct districts.

Income Levels and Economic Base

The median household income in Round Rock tops $99,287—about $34,231 (53 %) higher than San Antonio’s $65,056. This disparity is rooted in Round Rock’s tech‑driven economy; Dell’s international headquarters alone employs roughly 16,000 people, anchoring a cluster of high‑paying software, engineering, and support jobs. With a median age of 36.2 years, the city’s workforce is relatively young and highly skilled, which helps sustain the higher earnings. In contrast, San Antonio’s economy is more diversified, leaning on military installations, tourism, and a large healthcare sector. Although its median age is slightly lower at 34.9 years, the broader mix of lower‑wage service and government positions keeps the median income modest.

Higher earnings in Round Rock do not translate into an unaffordable lifestyle. Median gross rent is $1,763 per month—about 21 % of the median income—while San Antonio’s median rent of $1,324 consumes roughly 24 % of its households’ earnings. Home values also reflect the income gap: the median owner‑occupied home in Round Rock costs $418,600, nearly $183,000 (78 %) more than San Antonio’s $235,700. Yet 56 % of Round Rock’s occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, only slightly above San Antonio’s 52 %, indicating that despite pricier properties, a comparable share of residents can afford to buy. Ultimately, Round Rock’s higher wages help offset its steeper housing costs, while San Antonio’s lower wages are balanced by more affordable rent and home prices.

Rent and Housing Costs

Rent in Round Rock averages $1,763 per month, roughly $439 more than San Antonio’s median of $1,324. As a share of income, Round Rock’s rent is about 21% of median household earnings, while San Antonio’s rent consumes about 24% of its median income. Home values follow a similar pattern: the median owner‑occupied home in Round Rock is $418,600, $182,900 higher than San Antonio’s $235,700. Prospective renters will find cheaper monthly bills in San Antonio, whereas homebuyers may need a larger budget in Round Rock.

Homeownership Rates and Affordability

Owner‑occupied housing makes up 56% of occupied units in Round Rock and 52% in San Antonio, indicating comparable homeownership cultures. Despite higher prices, Round Rock’s higher incomes keep its affordability ratio modest, with rent at 21% of earnings. San Antonio’s lower incomes push its rent ratio to 24%, but the lower absolute costs still make it the more affordable option for many. Those prioritizing home ownership should weigh the higher price tag in Round Rock against the slightly lower ownership rate in San Antonio.

What Each City Is Known For

Round Rock is best known for its tech hub, especially Dell’s massive campus, and retail destinations like IKEA, Premium Outlets, and the La Frontera mixed‑use center. Its historic Old Town preserves early Texas architecture, and the city’s location on the Balcones Escarpment offers easy access to both prairie and Hill Country landscapes. San Antonio, on the other hand, is celebrated for its Spanish colonial heritage, the iconic River Walk, and a vibrant mix of museums, military installations, and a strong tourism sector. The city’s size supports a wide array of cultural events, dining options, and recreational activities.

Who Each City Suits Best

Round Rock appeals to professionals seeking higher salaries, tech‑focused careers, and a suburban feel with easy access to Austin’s amenities. Its median age of 36.2 years suggests a population of young families and early‑career adults. San Antonio, with a median age of 34.9 years, attracts those who value cultural diversity, lower living costs, and a larger urban environment. Families may appreciate Round Rock’s quieter neighborhoods and schools, while families seeking more varied extracurricular options might lean toward San Antonio’s extensive community resources.

Frequently asked questions

Is Round Rock or San Antonio cheaper to live in?

San Antonio is cheaper overall. Its median rent is $1,324 versus $1,763 in Round Rock, and the median home value is $235,700 compared with $418,600.

Which has higher incomes, Round Rock or San Antonio?

Round Rock has higher median household income at $99,287, while San Antonio’s median is $65,056.

Is rent higher in Round Rock or San Antonio?

Rent is higher in Round Rock, with a median of $1,763 per month versus $1,324 in San Antonio.

Which is better for families, Round Rock or San Antonio?

Round Rock offers a smaller, suburban setting with higher incomes and strong tech employment, which many families prefer. San Antonio provides more cultural amenities and lower housing costs, which can also be attractive to families.

Is Round Rock or San Antonio bigger?

San Antonio is far larger, with a population of 1,479,835, compared to Round Rock’s 127,786.

The bottom line

Round Rock delivers higher earnings and a tech‑centric, suburban lifestyle at a premium price, while San Antonio offers a larger, culturally rich city with lower housing costs. Choose the city that aligns with your budget, career goals, and preferred community vibe.
