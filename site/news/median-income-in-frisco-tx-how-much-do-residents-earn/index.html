What Does the Median Income Figure Represent?

The median household income of $150,212 means half of Frisco households earn more and half earn less. It reflects the middle point of all reported incomes, not an average. This figure comes from the 2020-2024 American Community Survey, which aggregates data over five years to smooth out short‑term fluctuations. For residents, the number offers a benchmark for budgeting, mortgage qualification, and comparing Frisco to neighboring markets.

Because the median is less affected by extreme high or low incomes, it gives a realistic picture of typical earnings in the community. Families can use this data to gauge purchasing power, while businesses may assess the local market’s capacity for goods and services.

How Does Population Size Influence the Local Economy?

Frisco’s estimated population of 219,304 shapes demand for housing, retail, and public services. A larger population supports a broader tax base, which can fund infrastructure projects and community amenities. The size also attracts businesses seeking a sizable customer pool, contributing to job growth and potentially higher wages.

With more than two hundred thousand residents, the city can sustain diverse commercial districts, from dining corridors to professional offices. This scale often leads to a competitive job market, which can help maintain the high median income reported by the ACS.

What Do Housing Costs Look Like in Frisco?

The median gross rent in Frisco is $2,014 per month, according to the latest ACS data. This rent level reflects the city’s strong demand for rental units and its overall affluence. Homeownership is also prominent, with about 66% of occupied housing units owner‑occupied. The median value of owner‑occupied homes stands at $642,100, indicating a robust real estate market.

Renters and buyers alike should consider these figures when planning budgets. High rent and home values can affect disposable income, but they also signal a stable, desirable community that retains value over time.

How Does Age Demographics Impact Lifestyle Choices?

Frisco’s median age of 38.3 years places the city squarely in the prime‑working‑age bracket, a demographic that shapes almost every facet of daily life. With a population of 219,304, a substantial share of residents are juggling full‑time careers, school‑age children, and active community participation. This convergence drives a strong demand for high‑quality public schools and extracurricular programs, prompting the school district to invest heavily in modern facilities, STEM curricula, and after‑school sports leagues. At the same time, commuters value efficient transportation corridors; Frisco’s proximity to major highways and expanding commuter rail options reflects the need for reliable, time‑saving travel to Dallas‑area job centers.

The financial profile of this cohort reinforces those preferences. A median household income of $150,212 gives families the purchasing power to afford the city’s median home value of $642,100, while still allocating roughly 31 % of income to the median gross rent of $2,014 for those who lease. Because about 66 % of occupied units are owner‑occupied, developers prioritize single‑family homes with amenities such as home offices, playgrounds, and nearby walking trails—features that appeal to both young professionals and growing families.

Local businesses respond accordingly. Fitness centers offering flexible class schedules, child‑care‑friendly cafés, and professional services such as financial planning and legal counsel thrive, catering to residents who seek work‑life balance. Meanwhile, retail and dining concepts that blend convenience with family‑friendly atmospheres—think grocery‑store‑plus‑marketplaces and casual‑fine‑dining with kids’ menus—are especially successful in neighborhoods designed around this age‑driven lifestyle.

What Should Newcomers Expect When Moving to Frisco?

New residents can anticipate a community with strong earning power, as reflected by the $150,212 median household income. Housing costs are higher than many surrounding areas, with median rent at $2,014 and median home values at $642,100. However, the high owner‑occupancy rate of about 66% points to a stable, invested neighborhood.

The city’s sizable population of 219,304 offers a range of amenities, from shopping districts to parks. The relatively young median age of 38.3 years creates a dynamic environment with active social and professional networks, making Frisco an attractive option for families and professionals alike.
