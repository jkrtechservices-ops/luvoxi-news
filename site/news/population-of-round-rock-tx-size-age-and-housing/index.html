Current Population Size of Round Rock, TX

The U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey estimates Round Rock’s population at 127,786. This reflects growth since the 2020 census, which recorded 119,468 residents. The increase shows the city’s appeal within the Greater Austin metropolitan area, drawing new families and professionals each year.

Round Rock spans both Williamson and Travis counties, giving it a strategic position for commuters and businesses alike. Its proximity to Austin—just 15 miles north—makes it a convenient residential hub for those working in the state capital while preferring a smaller‑city feel.

Age Demographics: Median Age in Round Rock, TX

Round Rock’s median age is 36.2 years, according to the latest ACS data. This places the city in the early‑mid‑adult bracket, indicating a strong presence of working‑age adults and young families. The age profile supports a vibrant labor market and steady demand for schools, childcare, and family‑oriented services.

The city’s age distribution helps explain its economic vitality, as a younger median age often correlates with higher mobility, tech adoption, and community engagement. Local planners use this data to shape housing, transportation, and recreational projects that match residents’ life stages.

Housing Ownership and Rental Landscape in Round Rock, TX

About 56% of occupied housing units in Round Rock are owner‑occupied, showing a majority of residents prefer to own rather than rent. The median gross rent stands at $1,763 per month, while the median value of owner‑occupied homes is $418,600. These figures illustrate a balanced housing market where both renters and buyers find options.

The ownership rate suggests a stable community base, which can attract businesses seeking reliable local customers. Meanwhile, rental rates remain competitive for those who need flexibility, especially young professionals commuting to Austin or working at nearby corporate campuses.

Income and Economic Indicators for Round Rock, TX

Households in Round Rock earn a median income of $99,287 per year, according to the 2020‑2024 American Community Survey. This relatively high income level reflects the city’s robust job market, anchored by major employers such as Dell, whose international headquarters sits in Round Rock and employs roughly 16,000 workers. The tech sector’s presence, together with a growing number of health‑care, education, and manufacturing firms, has helped push earnings above the state average and supports a consumer base that fuels local retail, dining, and professional services.

Higher household earnings also translate into greater housing purchasing power. The median value of owner‑occupied homes stands at $418,600, and about 56 % of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating a stable, investment‑oriented community. For renters, the median gross rent is $1,763 per month, a figure that remains affordable relative to income levels but also signals a competitive rental market for newcomers.

Economic planners monitor these trends closely. Rising incomes have prompted the city to prioritize infrastructure upgrades—such as expanding roadways along State Highway 45 and enhancing public transit links to Austin—to accommodate a growing commuter population. Likewise, the city invests in community services, including schools and parks, that cater to a median resident age of 36.2 years. In sum, Round Rock’s strong earnings base underpins both its housing market and the broader quality‑of‑life initiatives that keep the city attractive to families and businesses alike.

Location, Transportation and Major Employers in Round Rock, TX

Round Rock sits roughly 15 miles (24 km) north of downtown Austin and shares a common border with the capital near State Highway 45, positioning the city as a strategic gateway between suburban life and the bustling Austin job market. The proximity to Austin‑Bergstrom International Airport, major interstates (I‑35 and Loop 45), and commuter rail lines gives residents and businesses swift access to regional transportation hubs, making daily commutes and freight movement remarkably efficient.

The city’s economic engine is anchored by Dell Technologies, whose international headquarters occupies a sprawling campus that employs about 16,000 workers. Dell’s presence has spurred a cluster of ancillary firms—including software developers, data‑center providers, and professional‑service agencies—creating a robust tech ecosystem that draws talent from across the Greater Austin metropolitan area. In addition to Dell, Round Rock hosts a mix of manufacturing plants, healthcare providers, and retail giants, further diversifying the employment base.
