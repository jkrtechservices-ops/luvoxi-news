Round Rock vs Arlington: Which Texas City Fits Your Lifestyle?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 18, 2026 · 4 min read

Round Rock and Arlington are two fast‑growing Texas cities that often appear side by side in relocation decisions. Round Rock, a suburb of Austin, offers higher incomes and larger homes but also higher rent, while Arlington, part of the Dallas‑Fort Worth metro area, provides lower costs and a bigger population. Understanding the numbers behind size, earnings, housing costs, and what each city is known for will help you choose the community that matches your budget and lifestyle.

Key takeaways
  • Round Rock earns about $24,000 more per household than Arlington.
  • Arlington’s median rent is roughly $300 lower than Round Rock’s.
  • Home values in Round Rock exceed Arlington’s by over $110,000.
  • Arlington is three times larger in population than Round Rock.

Population and Size: How Big Are the Cities?

Arlington’s estimated population of 397,742 dwarfs Round Rock’s 127,786. In other words, Arlington is about 269,956 residents larger, roughly a 211% increase over Round Rock. This size difference influences everything from traffic patterns to the variety of services available. Round Rock feels more like a midsize suburb, while Arlington functions as a major urban hub within the Dallas‑Fort Worth metro area.

Household Income: Which City Pays More?

Round Rock’s median household income of $99,287 is a full $24,116 (about 32 %) higher than Arlington’s $75,171. This gap reflects the city’s concentration of high‑paying employers, most notably Dell’s international headquarters, which alone employs roughly 16,000 people in the area. With a population of 127,786, Round Rock’s earnings are spread across a relatively small community, helping to keep per‑capita income robust. In contrast, Arlington’s larger population of 397,742 dilutes its median income, even though the city benefits from major employers such as the General Motors Assembly plant, the University of Texas at Arlington, and the Nuclear Regulatory Commission’s Region IV office.

Higher wages in Round Rock also translate into a cost‑of‑living premium. The median gross rent is $1,763 per month—about 20 % more than Arlington’s $1,470, yet it still represents roughly 21 % of the city’s median household income, slightly lower than Arlington’s 23 % rent‑to‑income ratio. Home‑ownership follows the same pattern: the median value of owner‑occupied homes in Round Rock is $418,600, roughly $113,900 (37 %) above Arlington’s $304,700. Despite the higher price tag, about 56 % of occupied housing units in Round Rock are owner‑occupied, only a couple of points above Arlington’s 54 % rate, indicating a similarly strong appetite for home ownership in both markets.

For prospective residents, the income differential suggests that Round Rock may offer more lucrative job opportunities and higher purchasing power, while Arlington provides a more affordable housing landscape without sacrificing access to major employers and amenities.

Rent Costs: What Will You Pay Monthly?

The median gross rent in Round Rock is $1,763 per month, while Arlington’s median rent is $1,470. That makes Round Rock’s rent $293, or roughly 20%, higher than Arlington’s. Both cities keep rent at a similar share of household income—about 21% in Round Rock and 23% in Arlington—so renters in each market feel a comparable burden relative to earnings.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Owner‑occupied homes in Round Rock have a median value of $418,600, which is $113,900, or about 37%, higher than Arlington’s median of $304,700. About 56% of occupied housing units in Round Rock are owner‑occupied, compared with 54% in Arlington. The higher home prices in Round Rock reflect its proximity to Austin and the demand for larger, newer properties.

Affordability: Balancing Income and Housing Costs

Even though Round Rock’s rent and home values are higher, its residents also earn more, keeping rent at roughly 21% of median income. Arlington’s lower rent and home prices are offset by a lower median income, resulting in rent that consumes about 23% of household earnings. For buyers, the price gap of $113,900 may be a deciding factor, while renters might favor Arlington’s cheaper monthly cost.

What Each City Is Known For and Who It Suits Best

Round Rock is best known for being the headquarters of Dell and for its retail destinations like IKEA, Premium Outlets, and the La Frontera mixed‑use center. Its historic Old Town and location near Austin appeal to families and professionals who want suburban space with easy access to a larger city. Arlington, by contrast, hosts the University of Texas at Arlington, a major urban research university, the Arlington Assembly plant for General Motors, and the Nuclear Regulatory Commission Region IV. Its larger population, university presence, and lower housing costs make it attractive to students, young professionals, and families seeking more affordable living within a big‑city environment.

Frequently asked questions

Is Round Rock or Arlington cheaper to live in?

Arlington is generally cheaper. Its median rent is $1,470 versus $1,763 in Round Rock, and home values are $304,700 compared with $418,600 in Round Rock.

Which has higher incomes, Round Rock or Arlington?

Round Rock has higher median household income at $99,287, while Arlington’s median is $75,171.

Is rent higher in Round Rock or Arlington?

Rent is higher in Round Rock. The median gross rent is $1,763 per month, compared with $1,470 in Arlington.

Which is better for families, Round Rock or Arlington?

Round Rock’s higher income levels, larger homes, and proximity to Austin’s amenities make it appealing for families seeking space and jobs. Arlington offers lower housing costs and a university environment, which may suit families prioritizing affordability.

Is Round Rock or Arlington bigger?

Arlington is larger, with a population of 397,742 versus Round Rock’s 127,786.

The bottom line

Round Rock delivers higher earnings and larger homes at a higher price, ideal for those who value proximity to Austin and corporate jobs. Arlington offers a bigger city feel, lower housing costs, and a strong university presence, fitting renters and buyers who prioritize affordability. Weigh income, rent, home values, and lifestyle preferences to decide which city aligns with your next chapter.
