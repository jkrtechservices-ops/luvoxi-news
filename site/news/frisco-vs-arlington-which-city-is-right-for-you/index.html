Frisco vs Arlington: Which City Is Right for You?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 28, 2026 · 5 min read

Representative stock image via Pixabay, not a photo of the event.

ShareWhatsAppXFacebook

Frisco and Arlington are both fast‑growing Texas cities within the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex, but they differ sharply in population size, earnings, housing costs and overall vibe. Frisco boasts a smaller, wealthier community with higher home prices, while Arlington offers a larger, more affordable urban environment. Understanding these contrasts helps you choose the city that matches your budget and lifestyle.

Key takeaways
  • Frisco’s median income is double Arlington’s.
  • Home values in Frisco exceed Arlington’s by over $300,000.
  • Arlington’s rent is about 23% of household income; Frisco’s is 16%.
  • Frisco is better for affluent families; Arlington suits younger professionals.

Population and Physical Size

Frisco’s 2020‑2024 ACS estimate puts its population at 219,304. Arlington is considerably larger, with an estimated 397,742 residents—about 178,438 (81%) more than Frisco. Frisco covers 62.4 square miles, almost all land, while Arlington’s exact land area isn’t listed but it serves as the third‑largest city in the Dallas‑Fort Worth metro area. The size difference means Arlington feels more urban, whereas Frisco offers a suburban spread with a median resident age of 38.3 years compared to Arlington’s 33.7 years.

Income Levels and What They Mean

The median household income in Frisco is $150,212—exactly double Arlington’s $75,171. This stark gap gives Frisco residents considerably more discretionary spending power, which is evident in the city’s higher home values and premium amenities. The median value of an owner‑occupied home in Frisco is $642,100, more than $337,000 (111 %) above Arlington’s $304,700, and about 66 % of Frisco’s housing units are owner‑occupied compared with just 54 % in Arlington. Because rent in Frisco averages $2,014 per month—roughly 16 % of the median household income—renters still enjoy a relatively affordable proportion of earnings, whereas Arlington’s median rent of $1,470 consumes about 23 % of its median income.

These financial dynamics shape each city’s lifestyle. Frisco’s higher incomes attract professionals and families seeking upscale shopping, fine‑dining, and top‑rated schools, and the city’s 38.3‑year median age reflects a slightly older, more established population. Arlington, with a median age of 33.7 years, draws a younger, career‑focused crowd, many of whom are tied to the University of Texas at Arlington, the GM Assembly plant, or other regional employers. The lower cost of housing and rent in Arlington supports a more modest, middle‑class lifestyle, making it appealing for first‑time homebuyers and renters who prioritize affordability over luxury. Ultimately, your decision may hinge on whether you value the purchasing power and upscale environment of Frisco or the relative affordability and youthful energy of Arlington.

Rent Costs and Affordability Ratios

Frisco’s median gross rent is $2,014 per month, which is $544 (37%) higher than Arlington’s $1,470. However, when measured against household income, Frisco renters spend about 16% of their earnings on rent, while Arlington renters allocate roughly 23%. The lower rent‑to‑income ratio in Frisco suggests that, despite higher absolute rent, residents’ higher wages make housing feel more affordable. Arlington’s lower rent still represents a larger slice of income for many households.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Owner‑occupied homes in Frisco command a median value of $642,100, more than double Arlington’s $304,700 median—an $337,400 (111%) gap. This stark price differential reflects the broader economic picture: Frisco’s median household income is $150,212, roughly twice Arlington’s $75,171. As a result, the city attracts higher‑earning professionals and investors who can afford larger, newer homes in master‑planned neighborhoods. In contrast, Arlington’s more modest home values cater to first‑time buyers and families seeking entry‑level ownership options.

Homeownership rates reinforce this divide. About 66% of Frisco’s occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, compared with 54% in Arlington. The higher ownership percentage in Frisco aligns with its affluent demographic and the prevalence of single‑family subdivisions that encourage long‑term residence. Arlington’s lower rate is partially driven by a younger median age (33.7 years versus Frisco’s 38.3) and a larger rental market; the median gross rent in Frisco is $2,014 per month—about 16% of its median income—while Arlington’s $1,470 rent represents roughly 23% of its median income, making renting a more attractive short‑term option for many residents.

Together, these figures illustrate two distinct housing ecosystems: Frisco’s market is geared toward wealthier buyers looking for high‑value, owner‑occupied properties, whereas Arlington offers more affordable pathways to homeownership and a robust rental sector for moderate‑income households.

What Each City Is Known For

Frisco is celebrated for rapid growth—named the fastest‑growing U.S. city in 2017—and its Tree City USA designation since 2003. Its proximity to Dallas Love Field and DFW Airport (about 25 miles) supports commuters and travelers. Arlington, part of the Mid‑Cities region, hosts the University of Texas at Arlington, a major research university, and major employers like General Motors’ Assembly plant and the Nuclear Regulatory Commission Region IV headquarters. These anchors shape Arlington’s identity as an industrial and academic hub.

Who Thrives Best in Each City

Frisco’s high incomes, larger homes and family‑friendly suburbs make it a strong fit for affluent families seeking spacious living and a higher standard of amenities. Its median age of 38.3 suggests a mature, settled population. Arlington’s lower cost of living, younger median age of 33.7, and presence of a large university and manufacturing jobs attract younger professionals, recent graduates, and families looking for more affordable housing options without sacrificing access to urban amenities.

Frequently asked questions

Is Frisco or Arlington cheaper to live in?

Arlington is cheaper overall. Its median rent is $1,470 versus $2,014 in Frisco, and home values average $304,700 compared with $642,100 in Frisco.

Which has higher incomes, Frisco or Arlington?

Frisco’s median household income is $150,212, about $75,041 higher than Arlington’s median of $75,171.

Is rent higher in Frisco or Arlington?

Rent is higher in Frisco at $2,014 per month, compared with $1,470 in Arlington.

Which is better for families, Frisco or Arlington?

Frisco tends to suit families with higher incomes who want larger homes and suburban amenities, while Arlington offers more affordable options for families on a tighter budget.

Is Frisco or Arlington bigger?

Arlington is bigger, with an estimated population of 397,742, which is about 81% higher than Frisco’s 219,304 residents.

The bottom line

In short, Frisco delivers wealth, larger homes and a family‑centric suburb, while Arlington offers a larger, more affordable city with strong university and industrial ties. Match your budget, career stage and lifestyle preferences to these core differences to pick the right Texas city for your next move.
