Plano vs Katy: Which Texas Suburb Fits Your Lifestyle?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Plano and Katy are two Texas suburbs that often appear on relocation lists. Plano is a large Dallas‑Fort Worth suburb with a population of 290,594, while Katy is a smaller Houston‑area city of 25,184. Both report median household incomes above $110,000, but they differ in housing costs, ownership rates and overall vibe. This guide breaks down the key metrics so you can see which community aligns with your priorities.

Key takeaways

  • Median income is slightly higher in Katy.
  • Rent and home values cost more in Plano.
  • Homeownership is more common in Katy.

Size and Population

Plano’s estimated population of 290,594 makes it the ninth most‑populous city in Texas and far larger than Katy’s 25,184 residents. In raw terms, Plano is about 265,410 people larger, a difference of roughly 1,054 percent. The larger population supports a broader range of services, retail options and cultural amenities typical of a principal Dallas‑Fort Worth suburb. Katy’s smaller size creates a more intimate community feel, with a median age of 37.6 years compared with Plano’s 39.6 years, suggesting a slightly younger resident base.

Median Household Income and Affordability

Both cities report strong earnings. Plano’s median household income stands at $112,253, while Katy’s is $114,912, about $2,659—or 2 percent—higher. When you compare rent to income, Plano’s median rent of $1,841 equals roughly 20 percent of its median income. In Katy, median rent of $1,602 represents about 17 percent of household earnings. The lower rent‑to‑income ratio in Katy indicates a modestly more affordable rental market, even though both cities sit well above national averages.

Housing Costs: Rent and Home Values

Renters will notice a clear price gap between the two suburbs. According to the 2020‑2024 American Community Survey, Plano’s median gross rent is $1,841 per month, which is $239—or about 15 percent—higher than Katy’s median of $1,602. When you translate those figures into a share of household income, the difference becomes even more meaningful: the median rent in Plano represents roughly 20 percent of its median household income ($112,253), while in Katy it accounts for about 17 percent of the median income ($114,912). This suggests that, despite Katy’s slightly higher earnings, housing costs take a smaller bite out of a typical Katy budget.

Homebuyers face a similar premium in Plano. The median value of owner‑occupied homes stands at $465,900, which is $64,200—approximately 16 percent—more than Katy’s median home price of $401,700. Ownership patterns reinforce the contrast: about 81 percent of occupied housing units in Katy are owner‑occupied, compared with only 57 percent in Plano, where a larger share of residents rent. The higher home values in Plano are partly offset by its larger, more diversified economy, anchored by corporate headquarters such as Frito‑Lay, JCPenney and Toyota Motor North America, and a population of 290,594 versus Katy’s 25,184. For those weighing cost against amenities, Plano commands a premium on both rentals and purchases, while Katy offers comparatively lower housing expenses relative to its residents’ incomes.

Homeownership Rates

Ownership patterns diverge sharply between the two suburbs, and the numbers tell a story about lifestyle and stability. In Plano, roughly 57 % of the 290,594 residents live in owner‑occupied units, while the remaining 43 % rent. The median value of a Plano home sits at $465,900—about $64,200 (16 %) higher than in Katy—and the median gross rent is $1,841 per month, roughly 20 % of the city’s median household income of $112,253. This higher rent‑to‑income ratio can make renting feel less affordable for newcomers, but it also reflects Plano’s larger, more diverse housing market that accommodates a sizable transient workforce drawn by corporate headquarters such as Frito‑Lay and Toyota.

Katy, by contrast, boasts an 81 % owner‑occupancy rate among its 25,184 residents, signaling a strong preference for homeownership. The median home price in Katy is $401,700, and the median rent is $1,602—about 17 % of the median household income of $114,912, making renting comparatively cheaper. The higher ownership share often translates into longer‑term residents, tighter neighborhood bonds, and more consistent property upkeep. For families seeking stability and a community‑oriented atmosphere, Katy’s ownership‑heavy profile can be especially appealing. Meanwhile, Plano’s mix of renters and owners offers greater flexibility for those who prioritize job mobility or prefer not to commit to a mortgage, albeit at a slightly higher cost of living.

What Each City Is Known For

Plano is recognized as a major corporate hub within the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex. It hosts the headquarters of companies such as Frito‑Lay, JCPenney, Pizza Hut and Toyota Motor North America, giving the city a robust job market tied to corporate and tech sectors. Katy, by contrast, grew out of a historic railroad town and is now part of the Houston metropolitan area. Its identity is linked to the Interstate 10 corridor and a legacy of agriculture, especially rice farming, before suburban expansion in the 1960s reshaped the landscape.

Who Each City Suits Best

If you prioritize career opportunities in large corporations, a wide array of dining and shopping options, and don’t mind a higher cost of living, Plano’s scale and corporate presence make it a strong fit. Families or individuals seeking a tighter community, higher homeownership rates and slightly lower rent relative to income may lean toward Katy. The smaller population and younger median age also appeal to those who prefer a less crowded environment while still enjoying proximity to Houston’s job market.

Frequently asked questions

Is Plano or Katy cheaper to live in?

Katy generally costs less. Its median rent is $1,602 versus Plano’s $1,841, and its median home value is $401,700 compared with $465,900 in Plano. The rent‑to‑income ratio is also lower in Katy.

Which has higher incomes, Plano or Katy?

Katy’s median household income is $114,912, about $2,659 higher than Plano’s $112,253, making Katy’s earnings roughly 2 percent greater.

Is rent higher in Plano or Katy?

Rent is higher in Plano. The median gross rent is $1,841, which is $239 more than Katy’s median of $1,602.

Which is better for families, Plano or Katy?

Katy’s higher homeownership rate (81 %) and lower rent‑to‑income ratio often appeal to families seeking stability and affordability. Plano offers more schools and amenities due to its size, which can also benefit families.

Is Plano or Katy bigger?

Plano is much bigger, with a population of 290,594 compared to Katy’s 25,184. That makes Plano about 265,410 people larger, or roughly 1,054 % more populous.

The bottom line

Plano and Katy each bring distinct advantages: Plano offers a larger corporate economy and extensive amenities at a higher price, while Katy provides a more affordable, home‑owner‑focused environment with a tighter community feel. Match these traits to your budget, career goals and lifestyle preferences to choose the right Texas suburb for you.
