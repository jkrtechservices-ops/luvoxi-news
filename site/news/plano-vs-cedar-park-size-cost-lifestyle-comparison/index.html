Plano vs Cedar Park: Size, Cost & Lifestyle Comparison

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Representative stock image via Pixabay, not a photo of the event.

ShareWhatsAppXFacebook

Plano and Cedar Park are two fast‑growing Texas suburbs that often appear on relocation lists. Plano is a large Dallas‑area city with a strong corporate base, while Cedar Park sits just northwest of Austin and offers a slightly higher median income. Both have similar rent levels, but home values, ownership rates and overall size differ enough to influence which community suits a given lifestyle.

Key takeaways
  • Plano is three times larger in population than Cedar Park.
  • Cedar Park’s median household income is about 15% higher.
  • Home values are roughly $48k higher in Cedar Park.
  • Owner‑occupancy is higher in Cedar Park (67% vs 57%).
  • Both cities have comparable rent‑to‑income ratios.

Population and Physical Size

Plano’s 2020‑2024 ACS estimate puts its population at 290,594, making it the ninth most‑populous city in Texas. Cedar Park is home to 78,301 people, a fraction of Plano’s size. In terms of land area, Plano covers 71.6 square miles, while Cedar Park spans 25.6 square miles. The result is a city that feels much larger and more urban in Plano, versus a tighter‑knit community in Cedar Park.

Income Levels and What They Mean

The median household income in Plano is $112,253 per year. Cedar Park’s median household income is $129,545, about $17,292 (15%) higher than Plano’s. Higher incomes in Cedar Park can translate into greater purchasing power, especially for families seeking higher‑earning jobs in the Austin metro area. Plano’s income level still ranks well above national averages, reflecting its corporate headquarters and professional job market.

Rent Costs and Affordability

Median gross rent in Plano is $1,841 per month, while Cedar Park’s median rent is $1,846. The difference is only $5, essentially equal. When measured against income, rent consumes about 20% of Plano’s median household income and roughly 17% of Cedar Park’s. Both cities keep rent below the commonly cited 30% affordability threshold, but Cedar Park’s slightly lower rent‑to‑income ratio makes it marginally more affordable for renters.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Owner‑occupied homes in Plano have a median value of $465,900, while Cedar Park’s median sits at $513,600—about $47,700, or 10 percent, higher. This price gap reflects Cedar Park’s higher median household income of $129,545, which is $17,292 (roughly 15 percent) above Plano’s $112,253. Because residents in Cedar Park earn more, the median gross rent of $1,846 per month represents only about 17 percent of their income, compared with Plano’s rent of $1,841—about 20 percent of its median income. The affordability advantage of renting in Cedar Park is modest, but it underscores the city’s stronger purchasing power.

Home‑ownership is also more prevalent in Cedar Park. Approximately 67 percent of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, versus 57 percent in Plano. The higher ownership rate aligns with Cedar Park’s slightly older median age of 37.7 years (Plano’s median age is 39.6) and its smaller, more suburban character—78,301 residents spread across 25.6 square miles, compared with Plano’s 290,594 people living in 71.6 square miles. For prospective buyers, Cedar Park demands a larger upfront investment but offers a higher likelihood of joining the majority of homeowners. In Plano, the lower median home price may be attractive to first‑time buyers, yet the lower ownership rate suggests a larger rental market and potentially more turnover in neighborhoods.

What Each City Is Known For

Plano is a principal suburb of the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex and hosts corporate headquarters such as Frito‑Lay, JCPenney, Pizza Hut and Toyota Motor North America. Its history stretches back to the 1840s and it is the largest city in Collin County. Cedar Park is a suburb of Austin, known for its proximity to the state capital and a historic settlement that dates to around 5,000 BC. It offers easy access to Austin’s tech and cultural scene while retaining a smaller‑town feel.

Who Each City Suits Best

Plano’s size, corporate base and larger housing stock make it attractive to professionals seeking a metropolitan feel with many employment options. Its slightly lower home prices and higher rental inventory also appeal to newcomers and renters. Cedar Park’s higher incomes, higher home ownership rate and proximity to Austin attract families and individuals who value a tighter community, higher earnings potential and a short commute to Austin’s job market.

Bottom Line Differences

In short, Plano is a much larger city with a strong corporate presence, lower median home values and a slightly lower median income. Cedar Park is smaller, richer on average, has higher home prices, and a higher share of owners. Rent is virtually identical, but Cedar Park’s rent‑to‑income ratio is a touch more favorable. Choose Plano for scale and corporate jobs; choose Cedar Park for higher earnings, higher home‑ownership rates and closer ties to Austin.

Frequently asked questions

Is Plano or Cedar Park cheaper to live in?

Rent is almost the same in both cities, but home values are about $48,000 lower in Plano, making home purchase generally cheaper there.

Which has higher incomes, Plano or Cedar Park?

Cedar Park’s median household income is $129,545, roughly $17,292 (15%) higher than Plano’s $112,253.

Is rent higher in Plano or Cedar Park?

Cedar Park’s median rent is $1,846, just $5 more than Plano’s $1,841, so the difference is negligible.

Which is better for families, Plano or Cedar Park?

Cedar Park’s higher home‑ownership rate (67% vs 57%) and proximity to Austin’s schools and jobs often make it a strong choice for families, while Plano offers more amenities and a larger job market.

Is Plano or Cedar Park bigger?

Plano is larger, with a population of 290,594 compared to Cedar Park’s 78,301, and it covers 71.6 square miles versus 25.6 square miles.

The bottom line

Both Plano and Cedar Park provide solid Texas suburban living, but they cater to different priorities. Plano offers size, corporate jobs and lower home prices, while Cedar Park delivers higher incomes, greater home‑ownership and a closer connection to Austin. Weigh these factors against your work, family and budget needs to decide which fits best.
