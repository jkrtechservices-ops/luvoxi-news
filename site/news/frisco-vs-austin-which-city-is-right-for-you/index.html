Frisco vs Austin: Which City Is Right for You?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 01, 2026 · 4 min read

Frisco and Austin are two fast‑growing Texas cities that attract different kinds of movers. Frisco offers higher incomes and a suburban feel within the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex, while Austin provides a larger urban environment with a lower cost of rent. Understanding the numbers—population, earnings, housing costs—and what each city is known for helps you choose the best fit.

  • Frisco 66% owners, Austin 43% owners
  • Austin is four times larger by population

Population and Size: How Big Are They?

Frisco’s estimated population is 219,304, while Austin’s is 979,539. In other words, Austin is about 760,235 people larger—roughly 347% higher than Frisco. Frisco covers 62.4 square miles, most of it land, and sits about 25 miles from Dallas Love Field and DFW Airport. Austin spreads across 305.1 square miles, includes several lakes, and sits 182 miles south of Dallas. The size difference means Austin feels like a major metropolitan hub, whereas Frisco feels more like a large suburb within the Dallas‑Fort Worth area.

Income Levels: Who Earns More?

The median household income in Frisco is $150,212 per year, which is $56,554—or about 60%—higher than Austin’s median of $93,658. Higher earnings in Frisco reflect a concentration of professional and corporate jobs linked to the Dallas‑Fort Worth economy. Austin’s income, while lower, still exceeds many national averages and is supported by a growing tech and creative sector. If a higher paycheck is a priority, Frisco’s numbers stand out.

Rent Costs and Affordability

Frisco’s median gross rent is $2,014 per month, about $285—or 16%—more than Austin’s $1,729. When you compare rent to income, Frisco renters spend roughly 16% of the median household income on rent, while Austin renters spend about 22%. Although Frisco’s rent is higher in absolute terms, it consumes a smaller slice of residents’ earnings, making it relatively more affordable for those with higher incomes.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

The median value of owner‑occupied homes in Frisco stands at $642,100—about $86,800, or 16 %, higher than Austin’s median of $555,300. This price gap reflects Frisco’s higher purchasing power: the city’s median household income is $150,212, roughly 60 % above Austin’s $93,658. Consequently, Frisco’s residents can more comfortably absorb higher home prices, and the city’s rent‑to‑income ratio (median rent $2,014, about 16 % of income) is lower than Austin’s (median rent $1,729, about 22 % of income). Homeownership rates further illustrate the contrast. Approximately 66 % of Frisco’s occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, compared with just 43 % in Austin. The higher ownership share signals a community oriented toward long‑term settlement, often families and professionals seeking stability. Austin’s larger rental market, driven by a younger median age (34.7 vs. 38.3 in Frisco) and a population nearly five times larger (979,539 vs. 219,304), attracts more transient residents, students, and tech workers. These dynamics shape each city’s character: Frisco offers a suburban, family‑friendly environment with higher‑priced but more affordable‑relative homes, while Austin provides a vibrant, rental‑heavy landscape where housing costs, though lower in absolute terms, consume a larger slice of household income.

What Each City Is Known For

Frisco is part of the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex, known for rapid growth, a strong corporate presence, and a reputation as a family‑oriented suburb. It has been the fastest‑growing city in the nation at times and holds a Tree City USA designation. Austin, the state capital, is famous for its live‑music scene, tech startups, and outdoor recreation along the Colorado River and its lakes. Its cultural cachet draws artists, entrepreneurs, and young professionals.

Who Thrives Best in Each City

Frisco suits families and high‑earning professionals who value homeownership, good schools, and a suburban lifestyle with easy access to Dallas. Its higher income levels and higher owner‑occupancy rate support that profile. Austin appeals to young adults, creatives, and tech workers who prefer a larger city vibe, a vibrant music and food scene, and lower rent relative to income. Its larger population offers more diverse social and career opportunities.

Bottom Line: Main Differences at a Glance

Austin is far larger, with nearly one million residents, a lower median income, cheaper rent, and a smaller share of homeowners. Frisco is smaller, wealthier, has higher rent but a lower rent‑to‑income ratio, and a higher homeownership rate. Choose Frisco for suburban stability and higher earnings; choose Austin for urban culture, a bigger job market in tech and creative fields, and a lower cost of rent.

Frequently asked questions

Is Frisco or Austin cheaper to live in?

Austin’s median rent ($1,729) and home value ($555,300) are lower than Frisco’s, but rent takes up a larger share of income in Austin (22% vs 16%). Overall, Austin is cheaper on absolute housing costs, while Frisco is more affordable relative to earnings.

Which has higher incomes, Frisco or Austin?

Frisco has the higher median household income at $150,212, which is $56,554—or about 60%—more than Austin’s median of $93,658.

Is rent higher in Frisco or Austin?

Rent is higher in Frisco at $2,014 per month, about $285 (16%) more than Austin’s median rent of $1,729.

Which is better for families, Frisco or Austin?

Frisco’s higher homeownership rate (66% vs 43%), larger median income, and suburban setting make it a strong choice for families seeking stability and space.

Is Frisco or Austin bigger?

Austin is larger, with an estimated population of 979,539 compared with Frisco’s 219,304—about 347% more residents.

The bottom line

Both cities offer distinct advantages: Frisco delivers higher earnings and a family‑focused suburban environment, while Austin provides a larger, culturally rich urban setting with lower absolute housing costs. Match your career goals, lifestyle preferences, and budget to these metrics to decide which city fits you best.
