Katy vs Cedar Park: Which Texas Suburb Fits You?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 04, 2026 · 4 min read

Representative stock image via Pixabay, not a photo of the event.

ShareWhatsAppXFacebook

Katy and Cedar Park are two fast‑growing Texas suburbs that attract families, professionals, and commuters. Katy sits 29 miles west of downtown Houston, while Cedar Park is a 16‑mile northwest suburb of Austin. Both offer strong incomes and similar rent‑to‑income ratios, but they differ in population size, housing costs and overall vibe. Below is a side‑by‑side look to help you choose the community that matches your priorities.

Key takeaways
  • Cedar Park is three times larger than Katy in population.
  • Cedar Park’s median household income is about $14,600 higher.
  • Home values are roughly $112,000 cheaper in Katy.
  • Both cities have rent that equals about 17% of median income.
  • Cedar Park leans toward renters; Katy has higher homeownership.

Population and Land Area

Katy’s 2020‑2024 ACS estimate puts its population at 25,184. Cedar Park’s estimate is 78,301, making it about 211% larger. In terms of land, Katy covers 15.312 square miles, while Cedar Park spans 25.6 square miles. The larger population and footprint give Cedar Park a more suburban feel with a broader range of neighborhoods, whereas Katy feels more compact and community‑focused.

Median Household Income

Katy households earn a median of $114,912 per year, while Cedar Park’s median rises to $129,545—about $14,633 (13 %) higher. Both figures sit well above the national median, indicating that families in these suburbs can comfortably afford homeownership, child‑care, and discretionary spending. The income gap mirrors each city’s economic anchors: Cedar Park benefits from its proximity to Austin’s booming tech, biotech, and creative industries, whereas Katy draws on the diversified Greater Houston economy, including energy, healthcare, and logistics.

The higher earnings in Cedar Park translate into a pricier housing market. The median value of owner‑occupied homes is $513,600 in Cedar Park, roughly $111,900 (28 %) more than Katy’s $401,700 median. Correspondingly, median gross rent is $1,846 in Cedar Park versus $1,602 in Katy—about a 15 % difference. Despite these price variations, rent as a share of income remains consistent: both cities see median rent at roughly 17 % of household income, a level generally considered affordable.

Population size also influences the economic landscape. Cedar Park’s 78,301 residents (over three times Katy’s 25,184) create a larger labor pool and more demand for higher‑paid professional roles, reinforcing its higher income bracket. Meanwhile, Katy’s 81 % owner‑occupancy rate versus Cedar Park’s 67 % reflects a slightly more entrenched homeowner base, which can temper the impact of higher incomes on housing turnover. In short, if a higher paycheck and a larger, tech‑centric job market appeal to you, Cedar Park edges ahead; if you prefer a strong homeowner culture and slightly lower cost of living within the Houston sphere, Katy remains a compelling choice.

Rent Costs and Affordability

The median gross rent in Katy is $1,602 per month, compared with $1,846 in Cedar Park – a 15% difference. Despite the higher rent, both cities’ rent levels equal about 17% of their respective median household incomes, indicating similar affordability for renters. If lower monthly costs are a priority, Katy offers the cheaper option.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Owner‑occupied homes in Katy have a median value of $401,700, while Cedar Park’s median is $513,600 – a gap of $111,900, or about 28% higher. Homeownership is more common in Katy, with 81% of occupied units owned versus 67% in Cedar Park. Buyers seeking lower purchase prices and a higher likelihood of owning will find Katy more attractive.

What Each City Is Known For

Katy grew from a 1890s railroad town on the Missouri–Kansas–Texas line and retains a historic “Old Towne” district. It’s part of the western Greater Houston metro area, benefiting from easy access to I‑10 and Houston’s job market. Cedar Park, settled in the 1870s and linked by the Austin and Northwestern Railroad, is a suburb of Austin with convenient routes like US‑183 and the 183A Toll Road. Its larger size supports a wider variety of shopping, dining and recreation options.

Who Each City Suits Best

Katy appeals to families and homebuyers who value higher ownership rates, lower home prices and a tighter community feel. Its median age of 37.6 suggests a mature, family‑oriented demographic. Cedar Park attracts professionals and renters drawn to higher incomes, a larger urban‑suburban mix, and proximity to Austin’s tech scene. The median age of 37.7 is similar, but the greater population offers more diverse amenities and a livelier social scene.

Frequently asked questions

Is Katy or Cedar Park cheaper to live in?

Katy is cheaper overall. Median rent is $1,602 versus $1,846 in Cedar Park, and the median home value is $401,700 compared with $513,600.

Which has higher incomes, Katy or Cedar Park?

Cedar Park has higher median household income at $129,545, while Katy’s median is $114,912.

Is rent higher in Katy or Cedar Park?

Rent is higher in Cedar Park at $1,846 per month; Katy’s median rent is $1,602.

Which is better for families, Katy or Cedar Park?

Katy may suit families seeking higher homeownership (81%) and lower purchase costs. Cedar Park offers more amenities and a larger community for families who prefer a broader suburban environment.

Is Katy or Cedar Park bigger?

Cedar Park is larger, with a population of 78,301 versus Katy’s 25,184 – about three times as many residents.

The bottom line

Both Katy and Cedar Park provide strong incomes and affordable rent relative to earnings. Katy stands out for lower home prices and a higher ownership rate, while Cedar Park offers higher wages and a larger, more varied community. Weigh your priorities—cost of buying, renting preferences, and desired lifestyle—to pick the suburb that fits your next chapter.
