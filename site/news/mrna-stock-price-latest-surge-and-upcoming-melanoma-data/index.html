What triggered the mid‑October surge in Moderna's stock price?

On 15 Oct 2026, Moderna (NASDAQ:MRNA) shares surged to fresh 52‑week highs, according to StockTwits. The jump was fueled by two analyst upgrades. Goldman Sachs raised its price target ahead of detailed Phase 3 melanoma data, signaling confidence in the upcoming trial readout. At the same time, Bank of America lifted its target, echoing the same optimism. Investors interpreted both moves as a strong endorsement of Moderna's pipeline, especially the INTerpath‑001 melanoma trial. The rally was intense enough that Yahoo Finance reported a blistering rise on 16 Oct 2026, before the stock pulled back later in the week.

When will Moderna's Phase 3 melanoma data be released?

The Phase 3 INTerpath‑001 melanoma trial results are slated for presentation at the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) Congress in Madrid. The specific slot is Saturday, 24 Oct 2026 at 10:30 a.m. ET. This date marks the first public disclosure of detailed efficacy and safety data from the study, which compared intismeran autogene plus Keytruda against Keytruda alone in resected stage IIB‑IV melanoma patients. Investors and analysts will use the presentation to gauge the trial's impact on Moderna's future revenue streams.

Which analysts upgraded their price targets for Moderna in October 2026?

Two major Wall Street houses raised their price targets for Moderna during the week of 15 Oct 2026. Goldman Sachs increased its target ahead of the upcoming melanoma data, reflecting confidence in the INTerpath‑001 trial. Bank of America followed suit, also lifting its target as the company prepared to release Phase 3 results. Both upgrades were reported in separate financial news outlets—StockTwits for Goldman Sachs and Benzinga for Bank of America. The consensus among analysts suggests that the market expects positive trial outcomes to drive the stock higher.

How many patients participated in the INTerpath‑001 melanoma trial?

The INTerpath‑001 Phase 3 trial enrolled a total of 1,137 patients with resected stage IIB‑IV melanoma. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either intismeran autogene plus Keytruda or Keytruda alone. The large enrollment size provides robust statistical power to detect differences in disease‑free survival and overall survival between the two arms. The trial's scale also underscores Moderna's commitment to expanding its oncology portfolio beyond mRNA vaccines.
