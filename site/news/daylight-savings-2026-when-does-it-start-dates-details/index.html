When does daylight saving time end in 2026?

In 2026, daylight saving time ends on Sunday, Nov. 1, at 2 a.m. local time. At that moment, clocks are set back one hour, returning to 1 a.m. This “fall back” marks the conclusion of the daylight‑saving period for the year and restores standard time across the United States.

The change is consistent nationwide, though the exact local time zone determines the moment the clocks move. For example, in Florida the shift occurs at 2 a.m. Eastern Time, while other zones adjust at the corresponding local hour.

What time do clocks change on the 2026 daylight saving end date?

Clocks change at 2 a.m. on the night of Nov. 1, 2026. At that instant, they are turned back one hour to 1 a.m. This timing is built into the federal daylight‑saving schedule and applies to all states that observe the practice.

The 2 a.m. switch is chosen to minimize disruption to most daily activities. Businesses, schools, and public services typically adjust their schedules after the hour has been set back, ensuring a smooth transition back to standard time.

Which day of the week does daylight saving time end in 2026?

Daylight saving time ends on a Sunday in 2026. Specifically, the first Sunday in November—Nov. 1—marks the end of the daylight‑saving period. The federal calendar designates the first Sunday in November as the annual “fall back” day, and 2026 follows that rule.

Because the change occurs on a weekend, many workplaces and schools can accommodate the shift without interrupting weekday operations, making the transition easier for most Americans.

What is the current federal schedule for daylight saving time in 2026?

Under current law, daylight saving time begins on the second Sunday in March and ends on the first Sunday in November. This schedule places the 2026 end date on Nov. 1, as confirmed by multiple sources. The law has been in effect since 2007 and has not been altered by recent legislation.

All states that observe daylight saving follow this timetable, unless a state has opted out entirely, such as Arizona and Hawaii. The consistency of the schedule helps businesses, broadcasters, and travelers coordinate across time zones.

Does the Sunshine Protection Act affect the 2026 daylight saving time change?

The Sunshine Protection Act has not been enacted into law as of October 2026, so it does not change the daylight‑saving dates for this year. The act, if passed, would make daylight saving time permanent, eliminating the twice‑yearly clock change. Since the legislation remains pending, the 2026 schedule stays the same: start in March, end on Nov. 1.

Until Congress approves the act, the existing federal schedule continues to dictate when clocks move forward and back, keeping the 2026 “fall back” on Nov. 1 at 2 a.m. local time.
