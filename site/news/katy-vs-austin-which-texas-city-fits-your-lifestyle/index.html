Katy vs Austin: Which Texas City Fits Your Lifestyle?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 09, 2026 · 4 min read

Representative stock image via Pixabay, not a photo of the event.

Katy and Austin offer very different living experiences. Katy is a small, affluent suburb of Houston with a strong home‑ownership rate, while Austin is a large, fast‑growing capital city known for its culture and tech scene. Below we break down the key metrics and who each city serves best.

Key takeaways
  • Katy is much smaller but wealthier per household.
  • Austin’s housing costs are higher but rent is a larger share of income.
  • Home ownership dominates in Katy; renters are the majority in Austin.
  • Austin suits young professionals; Katy fits families seeking stability.

Population size and geographic footprint

Katy’s population stands at 25,184, covering about 15.312 square miles. Austin is a major metropolis with 979,539 residents spread across 305.1 square miles. In other words, Austin’s population is 954,355 higher – roughly 3,790% more than Katy’s. The size difference means Austin feels like a big‑city environment with many neighborhoods, while Katy feels like a tight‑knit community within the Greater Houston area.

Household income and what it means for buying power

The median household income in Katy is $114,912, which is $21,254 – about 23% – higher than Austin’s median of $93,658. Higher incomes in Katy translate to more discretionary spending power, especially for families looking to invest in larger homes or private schooling. Austin’s lower median still supports a vibrant economy, but residents may feel tighter budgets when it comes to big purchases.

Rent costs and affordability ratios

Median gross rent in Katy is $1,602 per month, while Austin’s median rent is $1,729. Austin’s rent is $127 – about 8% – higher than Katy’s. When measured against income, rent in Katy equals roughly 17% of the median household income, whereas in Austin it consumes about 22%. Lower rent‑to‑income ratio in Katy makes it a more affordable option for renters, especially those earning near the median.

Home values, ownership rates and market dynamics

Owner‑occupied homes in Katy have a median value of $401,700, which is $153,600 – about 38% – lower than Austin’s median of $555,300. Ownership is the norm in Katy, with 81% of occupied housing units owned. In Austin, only 43% are owner‑occupied, indicating a larger rental market and more turnover. Buyers in Katy get more house for their money and enjoy higher ownership stability.

What each city is known for and lifestyle vibe

Katy grew from a railroad town in the 1890s and today serves as the western gateway to the Greater Houston area. Its roots in rice farming and recent suburban expansion give it a family‑oriented, quieter feel. Austin, incorporated in 1839, is the state capital and sits in the Texas Hill Country on the Colorado River. It is famed for live music, tech startups, outdoor recreation on Lady Bird Lake and a youthful, progressive culture.

Who each city suits best

Katy’s demographics paint a picture of a relatively affluent, family‑oriented suburb. With a median household income of $114,912—about 23 % higher than Austin’s—its residents enjoy a comfortable rent‑to‑income ratio of roughly 17 % (median rent $1,602 versus income). Homeownership is the norm: 81 % of occupied units are owned, and the median value of those homes sits at $401,700, well below Austin’s $555,300 but still reflecting solid equity. The city’s median age of 37.6 years suggests a mature population, and its modest size of 25,184 people (just 2.6 % of Austin’s) fosters a tight‑knit community with stable schools, parks, and family‑focused amenities. Retirees and parents seeking safe neighborhoods, good public schools, and a slower pace will find Katy’s suburban layout and lower housing cost burden especially appealing.

Austin, by contrast, is a bustling metropolis of 979,539 residents—nearly 38 times Katy’s population—anchored by a diverse, high‑growth job market in tech, government, and creative industries. The median age of 34.7 years underscores a youthful, dynamic workforce. Although median household income trails Katy at $93,658, the city’s cultural cachet, live‑music scene, and entrepreneurial ecosystem attract young professionals and startups. Housing is less owner‑centric (only 43 % owner‑occupied) and pricier: median rent $1,729 consumes about 22 % of income, and median home values sit at $555,300, a 38 % premium over Katy. Those who thrive on urban energy, networking opportunities, and a vibrant nightlife will feel at home in Austin’s expansive 305‑square‑mile footprint, which also offers abundant outdoor recreation along Lady Bird Lake, Barton Springs, and the Hill Country.

Frequently asked questions

Is Katy or Austin cheaper to live in?

Katy is generally cheaper. Median rent is $1,602 versus $1,729 in Austin, and home values are $401,700 compared with $555,300. Rent also takes up a smaller share of income in Katy (17% vs 22%).

Which has higher incomes, Katy or Austin?

Katy has higher median household income at $114,912, which is $21,254 – about 23% – more than Austin’s $93,658.

Is rent higher in Katy or Austin?

Rent is higher in Austin. The median gross rent is $1,729 in Austin versus $1,602 in Katy.

Which is better for families, Katy or Austin?

Katy tends to be better for families because of its higher home‑ownership rate (81%), lower housing costs, and suburban environment. Austin’s larger rental market and higher cost of living may be less family‑focused.

Is Katy or Austin bigger?

Austin is far bigger. Its population of 979,539 dwarfs Katy’s 25,184, making Austin about 3,790% larger in terms of residents.

The bottom line

Katy offers a smaller, affluent suburb with lower housing costs and strong ownership, ideal for families and those who prefer a quieter setting. Austin provides a large, culturally rich city with higher rents and home prices but a dynamic job market for younger professionals. Weigh these factors against your lifestyle priorities to choose the right Texas home.
