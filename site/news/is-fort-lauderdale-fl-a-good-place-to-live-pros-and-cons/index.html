Is Fort Lauderdale, FL a Good Place to Live? Pros and Cons

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Fort Lauderdale, FL offers a mix of coastal charm, a sizable population, and a relatively high median income. With 165 miles of inland waterways, sunny winters, and a major airport nearby, the city has many attractions. However, housing costs and rent are above national averages. This guide breaks down the pros and cons so you can determine if Fort Lauderdale fits your lifestyle.

Population size and regional standing

Fort Lauderdale had an estimated population of 185,604 in the 2020-2024 ACS data, making it the tenth‑most populous city in Florida. Within the Miami metro area, it ranks third after Miami and Hialeah. The city also serves as the seat of government for Broward County, reinforcing its regional importance. A sizable population can mean more amenities, cultural events, and job opportunities, but also higher demand for housing and services.

Cost of living and housing market

The median household income in Fort Lauderdale is $83,130 per year, which is higher than the national median. Yet, the median gross rent stands at $1,854 per month, and the median value of owner‑occupied homes is $486,700. About 54% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating a balanced mix of renters and owners. Prospective residents should weigh the higher income against these housing costs to gauge affordability.

Waterways and the "Venice of America" nickname

Fort Lauderdale’s moniker “Venice of America” isn’t just a catchy nickname—it reflects a genuine, city‑wide network of water that shapes daily life for its 185,604 residents (2020‑2024 ACS). The 165 miles of inland canals snake through more than 60 officially recognized neighborhoods, linking upscale waterfront enclaves such as Las Olas and Harbor Beach with historic districts like Riverland. This extensive waterway system creates a unique blend of residential, recreational, and commercial opportunities that few U.S. cities can match.

For homeowners, proximity to a canal often translates into premium real‑estate values; the median price of an owner‑occupied home sits at $486,700, and over half of the housing stock (≈54 %) is owner‑occupied, indicating strong investment in waterfront property. Renters also benefit, with a median gross rent of $1,854 per month, and many choose apartments that offer private dock access or easy steps to boat ramps. Boating isn’t merely a pastime—it's a primary mode of transportation for commuters who can glide from their home to downtown offices in minutes, bypassing traffic on I‑95.

The canals support a vibrant leisure scene: kayakers paddle through mangrove‑lined passages, charter boats ferry diners to ocean‑front restaurants, and nightly “sunset cruises” draw both locals and tourists. Because the city’s total area is 38.6 sq mi, with 3.8 sq mi of water, water‑based activities are woven into the fabric of everyday life. Combined with a median household income of $83,130 and a median age of 42.8 years, Fort Lauderdale’s waterways not only define its identity but also underpin a thriving, affluent community that values both convenience and the serene allure of living on the water.

Climate throughout the year

The city experiences a tropical rainforest climate (Köppen Af). Winters are dry and sunny, while the wet season runs from May to October, bringing frequent rain and higher humidity. USDA hardiness zones range from 10b to 11a, supporting lush vegetation year‑round. Residents enjoy warm temperatures most of the year, but should be prepared for the summer’s heavy rain and occasional storms.

Transportation and airport access

Fort Lauderdale/Hollywood International Airport (FLL) sits just two miles south of downtown, making it one of the most conveniently located airports in South Florida. In 2023 the airport handled over 35 million passengers, offering nonstop service to major hubs such as New York, Chicago, Dallas, Atlanta, and international destinations in the Caribbean, Central America, and Europe. For residents—whose median household income is $83,130—this easy access translates into shorter travel times for business trips, weekend getaways, and family visits, often eliminating the need to drive the 30‑mile stretch to Miami International Airport.

Ground transportation around the airport is robust. The Brightline (now Virgin Trains USA) high‑speed rail line stops at a dedicated FLL station, linking Fort Lauderdale directly to Miami, West Palm Beach, and eventually Orlando, with travel times under an hour to Miami. Local buses operated by Broward County Transit (BCT) run frequent routes to downtown, the beach corridor, and neighboring cities, while ride‑share services and taxis are readily available on the airport’s extensive curbside network.

Beyond air travel, Fort Lauderdale’s road infrastructure connects residents to the broader region. Interstate 95 runs just east of the city, providing a north‑south artery to Jacksonville and Miami, while the Florida Turnpike offers an alternate east‑west route to the interior of the state. The city’s proximity to major highways, combined with a growing public‑transit system, makes commuting and regional travel relatively seamless for its 185,000‑plus inhabitants.

Frequently asked questions

What is the population of Fort Lauderdale and how does it rank within Florida?

Fort Lauderdale’s population is 185,604, placing it as the tenth‑most populous city in Florida and the third‑largest in the Miami metro area after Miami and Hialeah.

What is the climate like in Fort Lauderdale throughout the year?

Fort Lauderdale has a tropical rainforest climate with dry, sunny winters and a wet season from May to October. Temperatures stay warm year‑round, and the area falls in USDA zones 10b to 11a.

How extensive is Fort Lauderdale's canal system and why is it called the "Venice of America"?

The city features 165 miles of inland waterways, winding through over 60 recognized neighborhoods. This extensive canal network earned Fort Lauderdale the nickname "Venice of America".

What transportation options are available for flying into Fort Lauderdale?

Fort Lauderdale/Hollywood International Airport (FLL) is located two miles south of downtown and serves as the main commercial airport for the area, offering numerous domestic and international flights.

The bottom line

Fort Lauderdale combines coastal living, a robust canal system, and a warm climate with higher-than‑average housing costs. Weighing income potential against rent and home prices will help you decide if the city’s lifestyle aligns with your priorities.
