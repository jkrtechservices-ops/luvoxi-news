Is Arlington, TX a Good Place to Live? Pros and Cons

Arlington, TX offers a young population, solid median income and a mix of sports, entertainment and affordable housing. With a median household income of $75,171 and a median home value of $304,700, the city balances cost of living with access to major employers and attractions. Whether you value proximity to Dallas and Fort Worth, or a lively downtown scene, Arlington’s strengths and trade‑offs are worth weighing.

Population, Age and Housing Overview

Arlington is the third‑largest city in the Dallas‑Fort Worth metro area with a 2020 population of 394,266 and an estimated 397,742 in the 2020‑2024 ACS. The median age is 33.7 years, indicating a relatively young community. About 54% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, while the median gross rent sits at $1,470 per month. The median value of owner‑occupied homes is $304,700, offering a middle‑of‑the‑road price point compared with neighboring metros.

Employment Landscape and Economic Drivers

Arlington’s economy is anchored by several large employers. The Arlington Assembly plant, operated by General Motors, provides manufacturing jobs. Higher education contributes through the University of Texas at Arlington, a major urban research university. The city also benefits from sports and entertainment venues that generate service‑sector employment. These anchors help sustain a median household income of $75,171, positioning Arlington above many surrounding communities.

Recreation, Sports and Cultural Attractions

Arlington is a destination for sports fans and families alike. Globe Life Field hosts the Texas Rangers, while AT&T Stadium is home to the Dallas Cowboys. The Dallas Wings play at College Park Center, and Six Flags Over Texas and Hurricane Harbor provide theme‑park thrills. The International Bowling Campus adds a niche cultural draw. These venues create year‑round entertainment options and support a lively downtown atmosphere.

Location, Transportation and Regional Access

Situated in Tarrant County, Arlington anchors the Mid‑Cities corridor of the Dallas‑Fort Worth‑Arlington metropolitan statistical area. With a 2020‑2024 ACS estimate of 397,742 residents, it is the second‑largest city in Tarrant County and the third‑largest in the entire DFW metroplex, ranking 51st in the nation and seventh in Texas. Its central location—roughly equidistant from downtown Dallas and downtown Fort Worth—gives residents easy access to the cultural, employment, and entertainment options of both major cities while enjoying a more affordable cost of living.

Transportation is a key advantage. Interstate 20 and Interstate 30 intersect the city, providing direct east‑west links to Dallas, Fort Worth, and the surrounding suburbs. These highways also connect drivers to Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport and Dallas Love Field within 30‑45 minutes, making business travel and weekend getaways straightforward. Public‑transit options include the Trinity Railway Express commuter rail, which stops at Arlington’s downtown station and links the city to both Dallas and Fort Worth’s rail networks.

Housing reflects the city’s balanced appeal: about 54 % of occupied units are owner‑occupied, with a median home value of $304,700 and a median gross rent of $1,470. The median household income of $75,171 supports a relatively young population—the median age is 33.7—who benefit from the city’s proximity to major employers such as the GM Arlington Assembly plant, the University of Texas at Arlington, and a thriving entertainment district that includes Globe Life Field, AT&T Stadium, Six Flags Over Texas, and the International Bowling Campus.

Climate and Lifestyle Considerations

Arlington’s climate is quintessentially North Texan: long, scorching summers with temperatures frequently climbing into the high 90s °F (35‑37 °C) and occasional heat‑waves that push the mercury above 100 °F (38 °C). Winters are mild, with daytime highs usually in the 50s‑60s °F (10‑20 °C) but cold snaps can dip into the 30s °F (‑1 to 4 °C). This weather pattern means residents enjoy a virtually year‑round outdoor lifestyle. The city’s extensive park system, the nearby Trinity River trails, and its famed attractions—Six Flags Over Texas and Hurricane Harbor—remain accessible in every season, while the mild winter evenings are perfect for strolling around the vibrant College Park Center or catching a game at Globe Life Field.

The demographic makeup supports an active, youthful community: the median age is just 33.7 years, and more than half of the 397,742‑strong population (54 % of occupied housing units) own their homes. With a median household income of $75,171, many families can comfortably afford the median home value of $304,700 or the median gross rent of $1,470 per month, allowing them to invest in leisure activities and outdoor gear. Arlington’s position in the Mid‑Cities region of the Dallas‑Fort Worth‑Arlington metro area also provides easy access to a broader network of cultural, sporting, and recreational options, reinforcing its reputation as a city where the climate and lifestyle go hand‑in‑hand.

Frequently asked questions

What is the population of Arlington, TX?

Arlington had a population of 394,266 in 2020 and an estimated 397,742 in the 2020‑2024 American Community Survey.

What major employers and attractions are located in Arlington?

Key employers include the General Motors Arlington Assembly plant and the University of Texas at Arlington. Attractions feature Globe Life Field, AT&T Stadium, Six Flags Over Texas, Hurricane Harbor, College Park Center and the International Bowling Campus.

How far is Arlington from Dallas and Fort Worth?

Arlington sits roughly halfway between Dallas and Fort Worth, providing easy highway access to both cities via I‑20 and I‑30.

When was Arlington founded and incorporated?

Arlington was founded in 1876 along the Texas and Pacific Railway and incorporated on April 21, 1884.

The bottom line

Arlington blends affordable housing, a youthful demographic and strong employment anchors with a rich sports and entertainment scene. Its central location between Dallas and Fort Worth adds convenience, while the climate supports an active lifestyle. Weighing these factors will help you decide if Arlington, TX fits your priorities.
