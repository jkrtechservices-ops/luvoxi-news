Median Income in Fort Lauderdale, FL: How Much Do Residents Earn?

Fort Lauderdale residents earn a median household income of $83,130 per year. Below, we break down what this income means for renters, homeowners, and anyone considering a move to the area.

What Does a $83,130 Median Income Mean for Daily Life?

A median household income of $83,130 places Fort Lauderdale above the national median, indicating a relatively strong earning base. Families earning around this amount can typically cover essential expenses, such as utilities, groceries, and transportation, while still having room for discretionary spending. However, the city’s cost of living, especially housing, can absorb a significant portion of that income.

When budgeting, many residents allocate roughly 30% of their earnings to housing. With a median gross rent of $1,854 per month, renters using the median income would spend close to that benchmark, leaving the remaining earnings for other needs and savings.

How Affordable Is Renting in Fort Lauderdale?

The median gross rent in Fort Lauderdale is $1,854 per month. This figure reflects the middle point of all rental prices reported in the 2020-2024 survey. For a household earning the median income, rent consumes a sizable slice of the budget, but it remains within a range many consider manageable.

Renters should also factor in additional costs such as electricity, water, and internet, which can add several hundred dollars to monthly expenses. Comparing rent to the median income helps prospective tenants decide whether they need to look for more affordable neighborhoods or consider shared housing options.

What Is the Home‑Buying Landscape for Median Earners?

Owner‑occupied homes in Fort Lauderdale have a median value of $486,700. This price point is higher than the national median home value, reflecting the city’s desirability and coastal location. Prospective buyers earning the median household income will need to assess mortgage options, down payment requirements, and property taxes.

With 54% of occupied housing units being owner‑occupied, a little more than half of residents own their homes. This suggests a balanced market where both renting and owning are common choices. Buyers should work with lenders to understand how their income aligns with loan qualifications and explore programs that may assist first‑time homebuyers.

How Does Age Influence Income and Housing Choices?

The median age of Fort Lauderdale residents is 42.8 years. This age group often represents individuals in the peak of their careers, which aligns with the city’s median household income of $83,130. People in this age bracket may have accumulated savings for down payments, yet they also might be balancing family expenses such as childcare or education.

Understanding the median age helps explain why a significant portion of the population chooses to own homes, while others continue to rent. Those approaching retirement may prioritize stable housing costs, whereas younger professionals might favor the flexibility of renting while they advance their careers.

What Does the Population Size Tell Us About the Market?

Fort Lauderdale’s estimated population of 185,604 (U.S. Census Bureau 2020‑2024 ACS 5‑year) may seem modest compared with larger metros, but it is large enough to sustain a surprisingly varied economic base. The city’s workforce is split among tourism‑driven hospitality, marine‑industry services, health care, finance, and a growing tech‑and‑creative sector. This diversification means that a sizable portion of residents—about 54% of occupied housing units—are owner‑occupiers, while the remaining renters are drawn to the city’s vibrant downtown and beachfront districts. The median household income of $83,130 supports a relatively strong purchasing power, yet the median gross rent of $1,854 per month and a median owner‑occupied home value of $486,700 illustrate how demand pressures translate into housing costs.

Because the population is steadily climbing, competition for the most desirable neighborhoods intensifies, nudging both rent and home prices upward. Prospective newcomers should watch the city’s age profile, with a median age of 42.8 years, which signals a mature, established community that often favors stable, long‑term housing arrangements. As the population continues to grow, developers respond with new multifamily projects and condo conversions, but land constraints near the coast keep supply tight. Consequently, the interplay of a growing population, solid median incomes, and limited housing inventory suggests that future housing costs are likely to keep rising, while job opportunities expand across the city’s diverse economic sectors.

Frequently asked questions

Is $83,130 a high median income compared to other Florida cities?

Fort Lauderdale’s median household income of $83,130 is higher than many Florida cities, reflecting its strong job market and coastal appeal. It sits above the state median, indicating relatively higher earning power for residents.

Can I afford a home with a median value of $486,700 on the median income?

Affording a $486,700 home on a $83,130 income depends on loan terms, down payment, and other debts. Prospective buyers should calculate monthly mortgage payments and compare them to the 30% income guideline.

What percentage of Fort Lauderdale residents own their homes?

About 54% of occupied housing units in Fort Lauderdale are owner‑occupied, showing that just over half of residents own the places they live.

How does the median rent affect budgeting for a typical household?

With a median rent of $1,854 per month, renters using the median income will allocate a large portion of their earnings to housing. Budgeting should account for utilities, transportation, and savings after rent.

The bottom line

Fort Lauderdale’s median household income of $83,130 provides a useful benchmark for understanding the city’s cost of living. By comparing this figure to rent, home values, and demographic data, residents and newcomers can make informed decisions about budgeting, renting, or buying. Use these insights to plan financially and enjoy what the city has to offer.
