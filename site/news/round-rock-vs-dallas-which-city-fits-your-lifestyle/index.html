Round Rock vs Dallas: Which City Fits Your Lifestyle?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 21, 2026 · 4 min read

Round Rock and Dallas offer very different living experiences. Round Rock is a midsize suburb with higher incomes and a strong tech presence, while Dallas is a major metropolis with a larger population and lower housing costs. Understanding the numbers—population, earnings, rent, home values—and each city’s character helps you choose the right fit.

Key takeaways
  • Round Rock earns about 41% more than Dallas.
  • Dallas rent is roughly 20% lower than Round Rock.
  • Home values are 31% higher in Round Rock.
  • Dallas is nine times larger in population.

Population and Physical Size

Dallas is a major urban center with an estimated population of 1,307,930, making it the ninth‑most populous city in the United States. Round Rock’s population stands at 127,786, about 924% lower than Dallas. In terms of land, Dallas covers 385.8 square miles, while Round Rock occupies a much smaller footprint within the Greater Austin metropolitan area. The sheer scale of Dallas means more neighborhoods, cultural venues, and commuting options, whereas Round Rock feels more like a close‑knit community.

Income Levels and Purchasing Power

Median household income in Round Rock is $99,287, which is $28,769—or about 41%—higher than Dallas’s median of $70,518. Higher earnings in Round Rock translate to greater individual purchasing power, especially for families and professionals. Dallas, while offering a broader job market across multiple industries, reflects a lower median income, which can affect disposable income and savings rates.

Rent Costs and Affordability

The median gross rent in Round Rock is $1,763 per month, about $291 (20%) higher than Dallas’s median rent of $1,472. When measured against income, rent in Round Rock consumes roughly 21% of median household earnings, while Dallas renters spend about 25% of their income on rent. Although Dallas rent is lower, the higher rent‑to‑income ratio means renters may feel a tighter budget compared with Round Rock.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Owner‑occupied homes in Round Rock have a median value of $418,600, which is $97,900 (31%) higher than Dallas’s median home value of $320,700. About 56% of occupied housing units in Round Rock are owner‑occupied, compared with 42% in Dallas. The higher home prices in Round Rock reflect its desirability and higher incomes, while Dallas offers more affordable entry‑level options for buyers.

What Each City Is Known For

Round Rock is part of the Greater Austin area and hosts the international headquarters of Dell, employing roughly 16,000 people. It also features retail hubs like IKEA, Premium Outlets, and the mixed‑use La Frontera center, plus a historic downtown called Old Town. Dallas anchors the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, a massive 8.5‑million‑person region, and sits at the crossroads of four major interstates, making it a transportation and business hub with a rich cultural history.

Who Should Choose Round Rock

Round Rock is an ideal fit for professionals who want a solid paycheck without the high‑cost hustle of a major metropolis. With a median household income of $99,287—about 41 % higher than Dallas—its tech‑driven economy (anchored by Dell’s 16,000‑employee headquarters) offers salaries that comfortably outpace local expenses. Median rent sits at $1,763, which is only 21 % of that income, and the rent‑to‑income ratio remains lower than Dallas’s 25 %, giving renters more discretionary cash each month. Home‑buyers also benefit from a relatively strong equity base: 56 % of occupied units are owner‑occupied and the median home value is $418,600, roughly $98 k more than in Dallas, reflecting both demand and the city’s suburban appeal.

The demographic profile reinforces the family‑friendly vibe. At a median age of 36.2 years, Round Rock skews slightly older than Dallas (33.4), suggesting a settled community where many residents are raising children. The city’s 127,786 residents enjoy a quieter, “small‑town‑within‑a‑big‑city” atmosphere just 15 miles north of Austin, with easy access to the state capital’s cultural amenities via I‑35. Quality schools, plentiful parks, and mixed‑use developments such as La Frontera and the historic Old Town district create a safe, community‑oriented environment. For anyone who values financial stability, a higher home‑ownership rate, and a suburban lifestyle anchored by strong tech employment, Round Rock offers a compelling alternative to the larger, faster‑paced Dallas.

Who Should Choose Dallas

Dallas appeals to those who thrive in a large, diverse city with extensive job opportunities across many sectors. The lower rent and home prices make it a good option for first‑time buyers or renters on tighter budgets. Younger adults—median age 33.4 years—may enjoy the vibrant nightlife, cultural institutions, and the connectivity offered by multiple interstates.

Frequently asked questions

Is Round Rock or Dallas cheaper to live in?

Dallas has lower median rent ($1,472 vs $1,763) and cheaper median home values ($320,700 vs $418,600), making overall housing costs cheaper than Round Rock.

Which has higher incomes, Round Rock or Dallas?

Round Rock’s median household income is $99,287, which is $28,769 higher than Dallas’s median of $70,518.

Is rent higher in Round Rock or Dallas?

Rent is higher in Round Rock at $1,763 per month, compared with $1,472 in Dallas.

Which is better for families, Round Rock or Dallas?

Round Rock’s higher home‑ownership rate (56%), higher median income, and suburban setting often make it more family‑friendly, while Dallas offers more affordable entry‑level housing.

Is Round Rock or Dallas bigger?

Dallas is much larger, with a population of 1,307,930 versus Round Rock’s 127,786.

The bottom line

Round Rock delivers higher earnings and a suburban vibe with pricier homes, while Dallas offers a massive urban environment with lower housing costs. Choose Round Rock for higher income stability and family‑oriented living; pick Dallas for affordability, diversity, and metropolitan amenities.
