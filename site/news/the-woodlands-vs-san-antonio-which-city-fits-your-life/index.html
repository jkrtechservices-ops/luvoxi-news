The Woodlands vs San Antonio: Which City Fits Your Life?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 14, 2026 · 4 min read

If you’re weighing a move in Texas, the numbers tell a clear story: The Woodlands is a small, affluent suburb of Houston, while San Antonio is a large, culturally rich metropolis. Both offer distinct lifestyles, housing markets and cost‑of‑living profiles, so understanding the data helps you decide which community aligns with your priorities.

Key takeaways
  • The Woodlands is 43.9 sq mi with 121,000 residents; San Antonio spans 465.4 sq mi and houses 1,479,835 people.
  • Median household income in The Woodlands is $140,701, more than double San Antonio’s $65,056.
  • Home values are $511,700 in The Woodlands versus $235,700 in San Antonio.
  • Rent consumes about 16% of income in The Woodlands and 24% in San Antonio.
  • The Woodlands suits high‑earning families; San Antonio appeals to those seeking affordability and urban amenities.

Population size and geographic footprint

The Woodlands covers 43.9 square miles, of which 43.3 square miles are land. Its 2020‑2024 ACS estimate puts the population at 121,002. By contrast, San Antonio spreads over 465.4 square miles, with 460.93 square miles of land, and a 2020‑2024 ACS estimate of 1,479,835 residents. In other words, San Antonio is about 1123% larger in population and occupies roughly ten times the land area of The Woodlands. The difference matters for daily life: The Woodlands feels like a planned suburb with spacious neighborhoods, while San Antonio feels like a major city with dense neighborhoods, a larger downtown core and a broader range of services.

Income levels and what they mean for purchasing power

Median household income in The Woodlands is $140,701 per year, which is $75,645 higher—about 116%—than San Antonio’s median of $65,056. This gap reflects The Woodlands’ role as a corporate hub, home to headquarters for companies such as Occidental Petroleum and Chevron Phillips Chemical. Higher incomes translate into greater discretionary spending power, enabling residents to afford premium goods, services and extracurricular activities. In San Antonio, the lower median income aligns with a more modest cost of living and a broader mix of income brackets, making it attractive to young professionals, retirees and families on tighter budgets.

Housing costs: rent and home ownership

Rent in The Woodlands has a median gross amount of $1,822 per month, which is $498—about 38%—higher than San Antonio’s median rent of $1,324. However, rent in The Woodlands represents roughly 16% of the median household income, while in San Antonio it consumes about 24% of income. Home ownership also diverges sharply: the median value of owner‑occupied homes in The Woodlands is $511,700, $276,000—about 117%—more than San Antonio’s median of $235,700. Owner‑occupied units make up 73% of housing in The Woodlands versus 52% in San Antonio, indicating a stronger home‑ownership culture in the suburb.

Affordability snapshot

Affordability hinges on the relationship between income and housing costs. In The Woodlands, higher salaries offset the steeper rent and home prices, keeping housing cost ratios relatively low. San Antonio’s lower rents and home values are offset by lower incomes, resulting in a higher percentage of income devoted to housing. For families prioritizing lower absolute housing bills, San Antonio is cheaper. For those who value higher earnings and can manage larger mortgage payments, The Woodlands offers a more upscale living environment without stretching the budget beyond a reasonable proportion of income.

What each city is known for

The Woodlands is best known as a master‑planned community with extensive green spaces, a network of lakes, and a thriving corporate sector. It provides a suburban feel while still being part of the Greater Houston metropolitan area, just 28 miles north of downtown Houston. San Antonio, on the other hand, is celebrated for its historic missions, the River Walk, a strong military presence, and a vibrant blend of Mexican and Texan culture. Its size supports a diverse culinary scene, major sports venues and a larger array of cultural institutions.

Who each city suits best

The Woodlands appeals to high‑earning professionals, dual‑income families and retirees who value a safe, meticulously designed suburb with easy access to corporate campuses and high‑quality amenities. Its higher home‑ownership rate and median age of 40.6 suggest a stable, established community. San Antonio fits younger adults, growing families and those seeking cultural richness at a lower price point. The median age of 34.9 and a larger share of renters indicate a more dynamic, transitional population. If you prioritize affordability, cultural experiences and a larger urban environment, San Antonio is the better fit; if you prioritize income, upscale housing and a planned suburban layout, The Woodlands is the clear choice.

Frequently asked questions

Is The Woodlands or San Antonio cheaper to live in?

San Antonio is cheaper overall. Its median rent ($1,324) and home value ($235,700) are lower than The Woodlands, though the lower median income means a larger share of earnings goes to housing.

Which has higher incomes, The Woodlands or San Antonio?

The Woodlands has higher incomes. The median household income is $140,701, which is $75,645—or about 116%—more than San Antonio’s $65,056.

Is rent higher in The Woodlands or San Antonio?

Rent is higher in The Woodlands at $1,822 per month, compared with $1,324 in San Antonio.

Which is better for families, The Woodlands or San Antonio?

The Woodlands tends to suit families seeking higher incomes, larger homes and a suburban setting, while San Antonio offers more affordable housing and a wider range of cultural and recreational options for families on a tighter budget.

Is The Woodlands or San Antonio bigger?

San Antonio is much bigger. Its population of 1,479,835 dwarfs The Woodlands’ 121,002, and it covers 465.4 square miles versus The Woodlands’ 43.9 square miles.

The bottom line

Choosing between The Woodlands and San Antonio hinges on what you value most: higher earnings and upscale suburban living versus a larger, more affordable city with rich cultural assets. Use the data on income, housing costs and population size to match your financial situation and lifestyle goals.
