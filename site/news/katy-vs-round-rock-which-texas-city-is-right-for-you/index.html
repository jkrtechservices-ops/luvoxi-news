Katy vs Round Rock: Which Texas City Is Right for You?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 03, 2026 · 5 min read

Katy and Round Rock are both fast‑growing Texas suburbs, but they differ in population size, earnings, housing costs and overall vibe. Katy earns more on average, while Round Rock offers a larger market and a tech‑driven economy. Understanding these numbers helps you pick the community that fits your budget and lifestyle.

Key takeaways
  • Katy’s median income is $15,625 higher than Round Rock’s.
  • Round Rock’s population is about five times larger than Katy’s.
  • Rent is slightly cheaper in Katy, about 17% of household income.
  • Home values are comparable; Round Rock homes are $16,900 pricier.

Population and Land Area

Katy is a small city of 25,184 residents spread over 15.312 square miles. By contrast, Round Rock houses 127,786 people, making it roughly 407% larger than Katy. The size difference means Round Rock feels more like a midsize urban center, while Katy retains a tighter, small‑town feel within the western Greater Houston area.

Median Household Income

Katy’s median household income of $114,912 is roughly 16 % higher than Round Rock’s $99,287, according to the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 ACS 5‑year estimates. This income advantage translates into a stronger discretionary‑spending capacity for Katy families, allowing more flexibility for dining out, travel, and entertainment. While both cities sit well above the national median household income, the gap can be felt in everyday budgeting decisions.

In Katy, the median gross rent is $1,602 per month, which represents about 17 % of the median household income—a relatively affordable rent‑to‑income ratio. By contrast, Round Rock’s median rent is $1,763, or roughly 21 % of its median income, indicating a slightly tighter housing budget for renters despite the city’s higher home‑value median of $418,600 (versus $401,700 in Katy).

Homeownership also differs markedly. About 81 % of occupied housing units in Katy are owner‑occupied, compared with 56 % in Round Rock. The higher ownership rate, coupled with a modestly lower median home value, suggests that Katy residents can more readily leverage their higher incomes into equity building.

Both cities have comparable median ages—37.6 in Katy and 36.2 in Round Rock—so the income disparity is not driven by a significantly older or younger population. Instead, it reflects the economic composition of each community, with Katy’s higher earnings offering residents a marginally greater cushion for savings, investments, and lifestyle choices.

Rent Costs and Affordability

The median gross rent in Katy is $1,602 per month, roughly 17% of its median household income. In Round Rock, rent is $1,763 per month, representing about 21% of the median income. Although Round Rock’s rent is $161 higher, the larger share of income it consumes makes Katy the more affordable rental market.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Owner‑occupied homes in Katy have a median value of $401,700, while Round Rock’s median is $418,600 – a $16,900 difference, or about 4% higher. Homeownership is far more common in Katy, with 81% of occupied units owned versus 56% in Round Rock. This suggests Katy leans toward a stable, owner‑driven community, whereas Round Rock has a larger renter base.

What Each City Is Known For

Katy sits at the crossroads of Harris, Fort Bend and Waller counties and grew out of a historic railroad town. It is part of the western Greater Houston metro area and benefits from proximity to Houston’s job market. Round Rock, located 15 miles north of Austin, is home to Dell’s international headquarters and major retail hubs like IKEA and Premium Outlets, giving it a tech‑focused and commercial identity.

Who Will Feel Most at Home

Katy feels most like home to families and professionals who prize a higher‑earning, homeowner‑centric lifestyle without the bustle of a large metropolis. With a modest population of 25,184, the city’s median household income tops $114,900—about 16 % higher than Round Rock’s—while 81 % of occupied units are owner‑occupied. The median home value of $401,700 and rent of $1,602 (roughly 17 % of household income) make homeownership attainable and affordable for many middle‑class earners. The median age of 37.6 suggests a mature, settled community, and the city’s location 29 miles west of downtown Houston offers easy access to the energy, medical and corporate jobs that drive the region’s high wages. Because most residents own their homes, neighborhoods tend to be stable, with strong school districts and family‑oriented amenities such as parks, community events, and the historic “Old Towne Katy” district.

Round Rock, by contrast, attracts tech workers, commuters to Austin and renters who prefer a larger‑city feel. Its population of 127,786—more than five times Katy’s—creates a vibrant, diverse atmosphere with plentiful shopping, dining and entertainment options, including IKEA, Premium Outlets and the mixed‑use La Frontera center. Although the median household income is lower at $99,287, the city’s median rent of $1,763 (about 21 % of income) reflects a higher renter share (44 %). Home values sit at $418,600, slightly above Katy’s, and the median age of 36.2 points to a younger, more mobile demographic. Proximity to Austin (15 miles north) and the presence of Dell’s headquarters provide abundant tech‑sector jobs, making Round Rock ideal for professionals who thrive in a bustling, growth‑oriented environment.

Frequently asked questions

Is Katy or Round Rock cheaper to live in?

Katy is cheaper for renters because rent is $1,602 versus $1,763 in Round Rock and represents a lower share of household income (17% vs 21%). Home values are slightly lower in Katy as well.

Which has higher incomes, Katy or Round Rock?

Katy has the higher median household income at $114,912, which is $15,625 more than Round Rock’s $99,287.

Is rent higher in Katy or Round Rock?

Rent is higher in Round Rock at $1,763 per month, compared with $1,602 in Katy.

Which is better for families, Katy or Round Rock?

Katy may suit families better due to its higher homeownership rate (81% vs 56%) and higher median income, offering more financial stability and a smaller community feel.

Is Katy or Round Rock bigger?

Round Rock is bigger, with a population of 127,786 compared with Katy’s 25,184.

The bottom line

Katy offers higher earnings, lower rent and a strong owner‑occupied market in a compact setting, while Round Rock provides a larger population, a tech‑driven economy and slightly higher home values. Match these traits to your budget, career and lifestyle to choose the right Texas suburb.
