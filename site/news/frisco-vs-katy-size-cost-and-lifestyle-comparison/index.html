Frisco vs Katy: Size, Cost, and Lifestyle Comparison

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 25, 2026 · 5 min read

Representative stock image via Pixabay, not a photo of the event.

When choosing a Texas city to call home, Frisco and Katy offer distinct trade‑offs. Frisco is a large, fast‑growing suburb of Dallas with higher incomes and pricier housing, while Katy is a smaller, more affordable community on Houston’s western edge. Both have strong owner‑occupancy rates and similar rent‑to‑income ratios, but they differ in scale, cost of living, and the lifestyles they support.

Key takeaways
  • Frisco is about eight times larger than Katy.
  • Median household income is $35,300 higher in Frisco.
  • Home values in Frisco exceed Katy by $240,400.
  • Both cities have rent around 16‑17% of median income.

Population and Physical Size

Frisco’s 2020‑2024 ACS estimates put its population at 219,304, spread across 62.4 square miles. By contrast, Katy’s population is 25,184 within a much smaller footprint. In raw numbers, Frisco is 194,120 people larger – roughly a 771% difference. The larger scale gives Frisco a more urban feel as part of the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex, while Katy retains a small‑town atmosphere within the Greater Houston area.

Income Levels and Purchasing Power

Households in Frisco pull in a median income of $150,212, which is roughly $35,300—or 31 %—more than the $114,912 median earned by Katy residents. This income advantage gives Frisco families a larger discretionary budget, but it is offset by noticeably higher housing costs. The median gross rent in Frisco is $2,014 a month, about $412 (26 %) above Katy’s $1,602, yet rent still consumes only 16 % of the average Frisco income compared with 17 % in Katy. Home‑ownership follows a similar pattern: the median value of an owner‑occupied house in Frisco stands at $642,100, a $240,400 (60 %) premium over Katy’s $401,700. Despite the price gap, Frisco’s home‑ownership rate is slightly lower—66 % of occupied units versus 81 % in Katy—reflecting a larger rental market and the city’s rapid population growth (219,304 residents versus Katy’s 25,184). Both cities sit well above the national median household income, making them attractive to professionals, yet the cost‑of‑living differential means that Frisco earners can afford a broader range of amenities, while Katy’s more affordable housing may appeal to those prioritizing lower mortgage or rent payments. In short, higher earnings in Frisco translate into greater purchasing power, but the city’s elevated price tags keep the proportion of income devoted to shelter roughly comparable to Katy’s.

Rent Costs and Affordability Ratios

The median gross rent in Frisco is $2,014 per month, $412 (about 26%) higher than Katy’s $1,602. When expressed as a share of median income, rent in Frisco equals roughly 16% of household earnings, while Katy’s rent is about 17% of its median income. Both ratios sit in a comfortable range, indicating that despite higher absolute rents, Frisco’s higher wages keep the burden similar to Katy’s.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Frisco’s median owner‑occupied home value sits at $642,100, a striking $240,400 premium—about 60 % higher—than the $401,700 median in Katy. This price gap reflects Frisco’s larger, more diversified market and its proximity to the Dallas‑Fort Worth employment hub, where the median household income reaches $150,212, roughly $35,300 (31 %) above Katy’s $114,912. Higher incomes in Frisco can absorb the costlier housing, yet the city’s median gross rent of $2,014 per month still represents about 16 % of that income, only marginally lower than Katy’s 17 % ratio ($1,602 rent on $114,912 income).

Ownership patterns differ markedly. While 66 % of Frisco’s 219,304 residents live in owner‑occupied units, Katy’s smaller population of 25,184 enjoys an 81 % ownership rate. The higher ownership share in Katy suggests a community more oriented toward long‑term settlement and potentially lower turnover, which can translate into more stable neighborhood dynamics and lower property‑tax volatility for homeowners.

For buyers, Frisco’s higher price point often means a larger down‑payment and stricter financing thresholds, but it also offers access to a broader array of amenities, newer subdivisions, and a median resident age of 38.3 years—slightly older than Katy’s 37.6 years—indicating a mature, affluent demographic. Conversely, Katy’s more affordable median home value provides an entry point for first‑time buyers or families seeking the lower cost of living typical of the Greater Houston area, while still maintaining a strong ownership culture. These contrasting cost structures and ownership rates are key considerations for anyone weighing the financial and lifestyle implications of settling in either city.

What Each City Is Known For

Frisco is recognized for rapid growth, a Tree City USA designation since 2003, and its role in the McKinney‑Frisco urban area, which ranked 83rd nationally in 2020. Its location 25 miles from both Dallas Love Field and DFW International Airport makes it a commuter hub. Katy, rooted in historic railroad origins, is part of the Greater Houston region and sits on historic Karankawa tribal lands. Its growth followed Houston’s westward expansion along Interstate 10.

Who Should Choose Frisco or Katy

Frisco appeals to professionals and families who value higher incomes, a larger metropolitan environment, and access to Dallas‑area amenities. Its higher home prices suit buyers with strong purchasing power. Katy attracts those who prefer a smaller community, lower housing costs, and a higher rate of homeownership. It’s well‑suited for families seeking affordability while still enjoying proximity to Houston’s job market.

Frequently asked questions

Is Frisco or Katy cheaper to live in?

Katy is cheaper overall. Its median rent is $1,602 versus $2,014 in Frisco, and the median home value is $401,700 compared with $642,100 in Frisco.

Which has higher incomes, Frisco or Katy?

Frisco has higher incomes. The median household income is $150,212, which is $35,300 more than Katy’s $114,912.

Is rent higher in Frisco or Katy?

Rent is higher in Frisco at $2,014 per month, while Katy’s median rent is $1,602 per month.

Which is better for families, Frisco or Katy?

Both are family‑friendly, but Frisco offers more schools and amenities tied to a larger metro area, while Katy provides a tighter community feel and lower housing costs, which many families find attractive.

Is Frisco or Katy bigger?

Frisco is much bigger. Its population of 219,304 is about 194,120 more than Katy’s 25,184, making it roughly eight times larger.

The bottom line

Frisco and Katy each deliver a distinct blend of size, cost, and lifestyle. Choose Frisco for higher earnings and a big‑city edge, or Katy for affordability and a close‑knit community. Weigh the numbers against your priorities to find the Texas city that fits your next chapter.
