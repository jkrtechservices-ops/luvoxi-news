Main characters and the actors who portray them

The core of The Body revolves around eight teenage girls. Kristina Bogic plays Anya Butler, a sharp‑tongued newcomer who quickly becomes a leader in the group. Geena Meszaros portrays Lisbeth Anderson, the quiet but resilient member of the Lions dance team. Sofia Wylie appears as Grace Franklin, a charismatic dancer with a secret past. Jackson Kelly takes on the role of Leo Anderson, Lisbeth’s brother who provides a male perspective on the girls’ antics. Sara Boustany plays Elise Zakaria, the tech‑savvy strategist who keeps the group organized. Shirley Chen rounds out the main cast as Maddie, the outspoken friend who often pushes the group into trouble. Together, these actors bring a mix of humor, drama, and 2000s nostalgia to the screen.

What the series is about and when it begins

The Body follows a group of eight Catholic school girls and the Lions dance team as they navigate friendship, rivalry, and the pressures of teenage life. The story kicks off on Halloween night, a setting that sets the tone for the series’ blend of mischief and mystery. Over eight episodes, the characters confront secret crushes, family expectations, and the lure of rebellion, all framed by a soundtrack that echoes early‑2000s pop culture. The show’s premise is rooted in the tension between the strict school environment and the wild, carefree energy of the dance team, delivering a mix of comedy and drama that keeps viewers hooked from the first episode.

Who created The Body and the series’ tone

Quinn Shephard is the creator behind The Body, bringing her experience in teen‑focused storytelling to the project. Netflix markets the series as a ‘raunchy, sin‑filled ode to the 2000s,’ signaling a tone that is both nostalgic and unapologetically bold. The show balances explicit humor with heartfelt moments, reflecting the chaotic energy of early‑2000s teen dramas. This tone is reinforced by the Halloween‑night launch, which adds a spooky, rebellious flavor to the narrative. Shephard’s direction ensures that each episode feels like a snapshot of a bygone era, while still resonating with today’s audiences.
