Round Rock vs Cedar Park: Which City Fits Your Lifestyle?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Representative stock image via Pixabay, not a photo of the event.

Round Rock and Cedar Park are two fast‑growing suburbs north of Austin that often appear side by side in relocation decisions. While they share proximity to the capital, they differ in population size, income levels, housing costs and overall vibe. Understanding these differences helps you choose the community that aligns with your budget, family needs, and lifestyle preferences.

Key takeaways
  • Round Rock is larger but has lower median income.
  • Cedar Park’s homes cost about $95,000 more on average.
  • Rent takes a smaller share of income in Cedar Park.
  • Both cities attract families, but Cedar Park leans more affluent.

Population size and geographic footprint

Round Rock’s 2020‑2024 ACS estimate puts its population at 127,786, making it roughly 63% larger than Cedar Park’s 78,301 residents. Round Rock straddles both Williamson and Travis counties and sits about 15 miles north of Austin, while Cedar Park lies 16 miles northwest of downtown Austin and covers 25.6 square miles. The larger population gives Round Rock a broader range of retail options, such as IKEA and the Premium Outlets, whereas Cedar Park’s smaller footprint feels more like a tight‑knit suburb.

Household income and what it means for buying power

The median household income in Round Rock is $99,287, while Cedar Park’s median climbs to $129,545—about $30,258 (roughly 30 %) higher. This income gap translates into noticeably different buying power for everyday expenses and larger purchases. In Cedar Park, the higher earnings mean residents can allocate a larger share of their budget to discretionary items such as dining out, fitness memberships, and home‑improvement projects, while still keeping housing costs relatively affordable. Median rent in Cedar Park is $1,846 per month, just 5 % above Round Rock’s $1,763, but it represents only about 17 % of the city’s median income versus 21 % in Round Rock. Consequently, Cedar Park households retain a greater portion of their earnings after rent, expanding their capacity for savings or lifestyle upgrades.

Home‑ownership also reflects the income disparity. The median value of owner‑occupied homes in Cedar Park is $513,600—about $95,000 (23 %) higher than Round Rock’s $418,600. Moreover, a larger share of Cedar Park’s housing stock is owner‑occupied (67 % versus 56 % in Round Rock), indicating a more affluent, financially stable resident base. For prospective buyers, Cedar Park’s higher median income and home values suggest a market geared toward higher‑priced properties and a community with greater discretionary spending power, while Round Rock offers a solid, still‑above‑national‑average income environment with slightly lower cost pressures.

Rent costs and affordability ratios

Median gross rent in Round Rock is $1,763 per month, while Cedar Park’s median is $1,846, an $83 difference (about 5% higher). When expressed as a share of income, rent in Round Rock equals about 21% of the median household income, compared with roughly 17% in Cedar Park. Both cities keep rent below the 30% threshold often used to define affordable housing, but Cedar Park’s lower rent‑to‑income ratio makes it marginally more affordable for renters with higher earnings.

Home values, ownership rates, and what buyers face

The median value of owner‑occupied homes in Round Rock stands at $418,600, whereas Cedar Park’s median home price is $513,600—a $95,000 gap, or about 23% higher. Owner‑occupancy is 56% in Round Rock and 67% in Cedar Park, indicating a stronger propensity to own in Cedar Park. Prospective buyers should expect to pay more up front in Cedar Park, but the higher ownership rate suggests a stable, long‑term resident base.

What each city is known for

Round Rock is best known for being the international headquarters of Dell, employing roughly 16,000 people, and for its historic Old Town district with preserved buildings like the former Saint Charles Hotel. The city also features a mix of flat Blackland Prairie and hilly Hill Country terrain. Cedar Park’s identity is tied to its early railroad history, the Wilson‑Leonard archaeological site, and a network of major roads like U.S. Route 183 and the 183A Toll Road, which support commuter flow into Austin.

Who each city suits best

Round Rock’s larger size, slightly lower home prices and strong employment base make it attractive to young professionals and growing families seeking more space and a variety of shopping options. Cedar Park, with its higher incomes, higher home ownership rate and lower rent‑to‑income ratio, appeals to higher‑earning families and retirees who prioritize property investment and a more affluent neighborhood feel. Both cities have median ages in the mid‑30s, indicating a mix of young adults and families.

Frequently asked questions

Is Round Rock or Cedar Park cheaper to live in?

Round Rock’s median rent ($1,763) and home value ($418,600) are lower than Cedar Park’s $1,846 rent and $513,600 home price, making Round Rock the more affordable option overall.

Which has higher incomes, Round Rock or Cedar Park?

Cedar Park leads with a median household income of $129,545, which is about $30,258 higher than Round Rock’s $99,287.

Is rent higher in Round Rock or Cedar Park?

Rent is slightly higher in Cedar Park at $1,846 per month versus $1,763 in Round Rock.

Which is better for families, Round Rock or Cedar Park?

Both cities are family‑friendly, but Round Rock’s larger population and more diverse retail options may suit growing families, while Cedar Park’s higher home‑ownership rate and higher incomes attract families seeking a more affluent, stable neighborhood.

Is Round Rock or Cedar Park bigger?

Round Rock is bigger, with a population of 127,786 compared to Cedar Park’s 78,301.

The bottom line

Round Rock offers a larger community and lower housing costs, while Cedar Park provides higher incomes, higher home ownership and slightly better rent affordability. Your choice should hinge on whether you prioritize space and price (Round Rock) or income potential and a more upscale residential feel (Cedar Park).
