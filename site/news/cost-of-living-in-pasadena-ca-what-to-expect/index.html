Housing Costs You Need to Know

Renters in Pasadena pay a median gross rent of $2,265 each month. This figure includes all typical utilities and fees, giving a realistic picture of monthly out‑of‑pocket costs. For buyers, the median value of owner‑occupied homes sits at $1,093,300, which places Pasadena among the higher‑priced markets in Southern California. About 43% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, meaning a sizable portion of residents own rather than rent. Prospective residents should compare these numbers to their own income and savings to determine affordability.

Income Levels and Purchasing Power

The median household income in Pasadena stands at $105,192 per year, according to the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey (ACS) 5‑year estimates. This figure is notably higher than the national median, reflecting the city’s relatively affluent, well‑educated population and its proximity to high‑paying sectors such as technology, biotech, and the arts. With a population of roughly 136,969 residents and a median age of 40.1 years, many households are in their prime earning years, often supported by dual‑income earners.

When you pair that income with local housing costs, the picture becomes clearer. The median gross rent is $2,265 per month, while the median value of owner‑occupied homes reaches $1,093,300. About 43 % of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating that a sizable portion of residents are shouldering mortgage payments rather than rent. For a typical dual‑income family earning the median income, the rent alone consumes roughly 26 % of gross earnings, leaving enough room for utilities, transportation, childcare, and discretionary spending. However, single‑earner households or those with lower-than‑median wages may find the same housing costs more burdensome, as the rent could exceed 40 % of their income.

Because purchasing power varies widely, prospective residents should map out their personal budget—factoring in taxes, insurance, and lifestyle expenses—against the $105,192 benchmark. Doing so will reveal whether Pasadena’s high‑quality amenities and vibrant culture fit comfortably within their financial reality.

Population Size and Community Demographics

Pasadena’s estimated population of 136,969 — as reported by the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey (ACS) 5‑year estimates—places the city in the mid‑size tier for the Los Angeles metropolitan area. This population size supports a diverse housing stock: roughly 43 % of occupied units are owner‑occupied, while the remaining 57 % are rentals. The median gross rent sits at $2,265 per month, and the median value of owner‑occupied homes is $1,093,300, reflecting a market that ranges from historic Craftsman bungalows near the Rose Boulevard corridor to modern apartments and condominiums in the downtown core.

The median household income in Pasadena is $105,192, indicating a relatively affluent community that can sustain a variety of boutique shops, fine‑dining establishments, and cultural institutions such as the Norton Simon Museum and the Pasadena Playhouse. At a median age of 40.1 years, the city’s residents are generally mature professionals and families, which shapes the local service landscape: you’ll find well‑stocked farmers’ markets, high‑quality schools, and health‑focused amenities like yoga studios and specialty clinics.

Understanding these demographic details helps newcomers gauge the social vibe of Pasadena. The blend of owner‑occupied neighborhoods and rental‑dense areas fosters a mix of long‑term residents and transient students or young professionals, creating a community that values both stability and cultural dynamism. This demographic mix influences everything from the types of events hosted on Colorado Boulevard to the availability of community resources such as senior centers and family‑oriented recreation programs.

What the Cost of Living Means for Daily Life

Everyday expenses in Pasadena are shaped by the housing market and income levels. Grocery prices, transportation costs, and entertainment fees tend to align with the city’s overall affluence. While rent and home values dominate the budget, other costs such as utilities, health care and dining out are also higher than the national average. Residents often balance these expenses by taking advantage of local resources like farmers’ markets, public transit routes and community events that offer affordable alternatives.

Planning Your Move to Pasadena, CA

If you’re planning to relocate, start by mapping out your budget around the median rent of $2,265 and the median home value of $1,093,300.
