What Is the Median Income in Coral Springs, FL?

The U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey reports a median household income of $93,602 in Coral Springs. This figure places the city above the national median, indicating a relatively affluent community. Higher earnings can offset the cost of housing, utilities, and everyday expenses, but they also influence local price levels for goods and services.

How Much Does Rental Housing Cost?

Renters in Coral Springs can expect a median gross rent of $2,084 per month, according to the same ACS data set. This amount reflects the typical cost for a standard apartment or house rental in the area. Rent prices can vary by neighborhood, size, and amenities, but $2,084 serves as a reliable benchmark for budgeting purposes.

What Are Home Prices Like?

For prospective homebuyers, Coral Springs sits toward the higher end of the South‑Florida market. The median value of owner‑occupied properties is $545,400, according to the 2020‑2024 American Community Survey. This figure encompasses the city’s mix of single‑family homes and townhouses, which tend to range from modest three‑bedroom ranches in established neighborhoods to newer, larger builds in gated communities. Because roughly 60 % of the 54,000 occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, demand for purchase‑ready inventory remains steady, helping to sustain property values even when neighboring markets experience volatility.

Affordability is tempered by the city’s strong economic profile. The median household income in Coral Springs is $93,602, well above the national average, giving many families the financial capacity to secure a mortgage on a $545k home. At the same time, the median gross rent sits at $2,084 per month, indicating that renting is comparatively pricey and that many residents prefer to invest in ownership rather than continue paying high rental rates.

Demographically, the city’s median age of 37.5 years reflects a sizable cohort of mid‑career professionals and young families—precisely the group most likely to seek homeownership. With a population of 136,103, Coral Springs offers a stable, family‑friendly environment where property ownership is both a cultural norm and a sound financial decision, contributing to the city’s overall real‑estate resilience.

Who Lives in Coral Springs?

Coral Springs is a thriving, family‑oriented community whose demographic profile reflects a stable, middle‑class population. The 2020‑2024 American Community Survey estimates the city’s population at 136,103, with a median age of 37.5 years—a clear indication that the bulk of residents are working‑age adults, many of whom have school‑age children. This age distribution drives demand for quality public and private schools, after‑school programs, and youth sports leagues, all of which are plentiful in the city’s well‑maintained parks and recreation facilities.

Economic indicators reinforce the picture of a relatively affluent suburb. The median household income stands at $93,602, comfortably above the national median, allowing many families to afford homeownership and discretionary spending on leisure activities. Indeed, about 60 % of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, with the median value of those homes at $545,400. For renters, the median gross rent is $2,084 per month, reflecting a rental market that caters to both young professionals and families transitioning to homeownership.

Together, these figures suggest a community where dual‑income households are common, and where residents prioritize quality of life amenities such as well‑rated schools, healthcare facilities, and a variety of recreational options—from golf courses to cultural events. Understanding this demographic mix helps prospective movers anticipate the types of services, schools, and community activities that are most prevalent in Coral Springs.

How Do Demographics Influence Daily Costs?

A sizable portion of homeowners (about 60%) means many households have mortgage payments rather than rent, affecting overall spending patterns. Higher median incomes and home values often correlate with elevated costs for groceries, transportation, and personal services. However, the community’s stability can also bring benefits like well‑maintained infrastructure and consistent local business offerings.
