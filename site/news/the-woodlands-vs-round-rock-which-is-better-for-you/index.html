The Woodlands vs Round Rock: Which Is Better for You?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 10, 2026 · 4 min read

The Woodlands and Round Rock are two fast‑growing Texas communities that often appear on relocation lists. Both sit near major cities—The Woodlands north of Houston and Round Rock just north of Austin—but they differ in population size, earnings, housing costs and overall vibe. This guide breaks down the numbers and what each place is known for, so you can see which fits your budget and lifestyle.

Key takeaways
  • The Woodlands earns $41,414 more per household than Round Rock.
  • Home values are about $93,100 higher in The Woodlands.
  • Round Rock has a slightly larger population.
  • Rent takes a smaller share of income in The Woodlands.

Population and Land Area

The Woodlands is a special‑purpose district and census‑designated place with an estimated population of 121,002. It covers 43.9 square miles, almost all of it land. Round Rock, a city in Williamson and Travis counties, is a bit larger in headcount at 127,786 residents, roughly 6% more than The Woodlands. While The Woodlands spreads across Montgomery and a sliver of Harris County, Round Rock sits about 15 miles north of Austin and straddles the Balcones Escarpment, giving it a mix of flat prairie and hill country terrain.

Median Household Income

Earnings are a major dividing line. The Woodlands reports a median household income of $140,701, which is $41,414—or about 42%—higher than Round Rock’s median of $99,287. This gap reflects the concentration of large corporate campuses in The Woodlands, such as Occidental Petroleum and Halliburton, while Round Rock’s economy is anchored by Dell’s headquarters and a growing retail sector.

Rent Costs and Affordability

Rent is close in both markets but the income ratio differs. The median gross rent in The Woodlands is $1,822 per month, just $59 more than Round Rock’s $1,763. Because The Woodlands’ median income is much higher, rent represents about 16% of household earnings, whereas in Round Rock it consumes roughly 21%. For renters on a tighter budget, Round Rock’s lower rent‑to‑income percentage may feel more manageable.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Homeownership rates and home values paint a clear picture of the financial landscape in each community. In The Woodlands, a striking 73 percent of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, compared with only 56 percent in Round Rock. This higher ownership rate dovetails with a median home price of $511,700 in The Woodlands—about $93,100 (22 percent) more than Round Rock’s median of $418,600. The premium reflects The Woodlands’ higher median household income of $140,701, which is roughly $41,400 (42 percent) above Round Rock’s $99,287.

Because incomes are larger, the cost of renting is proportionally lower in The Woodlands: median gross rent sits at $1,822 per month, roughly 16 percent of the local median income. In Round Rock, rent averages $1,763 per month, representing about 21 percent of its median income. Both figures are close, but the lower rent‑to‑income ratio in The Woodlands suggests slightly more affordable rental housing relative to earnings.

The demographic context reinforces these trends. The Woodlands’ population of 121,002 is a bit smaller than Round Rock’s 127,786, yet its residents are older (median age 40.6 vs. 36.2) and generally wealthier, which supports the higher home‑ownership percentage and property values. Prospective buyers who prioritize equity building and are comfortable with a higher price tag may find The Woodlands more appealing, while those seeking a lower entry price and a higher proportion of renters might lean toward Round Rock.

What Each Community Is Known For

The Woodlands is praised for its master‑planned layout, extensive park system, and corporate presence. Its Township board provides municipal services, and the area has earned a Special Award for Excellence from the Urban Land Institute. Round Rock, meanwhile, is recognized for its historic Old Town, the Dell headquarters, and major retail draws like IKEA and Premium Outlets. Its proximity to Austin gives residents easy access to the state capital’s cultural scene while retaining a distinct small‑city feel.

Who Is Best Suited for Each City

If high earnings, larger homes and a suburban feel with corporate amenities appeal to you, The Woodlands aligns well with professionals and families seeking upscale living. Its median age of 40.6 suggests a mature, settled population. Round Rock attracts younger families and tech‑oriented workers who value a slightly lower cost of living, a vibrant historic downtown, and a shorter commute to Austin. The median age of 36.2 points to a younger demographic.

Frequently asked questions

Is The Woodlands or Round Rock cheaper to live in?

Round Rock is generally cheaper. Its median rent is $1,763 versus $1,822 in The Woodlands, and home values are $418,600 compared with $511,700. Rent also takes a larger share of income in Round Rock, making everyday costs lower for many households.

Which has higher incomes, The Woodlands or Round Rock?

The Woodlands has higher incomes. The median household income is $140,701, which is $41,414—about 42%—more than Round Rock’s median of $99,287.

Is rent higher in The Woodlands or Round Rock?

Rent is slightly higher in The Woodlands at $1,822 per month, compared with $1,763 in Round Rock.

Which is better for families, The Woodlands or Round Rock?

Both suit families, but The Woodlands offers higher homeownership rates and larger median home values, appealing to families seeking more space and upscale amenities. Round Rock provides a younger community vibe, lower overall costs and easy access to Austin’s schools and jobs.

Is The Woodlands or Round Rock bigger?

Round Rock is bigger in population, with 127,786 residents versus The Woodlands’ 121,002.

The bottom line

The Woodlands shines for higher earnings, homeownership and a polished suburban environment, while Round Rock offers a slightly larger, younger community with lower housing costs and close ties to Austin. Your choice should hinge on budget, desired lifestyle and proximity to work or cultural hubs.
