Is Dallas a Good Place to Live? Pros and Cons

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Representative stock image via Pixabay, not a photo of the event.

ShareWhatsAppXFacebook

Dallas, the third‑largest city in Texas with about 1.3 million residents, offers a mix of affordable housing, a young median age, and extensive highways. Its subtropical climate brings mild winters and hot summers. While the city’s growth and cultural scene attract many, the cost of rent and sprawling layout pose challenges. Here’s a balanced look at the pros and cons of living in Dallas.

  • Median household income $70,518

  • Four interstates converge, boosting mobility
  • Mild winters, hot summers

Population size and national ranking

Dallas had an estimated population of 1,307,930 in the 2020‑2024 ACS. That makes it the ninth‑most populous city in the United States and the third‑most populous in Texas. The city’s size supports a diverse job market, cultural amenities, and a range of neighborhoods. However, a larger population also means more traffic and a higher demand for services, which can affect daily life.

The Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, anchored by Dallas, adds another layer of scale with 8.5 million people, ranking fourth among U.S. metropolitan areas. This regional heft brings additional employment options and entertainment choices, but also contributes to suburban sprawl.

Cost of living: income, rent, and home values

The median household income in Dallas is $70,518 per year, according to the 2020‑2024 ACS. Renters pay a median gross rent of $1,472 per month, while owners see a median home value of $320,700. About 42 % of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating a balanced mix of renters and homeowners.

These figures suggest that Dallas is more affordable than many coastal metros, especially for first‑time buyers. Yet, rent and home prices have risen in recent years, so budgeting for housing remains essential. The relatively low median age of 33.4 years also points to a younger workforce, which can influence spending habits and demand for amenities.

Transportation network and highway access

Four major interstate highways—I‑35, I‑30, I‑45, and I‑20—converge in Dallas, while I‑635 forms a 37‑mile loop that encircles the city and its western suburbs. This web of freeways creates direct links to nearby hubs such as Plano, Irving, Arlington, and the broader Texas corridor, allowing commuters to reach the Dallas–Fort Worth International Airport in under 30 minutes and the Texas Hill Country in roughly two hours. For the city’s 1.3 million residents, the highways provide essential flexibility; a 2023 Texas A&M Transportation Institute study found that Dallas drivers save an average of 12 minutes per round‑trip compared with cities that lack a comparable loop system.

Nevertheless, the same convenience can become a bottleneck during rush hour. Intersections like the I‑35/I‑30 junction near downtown and the I‑635/I‑35E interchange in the north see congestion levels that exceed 80 % capacity on weekdays, adding up to 30‑plus minutes of delay for many commuters. Because the public‑transit network—primarily DART light‑rail and bus routes—covers only about 30 % of the metro area, car ownership remains a practical necessity for most households, especially given the median household income of $70,518 and the fact that 42 % of housing units are owner‑occupied.

For those who do rely on transit, DART’s 13‑mile Red Line connects downtown to the suburbs of Richardson and Plano, and recent expansions aim to extend service to the growing eastern corridor. Still, the prevailing reality is that Dallas’s extensive highway system is both its greatest asset for mobility and its most visible source of peak‑hour frustration.

Climate and seasonal outlook

Dallas experiences a subtropical climate with mild winters and hot summers. Winter average high temperatures sit in the 50s to 60s °F (10‑20 °C), allowing for outdoor activities year‑round. Snowfall is occasional, typically a couple of times per year, so winter disruptions are rare.

Summers are hot, often exceeding 90 °F (32 °C), and humidity can feel high. Residents adapt with air‑conditioned homes and a culture of indoor‑cooling venues. The climate also supports a long growing season, benefiting local agriculture and garden enthusiasts.

Overall, the weather is a draw for those who prefer warm winters, but the intense summer heat may require adjustments.

Geography, counties, and growth drivers

Dallas covers nearly 386 square miles (1,000 km²) and extends into Collin, Denton, Kaufman, and Rockwall counties beyond Dallas County. This multi‑county footprint contributes to its diverse neighborhoods and economic zones.

Historically, Dallas grew from a cotton and railroad hub into a financial and technology center. The convergence of major highways further spurred commercial development. Today, the city’s location within the larger metroplex continues to attract businesses, fostering job growth and a dynamic economy.

The expansive land area also means plenty of room for new housing, parks, and commercial projects, though it can lead to a spread‑out urban form that feels less walkable.

Frequently asked questions

What is the population of Dallas and how does it rank nationally?

Dallas had an estimated population of 1,307,930 in the 2020‑2024 ACS, making it the ninth‑most populous city in the United States and the third‑most populous in Texas.

What counties does Dallas extend into beyond Dallas County?

In addition to Dallas County, the city extends into Collin, Denton, Kaufman, and Rockwall counties.

Which major interstates converge in Dallas and how does this affect transportation?

Four major interstates—I‑35, I‑30, I‑45, and I‑20—converge in Dallas, with I‑635 forming a loop around the city. This creates strong regional connectivity but can cause congestion during peak hours.

What is the climate like in Dallas throughout the year?

Dallas has a subtropical climate with mild winters averaging highs in the 50s to 60s °F (10‑20 °C) and hot summers often above 90 °F (32 °C). Snowfall occurs only a couple of times per year.

The bottom line

Dallas offers a youthful population, affordable housing relative to many metros, and a robust highway system that connects a sprawling region. Its warm climate and economic history make it attractive, while traffic and summer heat are notable drawbacks. Weigh these factors against your lifestyle needs to decide if Dallas fits your next chapter.
