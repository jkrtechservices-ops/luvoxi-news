Scottsdale vs Chandler: Which City Is Right for You?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Scottsdale and Chandler are two of the fastest‑growing cities in the Phoenix metro area. Both offer high median incomes and strong job markets, but they differ in population size, housing costs and overall vibe. Understanding these differences helps you choose the community that matches your budget, family needs and lifestyle preferences.

Key takeaways
  • Scottsdale is smaller and older, Chandler is larger and younger.
  • Median income is slightly higher in Scottsdale.
  • Rent is a few dollars more in Scottsdale, but home prices are 56% higher.
  • Homeownership rates are similar, around two‑thirds of households.
  • Scottsdale leans toward retirees and luxury, Chandler attracts families and tech workers.

Size and Population

Scottsdale covers 184.5 square miles and had an estimated population of 243,821 in the 2020‑2024 ACS. Chandler spans 65.55 square miles with a population of 280,136, about 15% larger than Scottsdale. The larger headcount in Chandler reflects its role as a major suburb in the East Valley, while Scottsdale’s lower density aligns with its reputation as a more spread‑out, resort‑style community.

Median Household Income

Scottsdale’s median household income — $110,886—edges just above Chandler’s $108,095, a difference of $2,791 (about 3 percent). Both figures sit far beyond the national median, underscoring the relative affluence of the East Valley. However, income alone does not tell the whole story; the cost of housing and the demographic profile of each city shape how far that income stretches.

In Scottsdale, the higher earnings are matched by a premium housing market. The median gross rent is $2,013 per month, roughly 22 percent of the median household income, while the median value of owner‑occupied homes sits at $789,800—about $282,000 (56 percent) more than Chandler’s $507,800. With 67 percent of occupied units owner‑occupied, many residents are investing in high‑value properties, which can offset the modest income advantage.

Chandler, by contrast, offers slightly lower rent at $1,902 per month (about 21 percent of its median income) and more affordable home values. About 65 percent of its housing is owner‑occupied, and the median home price of $507,800 reflects a market that is roughly $282,000 cheaper than Scottsdale’s. This affordability can make Chandler’s median income stretch farther for families and first‑time buyers.

Population size also plays a role: Chandler’s 280,136 residents (≈15 percent more than Scottsdale’s 243,821) create a larger labor pool and more diverse housing options. Ultimately, while Scottsdale residents earn a touch more on average, the higher cost of rent and homeownership often makes Chandler the more budget‑friendly choice for those weighing income against everyday expenses.

Rent Prices and Affordability

The median gross rent in Scottsdale is $2,013 per month, about $111 higher than Chandler’s $1,902. When expressed as a share of median household income, rent in Scottsdale equals roughly 22% of earnings, while Chandler’s rent is about 21% of earnings. Both cities keep rent below the 30% affordability threshold, but Chandler offers a marginally cheaper rental market.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Owner‑occupied homes in Scottsdale command a premium, with a median value of $789,800—about 56 % higher than Chandler’s $507,800. This price gap reflects Scottsdale’s reputation for upscale, resort‑style neighborhoods, high‑end shopping districts, and extensive amenities that attract affluent buyers. Despite the steeper price tag, 67 % of Scottsdale’s occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, only slightly above Chandler’s 65 % rate, indicating a strong commitment to homeownership even in a market where many might consider renting.

The financial picture supports this trend. Scottsdale’s median household income sits at $110,886, roughly 3 % higher than Chandler’s $108,095, giving residents a modest income cushion to absorb higher mortgage payments. Correspondingly, the median gross rent in Scottsdale is $2,013 per month—about 6 % more than Chandler’s $1,902—yet both cities keep rent at roughly 21–22 % of median household income, a level generally deemed affordable.

Demographically, Scottsdale’s older median age of 49 years suggests many residents are in later career stages, often with greater equity and purchasing power, whereas Chandler’s younger median age of 37.5 years points to a larger share of first‑time buyers and families seeking more affordable entry points. Ultimately, if you prioritize luxury homes, higher income potential, and a mature community, Scottsdale’s higher home values align with that lifestyle; if you prefer a more moderate price point while still enjoying a solid ownership rate, Chandler offers a compelling alternative.

What Each City Is Known For

Scottsdale is famous for its upscale resorts, golf courses and the historic Old Town district, earning the slogan “The West’s Most Western Town.” Its proximity to the McDowell Mountains and Tonto National Forest adds outdoor appeal for retirees and affluent buyers. Chandler, by contrast, is a tech hub anchored by Intel’s two facilities and a growing Price Road Corridor of jobs and investment. The city’s suburban layout and family‑friendly neighborhoods make it popular with younger professionals and growing families.

Who Each City Suits Best

If you are looking for a higher‑end lifestyle, luxury housing and a community with a median age of 49 years, Scottsdale aligns well with retirees, empty‑nesters and those who value resort amenities. Chandler’s median age of 37.5 years, larger population and strong tech employment base make it a better fit for young families, first‑time homebuyers and professionals seeking a more affordable entry point into homeownership.

Frequently asked questions

Is Scottsdale or Chandler cheaper to live in?

Chandler is generally cheaper. Its median rent is $1,902 versus $2,013 in Scottsdale, and its median home value is $507,800 compared with $789,800 in Scottsdale.

Which has higher incomes, Scottsdale or Chandler?

Scottsdale has a slightly higher median household income at $110,886, while Chandler’s median is $108,095.

Is rent higher in Scottsdale or Chandler?

Rent is higher in Scottsdale. The median gross rent is $2,013 per month, compared with $1,902 in Chandler.

Which is better for families, Scottsdale or Chandler?

Chandler tends to be more family‑oriented. Its younger median age of 37.5 years, larger population and more affordable home values attract families and first‑time buyers.

Is Scottsdale or Chandler bigger?

Chandler is bigger in terms of population, with 280,136 residents versus Scottsdale’s 243,821.

The bottom line

Scottsdale offers higher incomes, luxury housing and a resort‑style atmosphere suited to older, affluent residents. Chandler provides a larger, younger population, more affordable homes and a strong tech job market, making it attractive for families and young professionals. Weighing rent, home values and lifestyle priorities will guide you to the city that fits your next chapter.
