Is San Antonio a Good Place to Live? Pros and Cons

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 21, 2026 · 5 min read

San Antonio offers a mix of affordable housing, a young population and a rich cultural scene, making it attractive to many newcomers. With a median household income of $65,056 and median rent of $1,324, the city balances cost and quality of life, though traffic and summer heat can be challenges. Here’s a practical look at the pros and cons.

Cost of Living and Housing Market

San Antonio’s housing market remains one of the most affordable among large U.S. cities. The median value of owner‑occupied homes sits at $235,700, while the median gross rent is $1,324 per month. About 52% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating a balanced mix of renters and buyers. Median household income is $65,056, which helps many residents afford these costs without stretching budgets. Compared with national averages, both home prices and rents are lower, giving newcomers more purchasing power.

However, rising demand can push prices up, especially in neighborhoods close to downtown attractions and the River Walk. Prospective buyers should budget for property taxes and maintenance, while renters might face modest increases as the market tightens.

Demographics and Community Life

With an estimated population of 1,479,835, San Antonio ranks as the seventh‑most populous city in the United States. The Greater San Antonio metropolitan area expands that number to 2.76 million, making it the third‑largest metro area in Texas. The median age of residents is 34.9 years, reflecting a youthful workforce and a vibrant social scene.

The city’s diversity is evident in its cultural events, most notably Fiesta week, which draws about 3.5 million visitors to over one hundred events each year. This influx adds energy to local businesses and creates seasonal job opportunities. Residents enjoy a blend of historic sites, such as the Alamo, and modern amenities, fostering a sense of community that appeals to families, young professionals and retirees alike.

Geography, Climate and Outdoor Recreation

San Antonio covers 465.4 square miles, with 460.93 square miles of land and 4.5 square miles of water. The city sits at an altitude of approximately 662 feet (202 m) above sea level. Its primary drinking water source is the Edwards Aquifer, supplemented by Victor Braunig Lake and Calaveras Lake, which were impounded in 1962 and 1969 respectively.

The climate features hot summers and mild winters, typical of South‑Central Texas. Residents often enjoy outdoor activities along the San Antonio River Walk and at nearby lakes. The city’s location—about 75 miles southwest of Austin, 150 miles north of the Mexican border, 190 miles west of Houston and 250 miles south of Dallas–Fort Worth—makes weekend trips to other major Texas destinations convenient.

Transportation and Connectivity

San Antonio is well‑served by several transportation options. Major highways such as I‑35, I‑10 and I‑37 connect the city to Austin, Houston and the Mexican border, facilitating road travel for commuters and freight. The San Antonio International Airport offers domestic flights and limited international service, linking the region to larger hubs.

Public transit includes VIA Metropolitan Transit’s bus network, which covers most neighborhoods and downtown. While the city does not have a commuter rail system, ongoing plans aim to expand transit options to reduce traffic congestion. For cyclists and pedestrians, the River Walk and expanding trail system provide safe, scenic routes for daily travel and recreation.

Pros and Cons Summary

Pros and Cons Summary

San Antonio’s appeal begins with its affordability. The median home value sits at just $235,700, and median gross rent is $1,324 per month—both well below the national averages—making it one of the most budget‑friendly large cities in the United States. Nearly half of the housing stock (52 %) is owner‑occupied, and the median household income of $65,056 provides a comfortable cushion for most families and young professionals. The city’s median age of 34.9 years reflects a youthful, dynamic population that fuels a vibrant cultural scene, from the 3.5 million‑person Fiesta week to year‑round museum exhibitions and live‑music venues. Its central Texas location—75 miles from Austin, 150 miles from the Mexican border, and within a three‑hour drive of Houston, Dallas–Fort Worth, and the Gulf Coast—offers easy access to regional job markets and travel corridors, while the Edwards Aquifer and nearby lakes secure ample water resources for a growing metropolitan area of 2.76 million residents.

On the downside, the climate can be a challenge; summer temperatures routinely climb into the high 90s °F with high humidity, prompting higher utility bills and limiting outdoor activities. Traffic congestion on I‑35, I‑10, and Loop 1604 is a growing headache, especially during rush hour and major events like Fiesta. Public‑transit options remain limited—San Antonio lacks a commuter rail system, relying heavily on buses and rideshare services. Finally, rising demand for housing could push prices upward over time, potentially eroding the current cost advantage. Weighing these strengths and drawbacks against your personal priorities will help determine whether San Antonio aligns with your lifestyle goals.

Frequently asked questions

What is the population of San Antonio and its metropolitan area?

San Antonio has an estimated 1,479,835 residents, while the Greater San Antonio metro area totals about 2.76 million people.

How far is San Antonio from major Texas cities and the Mexican border?

It lies roughly 75 miles southwest of Austin, 190 miles west of Houston, 250 miles south of Dallas–Fort Worth and 150 miles north of the Mexican border.

What are the main sources of drinking water for San Antonio?

The city relies primarily on the Edwards Aquifer, with supplemental supplies from Victor Braunig Lake and Calaveras Lake.

What transportation options are available for getting to and around San Antonio?

Interstates I‑35, I‑10 and I‑37 serve road travel; San Antonio International Airport handles air travel; VIA Metropolitan Transit provides bus service; and the River Walk and trail network support walking and cycling.

The bottom line

San Antonio balances affordability, a youthful demographic and cultural richness against hot summers and growing traffic. By matching these attributes with your personal needs, you can decide whether the city’s advantages outweigh its drawbacks.
