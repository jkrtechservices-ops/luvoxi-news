Is Scottsdale, AZ a Good Place to Live? Pros and Cons

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Scottsdale, AZ scores high on income and amenities, but its hot summers and pricey housing can be challenging. With a population of about 243,821, a median household income of $110,886 and a median home value near $789,800, the city offers comfort for many, yet cost considerations remain. Below we weigh the key pros and cons to help you decide if Scottsdale fits your lifestyle.

Key takeaways
  • High median income and strong tourism economy
  • Housing costs above national average
  • Arid climate with extreme summer heat
  • Rapid growth and extensive outdoor recreation

Population Size and Growth Trends

Scottsdale’s 2020-2024 American Community Survey estimates the city’s population at 243,821, up from 241,361 in the 2020 census and 217,385 in 2010. The city’s growth reflects its status as one of the fastest‑expanding housing markets in the United States over the past two decades. Residents tend to be older, with a median age of 49 years, which influences the community’s pace and services. The city’s incorporation in 1951 began with just 2,000 people, showing how dramatically it has expanded.

These numbers signal a stable, mature community, but also suggest that newcomers may encounter a competitive housing market and a population skewed toward retirees and established professionals.

Housing Costs and Affordability

Homeownership is common in Scottsdale, with about 67% of occupied housing units owned rather than rented. The median value of owner‑occupied homes stands at $789,800, while the median gross rent is $2,013 per month. These figures place Scottsdale well above the national median, reflecting strong demand and limited supply.

Prospective residents should weigh the high median income of $110,886 against these costs. While the income level can support the market for many, those on tighter budgets may find the price points restrictive. Renters also face a premium, though the city’s rental market remains robust due to steady demand from workers in the tourism sector.

Geography, Boundaries and Climate

Scottsdale occupies 184.5 square miles in eastern Maricopa County, stretching 31 miles from north to south. It borders Phoenix to the west, the Tonto National Forest to the north, the McDowell Mountains to the east, and the Salt River to the south. The highest point, Butte Peak, reaches 4,890 ft.

The city sits in the Salt River Valley, also called the “Valley of the Sun,” within the Sonoran Desert. Its arid climate brings mild winters and extremely hot summers. Record temperatures range from a low of 16.0 °F on January 7, 1913, to a high of 122 °F on June 26, 1990. Residents enjoy abundant sunshine year‑round, but summer heat can be intense, influencing outdoor activities and energy use.

Economic Base and Major Employers

Tourism is the engine that powers Scottsdale’s economy, accounting for the majority of jobs and a sizable share of the city’s tax base. The city’s world‑renowned resorts, championship golf courses, upscale art galleries, and desert‑adventure attractions draw millions of visitors each year, sustaining a robust hospitality sector that includes hotels, restaurants, boutique shops, and entertainment venues. In 2023, the tourism industry alone supported roughly 30 % of the city’s employed workforce, making it the single largest employer in the metropolitan area.

The prosperity generated by this visitor‑driven economy is reflected in Scottsdale’s high median household income of $110,886, well above the national average. While the local economy has diversified over the past decade—adding health‑care providers, technology firms, and financial services—the city still feels the seasonal pulse of tourism. Summer heat can depress resort occupancy, while major events such as the Scottsdale Culinary Festival or the Waste Management Phoenix Open create temporary spikes in hiring for hospitality, retail, and event‑staff positions.

Despite these fluctuations, the job market remains resilient. The city’s 2020‑2024 ACS data shows a stable employment rate, with strong demand for skilled workers in hotel management, culinary arts, and retail merchandising. Moreover, the high home‑ownership rate (about 67 % of occupied units) and median home value of $789,800 indicate that many residents benefit from the steady income streams generated by tourism‑related businesses, reinforcing Scottsdale’s reputation as a financially vibrant place to live and work.

Lifestyle, Recreation and Community Feel

Scottsdale’s slogan, “The West’s Most Western Town,” captures its blend of modern amenities and desert heritage. Residents benefit from proximity to the McDowell Mountains and Tonto National Forest, offering hiking, biking and horseback riding. The city’s layout includes extensive park systems and a vibrant downtown with galleries, boutiques and dining.

The median age of 49 suggests a community oriented toward mature lifestyles, with many amenities catering to retirees and professionals. While the cost of living is high, the combination of high income, cultural offerings and outdoor recreation makes Scottsdale attractive for those who value a polished, active environment.

Frequently asked questions

What is the population of Scottsdale, AZ according to the most recent census?

The 2020 census recorded Scottsdale’s population at 241,361. The latest ACS 5‑year estimate (2020‑2024) places it at 243,821, showing modest growth since 2010.

What are the geographic boundaries of Scottsdale and its neighboring cities?

Scottsdale covers 184.5 square miles, stretching 31 miles north‑to‑south. It borders Phoenix on the west, Tonto National Forest to the north, the McDowell Mountains to the east, and the Salt River to the south.

What is the climate like in Scottsdale, including record high and low temperatures?

Scottsdale has an arid climate with mild winters and extremely hot summers. The record low is 16.0 °F (−8.9 °C) on January 7, 1913, and the record high is 122 °F (50 °C) on June 26, 1990.

Which industry employs the most people in Scottsdale?

Tourism is the city’s primary employer, supporting jobs in hotels, restaurants, retail and related services throughout Scottsdale.

The bottom line

Scottsdale offers high income, strong tourism jobs and abundant outdoor recreation, but its housing costs and scorching summers may deter some. Weigh these factors against your budget and lifestyle to decide if Scottsdale, AZ is the right fit.
