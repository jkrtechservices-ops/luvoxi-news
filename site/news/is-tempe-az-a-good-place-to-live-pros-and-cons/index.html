Is Tempe, AZ a Good Place to Live? Pros and Cons

Tempe, AZ offers a youthful vibe, solid median income, and easy transit, but higher rents and desert heat can be challenging. With a population of about 188,065 and a median household income of $79,663, the city balances urban amenities with the costs of a growing inner‑suburb of Phoenix.

Economic Opportunities and Job Market

Tempe hosts a cluster of corporate headquarters, including DriveTime, the only Fortune 500 company based in the city. Other notable firms such as Benchmark Electronics, Carvana, GoDaddy, NortonLifeLock, First Solar, the Salt River Project, Circle K, and Fulton Homes also maintain headquarters here. This concentration of employers creates a diverse job market across tech, finance, manufacturing, and renewable energy. The median household income of $79,663 reflects the relatively strong earning potential for residents. While the presence of these companies can boost career prospects, competition for positions can be intense, especially for recent graduates.

The city’s economic health benefits from its proximity to Phoenix and the East Valley, allowing commuters to tap into a broader regional job pool while enjoying Tempe’s urban amenities.

Housing Costs and Homeownership

Housing affordability is a mixed picture in Tempe. The median gross rent stands at $1,743 per month, which is higher than many surrounding suburbs. For those looking to buy, the median value of owner‑occupied homes is $455,400. About 42% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating a sizable rental market alongside homeownership. Prospective residents should weigh the higher rent against the median household income of $79,663 to gauge budget fit. The city’s dense, urbanized northern area near the Valley Metro Line often commands premium prices, while neighborhoods farther south may offer more modest options.

Potential buyers and renters should also consider the 40.2‑square‑mile footprint of the city, which includes limited water space, influencing development patterns and property availability.

Education, Youthful Demographics, and Lifestyle

At the core of Tempe’s identity is Arizona State University, the nation’s third‑largest public university by enrollment. The campus draws more than 70,000 students, faculty, and staff, which pushes the city’s median age to just 30 years—a figure that is markedly younger than the national median. This concentration of college‑aged residents creates a lively, entrepreneurial atmosphere that spills onto the streets in the form of late‑night coffee shops, craft breweries, and a rotating calendar of concerts, film festivals, and art installations at venues such as the Tempe Center for the Arts.

The youthful demographic also shapes the housing market. Only about 42 % of occupied units are owner‑occupied, while the median gross rent sits at $1,743 per month and the median home value is $455,400. The demand for affordable, transit‑friendly living has encouraged the city to expand its free circulator buses (Orbit and Flash) and to integrate the Valley Metro Light Rail, which threads through downtown and connects to Phoenix and Mesa. These transportation options make it easier for students and young professionals to commute without a car, reinforcing Tempe’s reputation as a walkable, bike‑friendly community.

Beyond the campus, the presence of Fortune‑500 headquarters such as DriveTime and First Solar provides internships and entry‑level positions that complement ASU’s robust research programs. Together, the university’s academic resources, the city’s youthful population, and the supportive infrastructure create a dynamic environment that appeals to students, recent graduates, and anyone seeking an energetic, forward‑looking lifestyle.

Transportation Options in Tempe

Tempe’s transit network is robust for an inner suburb. Residents can ride two free neighborhood circulator buses—Orbit and Flash—available on weekdays and weekends. The Valley Metro Light Rail runs through the city, linking Tempe with Mesa and Phoenix, making commuting without a car feasible. Additionally, major roadways and proximity to Phoenix provide easy access for drivers. The dense development near the northern part of the city, especially close to the Light Rail line, encourages walkability and transit‑oriented living.

These options help offset the higher cost of housing by reducing the need for multiple vehicles and provide flexibility for students, professionals, and families alike.

Climate and Outdoor Recreation – Is Tempe, AZ a Good Place to Live?

Tempe experiences a hot‑desert climate (Köppen BWh) with high diurnal temperature variation. Summers are extremely hot, while winters are mild, offering plenty of sunshine year‑round. The Salt River runs west through the northern part of the city and is dammed to create Tempe Town Lake, a popular spot for kayaking, paddleboarding, and festivals. Tempe Center for the Arts, opened in 2007, adds cultural depth with a 600‑seat theater and smaller performance spaces. The city’s history of hosting the Fiesta Bowl and Insight Bowl highlights its capacity for large‑scale events.

While the heat can be intense, many residents appreciate the dry climate and outdoor lifestyle. Adequate water features and shaded public areas help mitigate the temperature extremes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the population of Tempe, Arizona?

Tempe’s population is estimated at 188,065 according to the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey.

What major university is located in Tempe?

Arizona State University, one of the largest public universities in the United States by enrollment, has its main campus in Tempe.

What are the primary transportation options in Tempe?

Tempe offers free circulator buses (Orbit and Flash), the Valley Metro Light Rail, and a network of major roadways, providing both local and regional connectivity.

Which Fortune 500 company is headquartered in Tempe?

DriveTime is the only Fortune 500 company headquartered in Tempe.

What is the climate like in Tempe?

Tempe has a hot‑desert climate with very high summer temperatures, mild winters, and a large daily temperature swing.

The bottom line

Tempe, AZ blends a strong job market, youthful culture, and extensive transit with higher rent and desert heat. Weigh the income potential against housing costs and climate preferences to decide if it fits your lifestyle.
