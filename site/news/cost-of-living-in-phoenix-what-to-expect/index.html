Cost of Living in Phoenix: What to Expect

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 24, 2026 · 4 min read

Phoenix’s cost of living balances affordable housing with a solid median income. Rent sits around $1,582 per month, while the median home price is $420,700. With a median household income of $81,332, many residents find the city manageable, especially given its young median age of 34.9 years. This guide breaks down what you’ll pay for housing, income expectations, and who lives here.

How Much Does Housing Cost in Phoenix?

Renters can expect a median gross rent of $1,582 each month. This figure reflects the middle of the market, meaning half of rentals cost more and half cost less. Homebuyers face a median owner‑occupied home value of $420,700. About 57% of occupied housing units are owned rather than rented, indicating a strong owner presence. These numbers give a baseline for budgeting whether you’re looking to rent an apartment or purchase a house.

What Income Levels Support the Phoenix Lifestyle?

The median household income in Phoenix stands at $81,332 per year. This income level supports the median rent and home values described above, allowing many families and individuals to afford typical housing costs. While personal budgets vary, this median figure provides a useful benchmark for evaluating job offers or salary expectations in the area.

Who Calls Phoenix Home?

Phoenix’s population, according to the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey 5‑year estimates, stands at 1,642,323 residents. The city’s median age is 34.9 years, indicating a relatively youthful demographic that fuels a dynamic cultural scene and a robust labor market. This age profile translates into a sizable cohort of young professionals drawn to the city’s expanding tech, healthcare, and aerospace sectors, as well as families attracted by the comparatively affordable cost of living.

Income levels reflect this mix: the median household income in Phoenix is $81,332 per year, comfortably above the national median and supportive of a middle‑class lifestyle. Housing data reinforce the city’s appeal: 57 % of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, with the median value of those homes at $420,700. For renters, the median gross rent is $1,582 per month, which, while higher than the national average, remains lower than many coastal metros. These figures illustrate a market where both buying and renting are attainable for a broad spectrum of residents.

The combination of a young, educated workforce, moderate incomes, and a housing market that balances ownership and rental options makes Phoenix an attractive destination for newcomers. Whether it’s a recent graduate seeking an affordable launchpad for a career, a tech professional looking for a growing hub, or a family craving space and community amenities, the demographic and economic profile of Phoenix positions it as a vibrant, yet cost‑conscious, place to call home.

How Does Home Ownership Compare to Renting?

Owner‑occupied units account for roughly 57 % of all occupied housing in Phoenix, a figure that aligns closely with the city’s demographic and economic profile. With a 2020‑2024 ACS‑estimated population of 1,642,323 and a median household income of $81,332, many residents find home ownership financially attainable, especially when the median value of an owner‑occupied home sits at $420,700. For a typical household earning the median income, a conventional 30‑year mortgage at current rates translates to a monthly principal‑and‑interest payment of about $2,200, plus taxes and insurance—still comparable to the median gross rent of $1,582 per month.

The age distribution also plays a role: the median Phoenix resident is 34.9 years old, an age when many families are looking to settle down and invest in property rather than remain in transient rental arrangements. This youthful, upward‑mobile cohort contributes to the city’s relatively high ownership rate and helps stabilize neighborhoods, as owners are more likely to maintain their homes and stay longer in a given community.

Renters, who make up the remaining 43 % of occupied units, often face rising rent prices that outpace wage growth, prompting some to consider purchasing as a hedge against inflation. Meanwhile, the strong owner presence fuels local investment—home improvement projects, property‑tax revenues, and community involvement—all of which reinforce Phoenix’s growing reputation as a city where buying a home is a realistic and attractive option for a majority of its residents.

What Factors Influence Overall Cost of Living?

Beyond housing and income, Phoenix’s cost of living is shaped by its young median age, large population, and the proportion of owners versus renters. These elements combine to create a market where housing remains a central expense, but the median income helps keep overall costs within reach for many households.

Frequently asked questions

Is the median rent in Phoenix affordable?

The median rent of $1,582 per month aligns with the median household income of $81,332, making it affordable for many residents, especially when compared to national averages.

How many people live in Phoenix?

Phoenix’s estimated population is 1,642,323, according to the latest American Community Survey data.

What is the typical age of Phoenix residents?

The median age of people living in Phoenix is 34.9 years, reflecting a relatively young community.

Do most Phoenix residents own their homes?

Yes, about 57% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating a majority of residents prefer home ownership.

The bottom line

Phoenix offers a cost of living where median rent and home prices are supported by a solid median income. Understanding these figures helps you plan a realistic budget and decide if the city matches your financial goals.
