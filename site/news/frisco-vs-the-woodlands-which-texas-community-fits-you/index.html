Frisco vs The Woodlands: Which Texas Community Fits You?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Frisco and The Woodlands are two of Texas' most sought‑after suburbs, each offering high incomes, strong home ownership and a family‑friendly vibe. Frisco is larger and a bit richer, while The Woodlands boasts a higher ownership rate and slightly lower costs. This guide breaks down the numbers and what each place is known for, so you can see which community aligns with your priorities.

Key takeaways
  • Frisco is 81% larger than The Woodlands.
  • Median household income is about 7% higher in Frisco.
  • Home values are 25% higher in Frisco.
  • Both cities have rent at roughly 16% of median income.
  • The Woodlands has a higher owner‑occupancy rate.

Population size and density

Frisco’s 2020‑2024 ACS estimate puts the population at 219,304, while The Woodlands counts 121,002 residents. That makes Frisco about 98,302 people, or roughly 81%, larger than The Woodlands. Frisco spreads across 62.4 square miles, with only 0.92% water, giving it a lower overall density than The Woodlands, which is a special‑purpose district within Montgomery and Harris counties. The larger population means more amenities, schools and businesses clustered within the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex, whereas The Woodlands feels more like a planned community set 28 miles north of Houston.

Median household income and what it means

The median household income in Frisco is $150,212, compared with $140,701 in The Woodlands. Frisco’s income is $9,511, about 7% higher. Higher earnings often translate into greater discretionary spending power and a broader tax base for public services. Both cities sit well above the national median, indicating a generally affluent resident base. The slight edge in Frisco may appeal to professionals seeking higher salary benchmarks, while The Woodlands still offers a comfortable income level for most middle‑class families.

Rent costs and affordability ratio

Median gross rent in Frisco sits at $2,014 per month, while The Woodlands’ median is $1,822—a $192 gap that translates to roughly an 11 % premium for the larger Dallas‑Fort Worth suburb. When you pair those figures with each community’s median household income—$150,212 in Frisco and $140,701 in The Woodlands—the rent‑to‑income ratio in both places is about 16 %, a level that most housing affordability guidelines deem acceptable. For renters, The Woodlands therefore offers a modest savings edge, especially for those on tighter budgets, while Frisco’s higher rent reflects its larger population (219,304 versus 121,002) and its proximity to a broader array of DFW employment centers.

The affordability picture is further clarified by homeownership patterns. Approximately 66 % of Frisco’s occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, compared with 73 % in The Woodlands, indicating a slightly stronger buying culture in the Houston‑area community. Nonetheless, the median value of owner‑occupied homes is markedly higher in Frisco ($642,100) than in The Woodlands ($511,700), a $130,400 difference that underscores the premium placed on Frisco’s larger market and its reputation for newer, higher‑priced developments. Ultimately, renters who prioritize cost savings may lean toward The Woodlands, while those who value the amenities and job access that come with a bigger, faster‑growing city may find the extra $192 per month in Frisco a worthwhile trade‑off.

Home values, ownership rates and market pressure

Owner‑occupied home values differ sharply. Frisco’s median home price is $642,100, whereas The Woodlands’ median is $511,700—a $130,400 gap, or about 25% higher in Frisco. Ownership rates also vary: 66% of Frisco’s occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, while The Woodlands reaches 73%. The higher ownership share in The Woodlands suggests a more stable, long‑term resident base, whereas Frisco’s market may attract more investors and newer families looking to buy in a fast‑growing area.

What each community is known for

Frisco is part of the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex, just 25 miles from both Dallas Love Field and DFW International Airport. It earned the Tree City USA designation in 2003 and was the fastest‑growing U.S. city in 2017 and from 2000‑2009. Its roots trace back to a 1902 railway watering stop. The Woodlands, by contrast, is a master‑planned district celebrated for its corporate campuses—Occidental Petroleum, Chevron Phillips, Huntsman, and Halliburton—and won a 1994 Special Award for Excellence from the Urban Land Institute. Its design emphasizes green spaces and a township governance model.

Who each city suits best

Frisco’s larger size, higher incomes and proximity to Dallas make it a strong fit for young professionals, tech‑savvy commuters and families who value a wide selection of schools, shopping and entertainment options. The Woodlands, with its higher owner‑occupancy rate, slightly lower home prices and corporate hub, appeals to established families, retirees and professionals who prefer a more self‑contained community with easy access to Houston’s job market. Both cities offer a median age in the high‑30s to early‑40s, indicating a mix of families and seasoned residents.

Frequently asked questions

Is Frisco or The Woodlands cheaper to live in?

The Woodlands is generally cheaper. Its median rent is $1,822 versus $2,014 in Frisco, and median home values are $511,700 compared with $642,100 in Frisco.

Which has higher incomes, Frisco or The Woodlands?

Frisco has higher median household income at $150,212, while The Woodlands reports $140,701.

Is rent higher in Frisco or The Woodlands?

Rent is higher in Frisco. The median gross rent is $2,014 per month, compared with $1,822 in The Woodlands.

Which is better for families, Frisco or The Woodlands?

Both are family‑friendly, but The Woodlands’ higher ownership rate and slightly lower costs may suit families seeking stability, while Frisco’s larger schools and amenities attract families who want more options.

Is Frisco or The Woodlands bigger?

Frisco is bigger. Its population of 219,304 is about 98,302 (81%) higher than The Woodlands’ 121,002 residents.

The bottom line

In short, Frisco offers a larger, higher‑income market with higher home prices, while The Woodlands provides a more affordable, ownership‑focused environment with strong corporate ties. Your decision should hinge on whether you prioritize size and proximity to Dallas or a slightly lower cost of living and a tighter community feel.
