What Is the Median Household Income in Naperville?

The U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey reports that the median household income in Naperville stands at $155,105 per year. This amount represents the middle point of all household earnings, meaning half of the city’s households earn more and half earn less. Compared with national averages, Naperville’s median income is notably higher, indicating a relatively affluent community.

For families and individuals evaluating job opportunities or relocation, this median figure provides a baseline for expected earnings in the local labor market. It also influences tax revenue, public services, and community investment.

How Does Income Relate to Housing Costs?

Housing expenses are a key factor when assessing the purchasing power of a median income. In Naperville, the median gross rent is $1,885 per month, while the median value of owner‑occupied homes is $540,200. Renters typically allocate a portion of their income to monthly payments, and homeowners often consider the median home value when budgeting for mortgages.

When the median household earns $155,105 annually, the rent level represents roughly 15% of that income, aligning with conventional affordability guidelines. Homeownership costs, including mortgage, taxes, and maintenance, will vary, but the median home value offers a reference point for prospective buyers.

What Is the Typical Age of Earners in Naperville?

The median age of Naperville residents is 40.4 years, according to the 2020‑2024 American Community Survey. This figure marks the midpoint of the city’s age distribution, meaning roughly half of the population is younger and half is older than 40.4. In practical terms, the bulk of earners are in their mid‑career phase, often holding managerial or professional positions that command higher salaries. With a median household income of $155,105, these middle‑aged workers help sustain one of the nation’s most affluent suburban economies.

Because many of these earners are well into their professional trajectories, they tend to have stable employment histories, significant purchasing power, and a strong propensity for long‑term financial commitments. This is reflected in the housing market: about 75 % of occupied units are owner‑occupied, and the median value of those homes stands at $540,200. Renters, who make up the remaining 25 %, face a median gross rent of $1,885 per month, underscoring the overall high cost of living that accompanies higher incomes.

For businesses and service providers, the 40‑year‑old median age signals a demographic with established careers, family responsibilities, and a preference for quality amenities. Marketers targeting this group can focus on products and services that support family life, home improvement, and financial planning, while policymakers can use the data to shape housing, transportation, and educational initiatives that align with the needs of a stable, affluent, mid‑life population.

How Many Residents Own Their Homes?

Homeownership is a cornerstone of Naperville’s economic landscape, with roughly three‑quarters of all occupied housing units—about 75%—listed as owner‑occupied according to the 2020‑2024 American Community Survey. In a city of roughly 150,692 residents, this translates to more than 112,000 households that have chosen to purchase rather than rent. The high ownership rate dovetails with the city’s affluent profile: the median household income stands at $155,105, comfortably supporting the median value of owner‑occupied homes, which is $540,200.

Because a sizable share of residents have invested in property, neighborhoods tend to exhibit greater stability and longer‑term residency. Homeowners are more likely to maintain their properties, invest in renovations, and participate in local civic groups, all of which reinforce community cohesion. For prospective buyers, the 75% owner‑occupancy figure signals a market where long‑term investment is the norm rather than the exception, suggesting that resale values may remain robust and that the area enjoys relatively low turnover.

The contrast with the rental market further highlights this dynamic. While the median gross rent in Naperville is $1,885 per month, the strong preference for ownership means that rental units represent a smaller slice of the housing stock, often catering to transient populations such as students or short‑term employees. With a median resident age of 40.4 years, many homeowners are in a life stage where building equity and securing a stable environment are top priorities, reinforcing the city’s reputation as a family‑friendly, financially secure community.

What Does the Median Income Figure Mean for Newcomers?

For anyone moving to Naperville, the $155,105 median household income sets expectations for cost of living and lifestyle. It suggests that many households can comfortably afford the median rent of $1,885 and, if purchasing, can manage a home valued around $540,200. The city’s demographic profile—median age of 40.4 years and a 75% homeownership rate—indicates a community of established professionals and families.

New residents should compare their own earnings to the median figure to gauge affordability, consider local rental and purchase options, and plan budgets that reflect typical housing costs in the area.
