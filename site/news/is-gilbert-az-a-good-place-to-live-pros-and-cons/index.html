Is Gilbert, AZ a Good Place to Live? Pros and Cons

Gilbert, AZ ranks as a desirable suburb of Phoenix with a population of 287,285 in 2025, a median household income of $122,551, and a strong sense of community. Population, Demographics, and Community Size

Gilbert is the fourth‑most populous municipality in Arizona and the most populous incorporated town in the United States. The 2020‑2024 American Community Survey estimates a population of 280,262, while a 2025 count shows 287,285 residents. The median age is 36.1 years, indicating a relatively young community. About 73% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, reflecting stability and investment in the area. These figures suggest a growing, family‑friendly town with a solid base of homeowners.

Economic Outlook and Cost of Living

Household earnings in Gilbert are strong, with a median income of $122,551 per year. This financial health supports local businesses and services. However, housing costs are notable. The median value of owner‑occupied homes stands at $575,100, and the median gross rent is $2,110 per month. Prospective residents should weigh these costs against the income levels and consider budgeting for higher housing expenses compared to many other regions.

Climate and Outdoor Recreation

Gilbert’s climate is classified as hot‑arid (Köppen BWh), meaning summers often soar above 100°F (38°C) while winters remain mild to warm, with average lows in the mid‑50s°F (12‑13°C). Although the heat can be intense, the low humidity makes outdoor pursuits more tolerable than in many other desert cities, and the pleasant winter months invite residents to enjoy the town’s extensive recreation network year‑round.

Spanning 68.86 square miles—almost all of it land—Gilbert has devoted more than 600 acres to open‑space parks, nature trails, and natural preserves. These green corridors link over 40 well‑maintained sports fields, soccer complexes, baseball diamonds, and multi‑use courts, creating a seamless web for hikers, cyclists, and team‑sport enthusiasts. The town’s flagship spaces, such as the Heritage District Trail and the Gilbert Regional Park, offer paved paths shaded by native desert vegetation, while the Riparian Preserve provides bird‑watching and gentle walking routes that feel like a cool oasis after a hot day.

Because the median age in Gilbert is just 36.1 years, the community skews active, and the high median household income of $122,551 supports a robust investment in amenities, from well‑lit trail lighting to modern fitness stations. Even with the summer heat, residents frequently schedule early‑morning runs, evening bike rides, or weekend pick‑up games, taking advantage of the town’s commitment to an active, outdoor‑oriented lifestyle. This blend of climate‑adapted design and abundant open space makes Gilbert a town where staying fit is both practical and enjoyable.

Location and Accessibility

Situated southeast of Phoenix, Gilbert enjoys a strategic position that blends suburban tranquility with easy access to the bustling metropolitan corridor of the Valley. Covering nearly 69 square miles of mostly flat desert terrain, the town sits directly south of Mesa, northeast of Chandler, and northwest of the rapidly growing community of Queen Creek. This placement places Gilbert at the crossroads of three major freeways—Loop 202 to the north, the I‑10 to the south, and the I‑285 (the “Santan Freeway”) skirting its eastern edge—allowing residents to reach downtown Phoenix, the airport, and surrounding employment hubs in roughly 30 minutes. Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport, a key gateway for both business travelers and vacationers, is about a half‑hour’s drive away, making weekend getaways and long‑distance commutes remarkably convenient.

The town’s central location also supports a robust housing market: about 73 % of occupied units are owner‑occupied, with a median home value of $575,100, while renters face a median gross rent of $2,110 per month. With a 2025 population of 287,285—the fourth‑largest municipality in Arizona and the most populous incorporated town in the United States—Gilbert’s median age of 36.1 years reflects a youthful, family‑oriented community. High median household incomes of $122,551 further underscore the area’s economic vitality, enabling residents to take full advantage of the extensive network of open spaces, sports fields, and nearby cultural amenities while staying well‑connected to the greater Phoenix region.

Historical Roots and Community Identity

Gilbert was incorporated on July 6, 1920, and its origins trace back to 1902 when William "Bobby" Gilbert provided land for the Arizona Eastern Railway. Early development included Ayer's Grocery Store in 1910, which also housed the first post office in 1912. The town grew with the settlement of Mormons fleeing Mexican colonies in 1912, forming the Gilbert Ward in 1918. These historical milestones shape a strong sense of identity and pride among residents.

Frequently asked questions

What is the population of Gilbert, AZ?

Gilbert's population was estimated at 280,262 in the 2020‑2024 ACS and reached 287,285 in 2025, making it the fourth‑most populous municipality in Arizona.

When was Gilbert incorporated?

Gilbert was incorporated on July 6, 1920, establishing its official status as a town in Maricopa County.

What climate does Gilbert have?

Gilbert has a hot arid climate (Köppen BWh) with sweltering summers and mild to warm winters, typical of the Phoenix metropolitan area.

How far is Gilbert from Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport?

The town is about 30 minutes from Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport, providing convenient access for travelers.

The bottom line

Gilbert, AZ offers a growing, affluent community with extensive recreation space and easy airport access. While housing costs are high, the town’s strong economy and family‑oriented atmosphere make it a compelling option for many prospective residents.
