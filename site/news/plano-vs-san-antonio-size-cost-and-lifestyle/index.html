Plano vs San Antonio: Size, Cost, and Lifestyle

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 23, 2026 · 5 min read

Plano and San Antonio offer very different living experiences. Plano is a smaller, affluent suburb of Dallas‑Fort Worth with high incomes and higher housing costs. San Antonio is a large, historic city with lower costs and a younger median age. Your choice depends on budget, family needs, and the kind of community you prefer.

Key takeaways
  • Plano is smaller but wealthier.
  • San Antonio has lower rent and home prices.
  • Both cities have similar homeownership rates.

Population and Geographic Scale

Plano’s 2020‑2024 ACS estimate puts its population at 290,594. It spreads over 71.6 square miles, making it a compact suburb within the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex. San Antonio is far larger, with an estimated 1,479,835 residents and a land area of about 460.93 square miles. In other words, San Antonio’s population is 1,189,241 higher, roughly 409% larger than Plano’s. The size difference means San Antonio feels more like a big city with diverse neighborhoods, while Plano offers a tighter‑knit community feel.

Income Levels and What They Mean

Median household income in Plano stands at $112,253, more than $47,000—about 73 %—higher than San Antonio’s $65,056. This gap reflects the city’s concentration of corporate headquarters (Frito‑Lay, Toyota North America, JCPenney, Pizza Hut) and a workforce dominated by high‑paying tech, finance, and professional services. The higher earnings translate into a larger pool of discretionary spending; Plano residents can more comfortably afford frequent dining out, upscale shopping, and a wider array of cultural events. However, the upside is balanced by a costlier housing market: the median gross rent in Plano is $1,841 per month—roughly 20 % of the median income—while the median value of owner‑occupied homes sits at $465,900, nearly double San Antonio’s $235,700.

San Antonio’s median income of $65,056 points to a more modest economic profile, bolstered by a mix of service‑sector jobs, military employment, and tourism. Rent averages $1,324 per month, about 24 % of household income, and home values are significantly lower, making homeownership more attainable for a larger share of the population. The city’s younger median age (34.9 vs. Plano’s 39.6) and slightly lower owner‑occupancy rate (52 % vs. 57 %) suggest a more mobile, renter‑heavy demographic. In practice, Plano’s higher disposable income supports a lifestyle of premium amenities but comes with steeper housing costs, whereas San Antonio offers a lower‑cost, more flexible living environment that appeals to families and younger professionals seeking affordability over extravagance.

Rent Costs and Affordability

The median gross rent in Plano is $1,841 per month, compared with $1,324 in San Antonio. Plano’s rent is $517 higher, about 39% more. When expressed as a share of income, rent in Plano equals about 20% of median household income, while in San Antonio it equals about 24%. Both cities keep rent below the conventional 30% affordability threshold, but San Antonio provides a cheaper monthly bill for renters.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Owner‑occupied homes in Plano command a median value of $465,900, almost double the $235,700 median in San Antonio—a gap of $230,200, or roughly 98 percent higher. This disparity mirrors the broader income picture: Plano’s median household income is $112,253, compared with $65,056 in San Antonio, a difference of $47,197 (about 73 percent). Consequently, the cost of renting also diverges; Plano’s median gross rent is $1,841 per month, 39 percent more than San Antonio’s $1,324, yet both cities keep rent at a similar share of income—about 20 percent of Plano’s median earnings versus 24 percent in San Antonio. Homeownership rates follow the same trend, with 57 percent of occupied housing units in Plano owned by residents, versus 52 percent in San Antonio. The higher ownership share in Plano reflects its affluent, suburban character within the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex, where many professionals are attracted by corporate headquarters such as Frito‑Lay and Toyota North America. In contrast, San Antonio’s larger, younger population (median age 34.9 versus 39.6 in Plano) and lower price point provide a more accessible entry for first‑time buyers and families seeking a lower‑cost foothold in a major Texas city. Together, these figures illustrate how Plano’s market caters to higher‑earning buyers, while San Antonio offers a comparatively affordable path to homeownership.

What Each City Is Known For

Plano is known as a corporate hub, hosting headquarters such as Frito‑Lay, JCPenney, Pizza Hut and Toyota Motor North America. Its history dates back to the 1840s, with early mills and a railway that spurred growth. San Antonio is famed for its Spanish colonial heritage, the Alamo, and a strong military presence. It sits on the Balcones Escarpment and relies on the Edwards Aquifer for water. These identities shape local culture, dining, and recreation.

Who Each City Suits Best

Plano’s higher incomes, strong corporate base and suburban layout appeal to professionals, dual‑income families, and those who prioritize schools and safety in a smaller community. The median age of 39.6 suggests a mature population. San Antonio’s lower costs, younger median age of 34.9 and larger urban environment attract budget‑conscious families, military personnel, and younger adults seeking cultural amenities without a high price tag. Both cities have similar homeownership percentages, so either can support renters or buyers, depending on budget.

Frequently asked questions

Is Plano or San Antonio cheaper to live in?

San Antonio is cheaper. Its median rent is $1,324 versus $1,841 in Plano, and its median home value is $235,700 compared with $465,900 in Plano.

Which has higher incomes, Plano or San Antonio?

Plano has higher incomes. The median household income is $112,253 in Plano and $65,056 in San Antonio.

Is rent higher in Plano or San Antonio?

Rent is higher in Plano. The median gross rent is $1,841 per month in Plano, while San Antonio’s median is $1,324.

Which is better for families, Plano or San Antonio?

Plano often suits families seeking higher incomes, corporate jobs and a suburban feel, while San Antonio works well for families wanting lower housing costs and a larger city atmosphere.

Is Plano or San Antonio bigger?

San Antonio is bigger. Its population of 1,479,835 far exceeds Plano’s 290,594, making it about 409% larger.

The bottom line

Plano offers wealth, corporate jobs and a tight‑knit suburb, but at a higher price. San Antonio provides a larger, historic city with lower rent and home costs, attracting younger and budget‑focused residents. Weigh income potential against affordability to choose the right fit.
