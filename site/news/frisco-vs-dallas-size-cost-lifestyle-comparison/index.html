Frisco vs Dallas: Size, Cost & Lifestyle Comparison

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 31, 2026 · 5 min read

When deciding between Frisco and Dallas, look at size, earnings, housing costs and overall vibe. Frisco is a smaller, wealthier suburb with higher home values and rent, while Dallas is a large metropolis with lower costs and more renters. Both sit in the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex, but they serve different life stages and priorities.

Key takeaways
  • Frisco is far smaller but richer than Dallas.
  • Rent is higher in Frisco, but it’s a lower share of income.
  • Home ownership is more common in Frisco.
  • Dallas offers more affordable housing and a younger median age.

Population and Land Area

Frisco’s 2020‑2024 ACS estimate puts its population at 219,304. Dallas, by contrast, counts 1,307,930 residents – about 1,088,626 more, roughly 496% higher. Frisco covers 62.4 square miles, with only 0.92% water, while Dallas spans 385.8 square miles, including 45.3 square miles of water. The size difference means Dallas feels like a big city with many neighborhoods, whereas Frisco feels more like a compact suburb.

Income Levels

Median household income is the most striking point of divergence between the two cities. Frisco’s median household income sits at $150,212, more than double Dallas’s $70,518—a gap of $79,694, or roughly 113 % higher (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2020‑2024). This wealth premium reflects Frisco’s relatively young, professional population (median age 38.3) that is drawn to the city’s corporate campuses, high‑tech parks, and newer mixed‑use developments. The higher earnings also support a robust home‑ownership rate: about 66 % of occupied housing units in Frisco are owner‑occupied, compared with just 42 % in Dallas. Consequently, the median value of owner‑occupied homes in Frisco is $642,100—about twice Dallas’s $320,700—while the median gross rent is $2,014 per month, roughly 37 % higher than Dallas’s $1,472.

Despite the higher earnings, housing costs in Frisco remain proportionally manageable. Median rent consumes about 16 % of a Frisco household’s income, whereas in Dallas rent accounts for roughly 25 % of median earnings, indicating a tighter affordability squeeze for many Dallas renters. The disparity in income also mirrors the cities’ demographic scales: Frisco’s population of 219,304 is a fraction of Dallas’s 1,307,930, yet the per‑capita economic power in Frisco is markedly greater. For prospective residents, these figures suggest that Frisco offers a higher‑earning environment with a stronger home‑ownership base, while Dallas provides a broader, more varied job market but with comparatively lower median wages and tighter rent‑to‑income ratios.

Rent Costs and Affordability

The median gross rent in Frisco stands at $2,014 per month, which is $542 (about 37%) higher than Dallas’s $1,472. Yet rent consumes about 16% of Frisco’s median household income, compared with roughly 25% in Dallas. This means renters in Frisco spend a smaller slice of their paycheck on housing, despite the higher dollar amount.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Frisco’s median owner‑occupied home value sits at $642,100, exactly twice the $320,700 median price found in Dallas—a gap of $321,400. This price disparity is reinforced by the city’s higher household income: the median Frisco family earns $150,212 annually, more than double Dallas’s $70,518. Because of that purchasing power, about 66 % of Frisco’s occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, compared with just 42 % in Dallas. The ownership gap reflects Frisco’s suburban, family‑oriented character, where residents are typically older (median age 38.3 versus Dallas’s 33.4) and have the financial means to buy rather than rent.

Renters also feel the income divide. Frisco’s median gross rent is $2,014 per month, roughly 16 % of the local median income, whereas Dallas renters pay $1,472 monthly, about 25 % of their median income. Even though Dallas’s rental market is larger, the higher rent‑to‑income ratio can strain renters more than in Frisco.

Population size further illustrates the contrast: Frisco’s 219,304 residents are dwarfed by Dallas’s 1,307,930, a difference of over 1 million people (about 496 % larger). Yet the smaller city commands a real‑estate market that rivals the larger metropolis, driven by its affluent, predominantly owner‑occupied housing stock and the lifestyle expectations that come with higher earnings.

What Each City Is Known For

Frisco is recognized for rapid growth – it was the fastest‑growing U.S. city in 2017 and from 2000 to 2009 – and for being a Tree City USA since 2003. Its location 25 miles from both Dallas Love Field and DFW International Airport makes it convenient for commuters. Dallas anchors the fourth‑largest metro area in the nation, with four major interstates converging and a historic role as a transportation and oil‑cotton hub.

Who Should Choose Frisco or Dallas

Frisco suits professionals and families seeking higher incomes, more home ownership, and a suburban feel with newer amenities. Its median age of 38.3 suggests a mature, settled community. Dallas appeals to younger adults, renters, and those who prefer a big‑city environment with diverse neighborhoods, lower housing costs and easy access to a wide range of cultural and employment options. Its median age of 33.4 reflects a younger population.

Frequently asked questions

Is Frisco or Dallas cheaper to live in?

Dallas is cheaper overall. Median rent is lower ($1,472 vs $2,014) and home values are about half of Frisco’s. However, rent takes a larger share of income in Dallas (about 25%) than in Frisco (about 16%).

Which has higher incomes, Frisco or Dallas?

Frisco has higher median household income at $150,212, compared with Dallas’s $70,518 – roughly $79,694 more.

Is rent higher in Frisco or Dallas?

Rent is higher in Frisco at $2,014 per month, while Dallas’s median rent is $1,472 per month.

Which is better for families, Frisco or Dallas?

Frisco generally fits families better with higher incomes, more home ownership (66% vs 42%), and a suburban environment. Dallas offers more affordable options but a larger, more transient population.

Is Frisco or Dallas bigger?

Dallas is far larger, with a population of 1,307,930 versus Frisco’s 219,304 – about 496% more residents.

The bottom line

In short, Frisco delivers higher earnings, more ownership and a suburban feel at a premium price, while Dallas offers a larger, younger city with lower housing costs and greater rental options. Choose the one that matches your budget, lifestyle and family needs.
