Round Rock vs Houston: Size, Cost, and Lifestyle Comparison

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 19, 2026 · 4 min read

When choosing a new home in Texas, Round Rock and Houston offer very different experiences. Round Rock is a midsize city with high incomes and a tech‑focused economy, while Houston is a sprawling metropolis with lower costs but a far larger population. This guide breaks down the key numbers and characteristics so you can decide which environment matches your priorities.

Key takeaways
  • Round Rock has higher median income but also higher rent and home values.
  • Houston is far larger, cheaper to rent, and has a lower home‑ownership rate.
  • Round Rock suits professionals and families seeking a smaller, affluent community.
  • Houston appeals to those who want city amenities and more affordable housing.

Population and Geographic Scale

Round Rock’s estimated population is 127,786, making it a modestly sized city within the Greater Austin area. By contrast, Houston’s population stands at 2,328,253, more than 2.2 million people larger – about 1722% higher than Round Rock. Houston also covers 640.4 square miles, ranking it among the nation’s largest cities by area. The sheer scale of Houston means more neighborhoods, a wider variety of cultural venues, and a denser urban core, while Round Rock feels more like a close‑knit community with easy access to Austin’s amenities.

Income Levels and Purchasing Power

The median household income in Round Rock is $99,287, roughly $34,474 (about 53%) higher than Houston’s median of $64,813. Higher earnings in Round Rock reflect the presence of major employers such as Dell’s international headquarters, which employs about 16,000 people locally. In Houston, the lower median income is offset by a broader range of job sectors, from energy to healthcare, but the overall purchasing power is less than in Round Rock.

Rent Costs and Affordability Ratios

Median gross rent in Round Rock is $1,763 per month, about $402 (30%) higher than Houston’s $1,361. When measured against household income, rent in Round Rock equals roughly 21% of the median income, while Houston’s rent consumes about 25%. Although Houston’s rent is cheaper in absolute terms, the higher income share in Round Rock means renters there may feel slightly less strain relative to earnings.

Home Values and Ownership Rates

Owner‑occupied homes in Round Rock carry a median value of $418,600, which is $140,800—about 51 %—higher than the $277,800 median in Houston. This price premium reflects Round Rock’s strong demand, driven by its proximity to Austin, a thriving tech corridor anchored by Dell’s headquarters and a growing retail scene that includes IKEA, Premium Outlets, and the mixed‑use La Frontera center. The city’s median household income of $99,287 also outpaces Houston’s $64,813 by $34,474 (roughly 53 %), giving residents greater purchasing power and supporting the higher home‑price ceiling.

Homeownership is more prevalent in Round Rock, with 56 % of occupied housing units owned versus just 42 % in Houston. The higher ownership rate dovetails with a relatively affluent, younger demographic—the median age in Round Rock is 36.2 years, compared with 34.4 years in Houston—many of whom are drawn to the city’s blend of suburban amenities and easy access to Austin’s job market. While the median gross rent in Round Rock ($1,763 per month) is $402 higher than Houston’s $1,361, it still represents roughly 21 % of the local median income, a slightly lower affordability ratio than Houston’s 25 % share.

Together, these figures illustrate a clear trade‑off: Round Rock offers higher‑valued, more widely owned homes and stronger household earnings, whereas Houston provides a broader spectrum of entry‑level housing options and a lower cost of entry for first‑time buyers.

What Each City Is Known For

Round Rock is best known for its tech presence, especially Dell’s headquarters, and for retail hubs like IKEA, Premium Outlets, and the mixed‑use La Frontera center. Its historic “Old Town” area preserves early Texas architecture. Houston, the fourth‑largest city in the United States, is famous for its energy industry, diverse cultural scene, and proximity to the Gulf of Mexico. It also serves as the county seat of Harris County and anchors a metropolitan area of 7.8 million residents.

Who Should Choose Which City

Round Rock suits professionals seeking higher salaries, a smaller community feel, and easy access to Austin’s tech ecosystem. Families looking for higher home‑ownership rates and newer suburban amenities may also prefer Round Rock. Houston is a better fit for those who value a wide array of cultural, culinary, and employment options within a large urban environment, and who want lower rent or more affordable home purchase prices. The city’s size also offers more niche neighborhoods for varied lifestyles.

Frequently asked questions

Is Round Rock or Houston cheaper to live in?

Houston is cheaper overall. Its median rent is $1,361 versus $1,763 in Round Rock, and home values average $277,800 compared with $418,600. However, Round Rock’s higher median income offsets some of the cost difference.

Which has higher incomes, Round Rock or Houston?

Round Rock has the higher median household income at $99,287, which is $34,474 more than Houston’s $64,813.

Is rent higher in Round Rock or Houston?

Rent is higher in Round Rock. The median gross rent is $1,763 per month, about $402 more than Houston’s $1,361.

Which is better for families, Round Rock or Houston?

Round Rock may be better for families seeking higher home‑ownership rates (56% vs 42%) and newer suburban amenities. Houston offers more diverse neighborhoods and cultural options, which some families prefer.

Is Round Rock or Houston bigger?

Houston is far bigger. Its population is 2,328,253, more than 2.2 million people higher than Round Rock’s 127,786, and it spans 640.4 square miles.

The bottom line

Round Rock delivers higher earnings and a tighter community at a premium price, while Houston provides scale, lower housing costs, and a broader cultural canvas. Match your budget, career goals, and lifestyle preferences to the numbers above to decide which Texas city fits you best.
