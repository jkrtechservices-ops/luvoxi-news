Population of Frisco, TX: Size, Age and Housing Overview

Frisco, Texas, is home to an estimated 219,304 residents according to the 2020-2024 American Community Survey. The city spans 61.8 square miles of land, sits at a median age of 38.3 years, and enjoys a median household income of $150,212. About two‑thirds of its housing units are owner‑occupied, with a median rent of $2,014 and a median home value of $642,100. These figures give a clear snapshot of the size, age and housing landscape in Frisco.

Current population and recent growth

The U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey estimates Frisco’s population at 219,304. This follows a 2020 census count of 200,509, reflecting continued growth after the city was the fastest‑growing in the nation in 2017. The surge is part of a broader trend in the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, where Frisco contributes to a larger urban area of more than half a million residents.

Geographic size and location

Frisco covers a total area of 62.4 square miles, with 61.8 square miles of land and 0.58 square miles of water. The city sits in both Collin and Denton counties, placing it roughly 25 miles from Dallas Love Field and Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport. Its position within the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex makes it a convenient hub for commuters and travelers alike.

Age profile of residents

The median age of Frisco residents is 38.3 years, a figure that places the city squarely in the “young‑but‑mature” category. This age profile reflects a community in which many households are in the child‑rearing stage, yet a sizable portion of the population is also well‑established in their careers. In 2020‑2024 the city’s estimated population reached 219,304, up from 200,509 in the 2020 census, underscoring a steady influx of new families and professionals drawn by Frisco’s high‑quality schools, abundant recreational amenities, and its reputation as a family‑friendly enclave within the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex.

Economic data dovetail with the age distribution: the median household income stands at $150,212, allowing many residents to afford the city’s relatively high housing costs. Owner‑occupied units make up roughly 66 % of all occupied housing, with a median home value of $642,100, while renters face a median gross rent of $2,014 per month. These numbers suggest that a majority of the 38‑year‑old median cohort is financially positioned to purchase homes, reinforcing the city’s strong owner‑occupancy rate.

Frisco’s appeal to this demographic is amplified by its Tree City USA designation (held since 2003), extensive park system, and proximity—about 25 miles—to both Dallas Love Field and Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport. Together, these factors create a vibrant, age‑balanced environment where young families can settle, grow, and remain long‑term residents.

Housing market basics

Housing in Frisco leans heavily toward home ownership, with about 66% of occupied units owned by residents. The median value of owner‑occupied homes stands at $642,100. For renters, the median gross rent is $2,014 per month. These numbers reflect a robust housing market that balances affordability with the city’s higher median household income of $150,212.

Economic backdrop and income

Frisco’s median household income of $150,212—more than double the national median—reflects a robust, diversified economic base anchored by corporate headquarters, technology firms, and a thriving retail sector. This high earning power translates into strong consumer spending that fuels local businesses, from boutique restaurants in the Frisco Square district to large‑scale entertainment venues like the National Videogame Museum. The affluent tax base also underwrites some of the city’s most valued public assets, including top‑ranked schools, well‑maintained parks, and an extensive network of bike lanes and trails that enhance quality of life and draw new families.

Housing markets mirror the income profile. Owner‑occupied units make up roughly 66 % of all occupied dwellings, with a median home value of $642,100. Renters face a median gross rent of $2,014 per month, indicating a competitive rental environment that still benefits from the city’s strong employment landscape. The median age of residents, 38.3 years, suggests a predominantly prime‑working‑age population, many of whom are professionals and young families seeking both career opportunities and suburban amenities.

Geographically, Frisco sits within the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, about 25 miles from both Dallas Love Field and DFW International Airport, positioning it as a convenient hub for commuters and businesses alike. Its rapid growth—evidenced by a 2020‑2024 ACS estimate of 219,304 residents—combined with a high income level, reinforces Frisco’s reputation as an attractive, economically vibrant community within the broader McKinney‑Frisco urban area of over half a million people.

Frequently asked questions

What was Frisco’s population according to the most recent census?

The 2020 U.S. census recorded 200,509 people living in Frisco. More recent ACS estimates (2020‑2024) place the population at 219,304.

How large is Frisco in terms of land area?

Frisco has 61.8 square miles of land. Including water, the total area is 62.4 square miles.

What is the population of the larger McKinney‑Frisco urban area?

The U.S. Census Bureau defines the McKinney‑Frisco urban area as having 504,803 residents as of the 2020 census.

Which counties is Frisco located in?

Frisco spans two counties: Collin County and Denton County in the state of Texas.

How far is Frisco from major Dallas airports?

Frisco is about 25 miles from both Dallas Love Field and Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport, making air travel easily accessible.

The bottom line

Frisco, TX combines rapid growth with strong economic fundamentals, offering a sizable, relatively young population and a solid housing market. Whether you’re considering moving, investing, or simply learning about the area, these key statistics provide a practical snapshot of the city’s size, age and housing landscape.
