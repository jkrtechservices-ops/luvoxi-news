Explore Gilbert Arizona Suburbs: Neighborhoods, Stats & Tips

Gilbert, Arizona sits southeast of Phoenix and is often called a Phoenix suburb. With a 2025 population of 287,285, it offers a mix of family‑friendly neighborhoods, solid median incomes and a range of local attractions. This guide breaks down the surrounding suburbs, key demographics, housing costs, things to see and how to get around, so you can decide if Gilbert’s suburbs fit your lifestyle.

Geography and Nearby Suburbs

Gilbert covers 68.86 square miles, almost all land, and lies south of Mesa, northeast of Chandler and northwest of Queen Creek. Those three towns are the primary suburbs that border Gilbert and share similar desert‑climate living. Mesa offers a larger urban feel, Chandler is known for its tech corridor, and Queen Creek retains a more rural character. Together they form a network of communities that commuters often move between for work, school and recreation.

Population and Demographics

According to the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey (ACS) 5‑year estimates, Gilbert’s population stood at 280,262, and by 2025 it had grown to an estimated 287,285 residents, making the town the most populous incorporated municipality in the United States and the fourth‑largest city in Arizona. The median age of households is 36.1 years, a figure that underscores a relatively young, working‑age demographic compared with many other Sun‑belt suburbs.

Economic indicators point to a prosperous community: the median household income is $122,551 per year, well above the national average, while the median gross rent sits at $2,110 per month. Homeownership is strong, with roughly 73 % of occupied housing units owned rather than rented, and the median value of owner‑occupied homes is $575,100. These numbers reflect a stable residential base and a robust real‑estate market that continues to attract families and professionals alike.

The town’s demographic profile is also shaped by its geographic context. Situated southeast of Phoenix and bordered by Mesa, Chandler, and Queen Creek, Gilbert benefits from easy access to the greater Phoenix metropolitan job market while retaining a distinct suburban character. The combination of high income, youthful median age, and high home‑ownership rates contributes to a vibrant community atmosphere, supporting everything from new‑construction developments to well‑established neighborhoods throughout its 68.65 square‑mile land area.

Income and Housing Costs

The median household income in Gilbert is $122,551 per year, according to the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey. This figure is nearly double the national median and reflects the town’s strong employment base and highly educated population. With a median age of 36.1 years, many residents are in their prime earning years, which helps sustain the relatively high income level.

Housing costs in Gilbert track that prosperity. The ACS reports a median gross rent of $2,110 per month, meaning renters can expect to pay roughly $25,000 annually for a typical one‑ or two‑bedroom unit. For buyers, the median value of owner‑occupied homes sits at $575,100, a price point that aligns with the town’s 73 % owner‑occupancy rate. In other words, three‑quarters of the 280,262 people who lived in Gilbert as of the 2020‑2024 survey own their homes, reinforcing the community’s stability and long‑term investment outlook.

Prospective residents should factor these numbers into budgeting decisions. While the cost of housing is higher than in many surrounding Phoenix suburbs, the combination of high wages, a low crime rate, top‑rated schools, and abundant amenities often offsets the expense. For renters, the $2,110 median rent translates to a modest share of the average household income—about 17 %—well within the affordability guidelines used by most financial advisors. For buyers, a $575,100 median home price suggests a need for a solid down payment and stable credit, but also promises participation in a market that has consistently appreciated, making homeownership a viable long‑term investment in the thriving Gilbert suburb.

Local Attractions and Culture

Gilbert’s downtown area features mural‑clad brick walls, a color‑changing water tower and public art installations like the solo gopher and glowing benches. The town’s history is visible in sites such as Ayer’s Grocery Store, the first store opened in 1910, and the original post office from 1912. These attractions give residents a sense of place while providing easy spots for a weekend stroll.

Transportation Options

Travelers to and from Gilbert can rely on several modes of transport. Major highways such as Loop 202 and US‑60 provide direct routes to Phoenix and surrounding suburbs. Public transit includes Valley Metro bus routes that connect Gilbert with Mesa, Chandler and the broader Phoenix metropolitan area. For longer trips, the nearby Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport offers domestic and international flights.

Living in Gilbert’s Suburbs: What to Expect

Living in the suburbs around Gilbert means enjoying a hot arid climate with sweltering summers and mild winters. The town’s size—68.65 square miles of land—allows for spacious neighborhoods and plenty of green space. With a strong median income and high owner‑occupancy rate, the community tends to invest in local amenities, schools and infrastructure. Whether you prefer the bustling streets of Mesa, the tech‑focused environment of Chandler or the quieter feel of Queen Creek, each suburb offers a distinct flavor while staying within easy reach of Gilbert’s core services.

Frequently asked questions

What are the major suburbs surrounding Gilbert, Arizona?

The primary suburbs bordering Gilbert are Mesa to the north, Chandler to the west and Queen Creek to the southeast. Each offers its own mix of housing, employment and recreation while sharing Gilbert’s desert climate.

How many people live in Gilbert as of the latest census?

Gilbert’s population was estimated at 280,262 in the 2020‑2024 ACS 5‑year data, and it reached 287,285 in 2025, making it the fourth‑most populous municipality in Arizona.

When was Gilbert incorporated as a town?

Gilbert was incorporated on July 6, 1920, after beginning as a farming community supported by the railroad and irrigation canals.

What are the main attractions and cultural sites in Gilbert?

Key attractions include mural‑clad brick walls, a color‑changing water tower, public art pieces like the solo gopher and glowing benches, plus historic sites such as Ayer’s Grocery Store (1910) and the original post office (1912).

What transportation options are available for traveling to and from Gilbert?

Drivers use Loop 202 and US‑60 for quick highway access. Valley Metro bus routes connect Gilbert to neighboring cities, and Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport provides air travel options.

The bottom line

Gilbert’s suburbs blend affordable desert living with strong incomes, solid home ownership and easy access to Phoenix. By understanding the demographics, housing costs and transport links, you can decide which neighboring community best matches your needs.
