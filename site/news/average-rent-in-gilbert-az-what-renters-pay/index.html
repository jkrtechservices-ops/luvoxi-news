Average Rent in Gilbert, AZ: What Renters Pay

How the median rent compares to local income

The median gross rent of $2,110 per month translates to about 2% of the median household income of $122,551 per year. This ratio suggests that rent is a manageable portion of earnings for many residents, especially given the town’s relatively high income level. Renters should still consider other expenses, but the rent‑to‑income balance is healthier than in many larger cities.

When budgeting, factor in utilities, transportation, and any HOA fees that might apply to apartments or townhomes. Even with a modest budget, the median rent sits within reach for households earning near the median income.

What the owner‑occupied rate means for renters

About 73 % of occupied housing units in Gilbert are owner‑occupied, one of the highest home‑ownership rates in the Phoenix metropolitan area. This dominance of owners means that only roughly 27 % of the housing stock is available for rent, creating a relatively tight rental market. With a median gross rent of $2,110 per month, landlords can often command prices near the citywide median because demand outpaces supply, especially in neighborhoods that are close to top‑rated schools, major employers such as the Gilbert Regional Medical Center, and vibrant retail corridors along Greenfield and San Tomas.

The limited inventory also intensifies competition among prospective tenants. Renters who are looking for an apartment in desirable districts—like the family‑friendly Seville Village or the walkable Heritage District—may encounter multiple applicants for each unit and need to act quickly. Strong applications typically include a solid credit score, proof of steady income (the median household income in Gilbert is $122,551), and references from previous landlords.

Because the rental pool is constrained, savvy renters often broaden their search to less‑central parts of town or to adjacent communities such as Mesa or Chandler, where more units may be available at slightly lower rates. Understanding the owner‑occupied dynamic helps renters anticipate market pressure, prepare competitive paperwork, and strategically expand their geographic focus to secure the best possible deal in Gilbert’s high‑demand environment.

Age and household size factors

The median age of Gilbert residents is 36.1 years, indicating a relatively young adult population. Younger households often rent before buying, which supports a steady demand for rental properties. While the data does not break down rent by household size, the median rent figure reflects a typical one‑ to two‑bedroom unit that suits many young professionals and small families.

Renters should assess their space needs against the $2,110 median to ensure they are getting appropriate value for the size and amenities they require.

Impact of home values on rental prices

The median value of owner‑occupied homes in Gilbert is $575,100. High home values can influence landlords to set rents that cover mortgage costs, property taxes, and maintenance. This pressure helps keep the median gross rent at $2,110.

For renters, it’s useful to compare the rent to the cost of buying a comparable property. While buying may be out of reach for many, the rent figure provides a realistic benchmark for monthly housing costs in a market with $575,100 home values.

Tips for finding rent within the median range

Start by using online rental platforms that let you set a price ceiling of $2,110, the city’s median gross rent according to the 2020‑2024 American Community Survey. Filter for the specific neighborhoods you prefer, but also broaden the radius to include adjacent streets and nearby subdivisions; rent can dip a few hundred dollars below the median in older complexes or rise slightly above it in newer, amenity‑rich communities. When a listing catches your eye, reach out to the property manager or leasing office promptly—many landlords in Gilbert receive multiple inquiries, and early contact can give you a negotiating edge. Have your paperwork ready: recent pay stubs, a copy of your lease or mortgage statement, and at least two references. A complete, organized file signals reliability and often speeds up the approval process.

Timing also matters. The bulk of moves in the Phoenix metro area, including Gilbert, occur in late spring and early summer. Searching during the slower fall and winter months can find landlords more willing to trim rent or offer concessions such as a free month’s utilities, especially when vacancy rates rise. Knowing that Gilbert’s median household income is $122,551 and that roughly 73 % of housing units are owner‑occupied helps you gauge the market’s flexibility; higher‑income renters often set a ceiling that keeps median rents stable, but they also create room for competitive offers below that level. Armed with these data points, you can approach negotiations confidently, aiming for a lease that sits comfortably at or just under the $2,110 median.
