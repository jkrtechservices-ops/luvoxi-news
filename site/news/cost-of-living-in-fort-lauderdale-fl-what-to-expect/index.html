Population and Demographics

Fort Lauderdale’s estimated population stands at 185,604, according to the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey. The median age of residents is 42.8 years, indicating a mature community with many established households. This demographic mix influences local services, from healthcare to recreation, and shapes the overall demand for housing and amenities.

More than half of the occupied housing units—about 54%—are owner‑occupied, reflecting a sizable base of long‑term residents who tend to stay in the area for years rather than move frequently. This stability underpins a relatively steady rental market, where the median gross rent is $1,854 per month, and helps sustain demand for both single‑family homes and condominiums. The median value of owner‑occupied homes sits at $486,700, a price point that aligns with the city’s median household income of $83,130. Because many households are in the middle‑to‑upper‑income bracket, budgeting for housing costs becomes a central concern, especially for families and individuals approaching retirement age.

The age profile also drives consumption patterns: older residents often prioritize healthcare access, cultural activities, and low‑maintenance living spaces, while younger professionals gravitate toward vibrant nightlife and coworking environments. Consequently, city planners and local businesses tailor services to accommodate both groups, from expanding senior‑friendly medical facilities to enhancing beachfront recreation that appeals to a broader, age‑diverse clientele. This interplay of population size, income, and age creates a unique cost‑of‑living landscape that prospective movers should consider.

Income Levels and What They Mean for Your Budget

The median household income in Fort Lauderdale is $83,130 per year, based on the latest ACS 5‑year estimates. This figure provides a benchmark for evaluating affordability across the city. Households earning around the median can typically cover essential expenses, but the high cost of housing can still present challenges.

When planning a move, compare your expected earnings to this median. If your income is below $83,130, you may need to allocate a larger share of your budget to rent or mortgage payments, or consider neighborhoods with more affordable options. Conversely, households earning above the median often have more flexibility for discretionary spending, such as dining out or leisure activities.

Rental Costs in Fort Lauderdale

Renters in Fort Lauderdale face a median gross rent of $1,854 per month. This amount reflects the average price for a typical rental unit and is a useful reference point for budgeting. Rent takes up a significant portion of most households’ expenses, especially for those whose income is near the median level.

Prospective tenants should factor in additional costs such as utilities, parking, and renter’s insurance, which can add several hundred dollars to the monthly outlay. Understanding the median rent helps you gauge whether a particular apartment or house fits within your financial plan before signing a lease.

Home Ownership and Property Values

For buyers, the median value of owner‑occupied homes in Fort Lauderdale is $486,700. This price point places the city in the higher‑end range of the South Florida housing market. About 54% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating that more than half of residents choose to invest in property rather than rent.

Potential homeowners should consider the total cost of ownership, which includes mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance, and maintenance. The median home value serves as a baseline for evaluating whether a specific property aligns with your long‑term financial goals and the level of equity you aim to build over time.

Balancing Housing Choices with Income

Balancing housing costs with the median household income of $83,130 is a central consideration for anyone living in Fort Lauderdale. Renters paying $1,854 per month spend roughly 27% of a median income on housing, not accounting for other expenses. Homeowners with a median property value of $486,700 may see a larger percentage of their income go toward mortgage and related costs.

Financial planners often recommend keeping housing costs below 30% of gross income. Using the median figures, residents can assess whether renting or buying better fits their budget, and whether they need to explore more affordable neighborhoods or consider shared housing options to stay within recommended spending limits.
