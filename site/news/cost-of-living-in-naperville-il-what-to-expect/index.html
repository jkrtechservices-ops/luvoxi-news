How Much Do You Earn? Median Household Income

The U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 ACS reports a median household income of $155,105 in Naperville. This figure places the city well above the national median, indicating that many families have the financial flexibility to cover higher housing costs and discretionary spending. Earnings tend to support a lifestyle that includes quality schools, dining options, and recreational amenities without straining budgets.

For newcomers, it’s useful to compare personal earnings to this median. If household income falls below $155,105, budgeting for rent or mortgage payments may require tighter financial planning, whereas incomes above this level often allow for a more comfortable cushion.

Rent Realities: What to Expect for Housing Costs

Renters in Naperville can expect a median gross rent of $1,885 per month, according to the same ACS data set. This amount reflects the typical cost for a standard apartment or rental home in the area. While individual lease prices vary by size, location, and amenities, $1,885 serves as a reliable benchmark for budgeting.

Prospective renters should factor in utilities, parking, and possible pet fees when calculating total monthly expenses. Comparing this median rent to personal income helps determine affordability. For households earning the median $155,105, the rent represents roughly 15% of annual income, which aligns with common affordability guidelines.

Buying a Home: Median Value and Ownership Rates

The median value of owner‑occupied homes in Naperville stands at $540,200. This price point reflects a market where many families invest in long‑term residence rather than renting. With about 75% of occupied housing units owned, the city exhibits a strong ownership culture.

Potential buyers should consider down‑payment requirements, mortgage rates, and property taxes when assessing affordability. The high ownership rate suggests stable neighborhoods and potentially lower turnover, which can be appealing for families seeking continuity. For those transitioning from renting, the median rent of $1,885 can be a useful reference for estimating monthly mortgage payments.

Financing options and local lender incentives may further influence the overall cost of homeownership in Naperville.

Population Size and Age: What the Demographics Mean

Naperville’s estimated population of 150,692, coupled with a median age of 40.4 years, paints a picture of a sizable, mature community. The population size supports a broad range of services, from healthcare to retail, while the median age suggests many residents are in their prime working years or raising families.

These demographics affect everyday life. A larger population sustains diverse dining, shopping, and cultural options, while a median age of 40.4 often correlates with stable employment sectors and community involvement. Understanding these figures helps newcomers anticipate the social environment and available amenities.

Cost of Living Summary: Putting the Numbers Together

When you line up the key financial indicators—median household income of $155,105, a median gross rent of $1,885 per month, and a median owner‑occupied home value of $540,200—Naperville’s cost of living clearly sits above many Midwestern peers. However, the higher price tags are offset by the city’s robust earnings potential; the median income is roughly 45 % greater than the national median, giving residents ample wiggle room to cover housing, transportation, and discretionary expenses without stretching their budgets to the breaking point.

The housing market further underscores a community geared toward stability: about three‑quarters (75 %) of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating that most families plan to stay put for the long haul. This high ownership rate also supports stronger property‑tax revenues, which in turn fund well‑maintained schools, parks, and public services that many newcomers value.

For practical budgeting, financial planners typically advise that housing costs not exceed 30 % of gross income. In Naperville, a household earning the median $155,105 should aim to keep rent or mortgage payments around $3,878 per month, comfortably above the median rent figure and well within the range for a mortgage on a $540,200 home given current interest rates.

With a population of roughly 150,692 and a median age of 40.4 years, the city enjoys a mature, stable demographic that sustains a diverse array of amenities—from top‑rated schools to extensive recreational facilities—making the higher cost of living a trade‑off many families find worthwhile.
