Frisco vs Round Rock: Which Texas City Fits Your Lifestyle?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Frisco and Round Rock are two fast‑growing Texas cities that attract families, professionals and retirees. Frisco is larger, richer and more expensive, while Round Rock offers a lower‑cost entry point with a strong tech presence. Understanding their demographics, housing costs and local character helps you choose the community that matches your budget and lifestyle.

  • Median household income in Frisco exceeds Round Rock by $50,925.
  • Home values in Frisco are roughly 53% higher.
  • Rent consumes a smaller share of income in Frisco.
  • Frisco leans toward affluent families; Round Rock suits cost‑conscious professionals.

Population size and density

Frisco’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey (ACS) estimates a population of 219,304, while Round Rock counts 127,786 residents—an 91,518‑person gap, or roughly 72 % more people in Frisco. Spread over 62.4 square miles (61.8 sq mi of land), Frisco’s overall density works out to about 3,500 people per square mile, noticeably lower than Round Rock’s roughly 2,050 people per square mile in its more compact footprint. This larger, slightly less dense populace fuels a broader retail and entertainment mix in Frisco, from the 165‑store Stonebriar Centre and a 310,000‑sq‑ft IKEA to The Star, home of the Dallas Cowboys headquarters. By contrast, Round Rock’s tighter geography concentrates its amenities—an IKEA, Premium Outlets, and the mixed‑use La Frontera center—within a walkable “Old Town” core that feels more neighborhood‑like.

The demographic profile reinforces these differences. Frisco’s median household income sits at $150,212, about 51 % higher than Round Rock’s $99,287, and 66 % of its housing units are owner‑occupied versus 56 % in Round Rock. Home values reflect the gap: the median owner‑occupied home in Frisco is $642,100, roughly $223,500 (53 %) above Round Rock’s $418,600. Rent follows a similar pattern, with Frisco’s median gross rent at $2,014 per month (about 16 % of its median income) compared with $1,763 in Round Rock (about 21 % of its median income). These numbers suggest that Frisco offers a larger, more affluent market with a wider variety of services, while Round Rock provides a smaller, more tightly knit community that may feel more affordable and personable for those who prefer a compact city vibe.

Median household income and what it means

The median household income in Frisco is $150,212, compared with $99,287 in Round Rock. Frisco’s median is $50,925, or about 51%, higher than Round Rock’s. Higher earnings in Frisco translate into greater discretionary spending power, supporting upscale shopping at Stonebriar Centre and the Dallas Cowboys headquarters at The Star. Round Rock’s income level still ranks well above the national average, bolstered by Dell’s headquarters and a growing tech sector, but it reflects a more modest purchasing capacity.

Housing costs: rent and home values

Renters in Frisco pay a median gross rent of $2,014 per month, while Round Rock’s median is $1,763. The Frisco rent is $251, about 14%, higher than Round Rock’s. However, rent in Frisco equals roughly 16% of its median household income, whereas Round Rock’s rent represents about 21% of its median income, indicating a tighter budget stretch for renters in Round Rock.

Homeownership follows a similar pattern. The median value of owner‑occupied homes in Frisco is $642,100, compared with $418,600 in Round Rock. Frisco’s median home price is $223,500, or about 53%, higher than Round Rock’s. Despite the higher price, 66% of occupied housing units in Frisco are owner‑occupied, versus 56% in Round Rock, suggesting a stronger home‑ownership culture in Frisco.

Affordability snapshot

Affordability hinges on both income and cost. Frisco’s higher incomes offset its steeper rent and home prices, leaving rent at 16% of household income. Round Rock’s lower incomes mean rent consumes a larger slice—21%—of earnings. Homebuyers in Frisco will need a larger mortgage to afford the $642,100 median home, while Round Rock buyers face a more modest $418,600 price tag. For families prioritizing lower housing expenses, Round Rock is the cheaper place to live, but Frisco offers more purchasing power to cover those costs.

What each city is known for

Frisco is recognized for rapid growth—fastest‑growing U.S. city in 2017 and the 2000‑2009 decade—and for being a Tree City USA since 2003. Its attractions include the 165‑store Stonebriar Centre, a massive IKEA showroom and The Star, the Dallas Cowboys headquarters. The city’s location 25 miles from Dallas Love Field and DFW International Airport adds travel convenience.

Round Rock is part of the Greater Austin metro area and sits on the Balcones Escarpment, offering a mix of flat prairie and hill country terrain. It hosts Dell’s international headquarters, a major tech employer, and retail hubs like IKEA, Premium Outlets and La Frontera. Historic “Old Town” preserves early Texas architecture, reflecting its 1850s roots.

Who each city suits best

Frisco appeals to affluent families and professionals who value high‑income potential, upscale amenities and a suburban feel with easy access to Dallas. Its higher home‑ownership rate and extensive retail and entertainment options make it attractive for those seeking a polished, growth‑oriented environment.

Round Rock fits cost‑conscious professionals, especially in tech, who appreciate a lower cost of living, proximity to Austin’s cultural scene, and a blend of historic charm with modern retail. The city’s moderate rent‑to‑income ratio and more affordable home prices make it a practical choice for first‑time buyers and families prioritizing budget.

Frequently asked questions

Is Frisco or Round Rock cheaper to live in?

Round Rock is cheaper overall. Its median rent is $1,763 versus $2,014 in Frisco, and its median home value is $418,600 compared with $642,100 in Frisco.

Which has higher incomes, Frisco or Round Rock?

Frisco has higher incomes. The median household income is $150,212, which is $50,925 more than Round Rock’s $99,287.

Is rent higher in Frisco or Round Rock?

Rent is higher in Frisco. The median gross rent is $2,014 per month, while Round Rock’s median rent is $1,763.

Which is better for families, Frisco or Round Rock?

Frisco generally suits families seeking higher incomes, extensive amenities and a strong home‑ownership culture. Round Rock works well for families who prioritize lower housing costs and a smaller‑city feel near Austin.

Is Frisco or Round Rock bigger?

Frisco is bigger. Its population of 219,304 is about 91,518, or 72%, higher than Round Rock’s 127,786 residents.

The bottom line

Frisco offers higher earnings and upscale amenities at a premium price, while Round Rock delivers a more affordable cost of living with a strong tech presence. Your choice should hinge on budget, desired lifestyle and proximity to Dallas or Austin.
