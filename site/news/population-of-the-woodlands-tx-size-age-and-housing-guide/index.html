Population Size and Recent Growth

The Woodlands recorded 114,436 people in the 2020 census. Updated ACS 5‑year estimates (2020‑2024) raise that figure to 121,002, reflecting modest growth over the past few years. The community is part of the Woodlands‑Conroe urban area, which totaled 402,454 residents in 2020, ranking it the 103rd‑largest urban area in the United States. This steady increase underscores the appeal of the master‑planned community within the Greater Houston region.

Land Area and Geographic Context

Covering 43.9 square miles (113.6 km2) in total, The Woodlands includes 43.3 square miles (112.1 km2) of land and 0.58 square miles (1.5 km2) of water. The community lies primarily in Montgomery County, with a small portion extending into Harris County, and sits 28 miles (45 km) north of downtown Houston along Interstate 45. Its special‑purpose district status gives it a unique governance structure compared with surrounding municipalities.

Income Levels and Housing Costs

The median household income in The Woodlands stands at $140,701 per year, well above the national median. Owner‑occupied homes dominate the market, with roughly 73% of occupied housing units owned by residents. The median gross rent is $1,822 per month, while the median value of owner‑occupied homes is $511,700. These figures illustrate a community with strong purchasing power and a preference for homeownership, supported by relatively high earnings and stable employment opportunities.

Education Attainment and Workforce Profile

Educational achievement is a defining characteristic of The Woodlands’ population. According to the 2020‑2024 American Community Survey, 97.8 % of residents age 25 and older have earned a high‑school diploma, and a striking 64.3 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. This concentration of college‑educated adults fuels a robust labor market that includes corporate campuses for Occidental Petroleum, Chevron Phillips Chemical, Halliburton, and other Fortune‑500 firms. The high level of credentialing translates directly into earnings: the median household income stands at $140,701, while per‑capita income remains at $67,290, both well above national averages. Consequently, poverty is rare—only 3.9 % of the population fell below the federal poverty line in 2020.

The workforce profile is further shaped by the community’s age structure. With a median age of 40.6 years, The Woodlands is home to a mature, established cohort that balances professional experience with family stability. Homeownership is prevalent, with roughly 73 % of occupied housing units owned rather than rented, reflecting the area’s financial strength. The median value of owner‑occupied homes is $511,700, and median gross rent is $1,822 per month, underscoring the affluent nature of the market. Together, these factors—high educational attainment, strong incomes, low poverty, and solid homeownership—create a distinctive socioeconomic environment that supports both residential quality of life and a thriving corporate sector.

Corporate Campuses and Economic Base

The Woodlands’ economic engine is anchored by a cluster of high‑profile corporate campuses that not only employ thousands of professionals but also shape the community’s housing market and overall affluence. Occidental Petroleum, Chevron Phillips Chemical, and Halliburton each maintain expansive office complexes on the 43.9‑square‑mile master‑planned site, drawing a mix of senior executives, engineers, and support staff who often choose to live nearby. Because many of these firms offer corporate‑housing programs or partner with local developers, The Woodlands provides a seamless blend of upscale apartments, townhomes, and single‑family residences tailored to both short‑term assignments and long‑term family life. This integration helps sustain the area’s striking median household income of $140,701 and contributes to a low poverty rate of just 3.9 %.

Owner‑occupied units dominate the housing stock—about 73 % of occupied dwellings—reflecting the community’s stability and the high value placed on homeownership. The median value of owner‑occupied homes stands at $511,700, while median gross rent is $1,822 per month, figures that align with the affluent, highly educated populace (64.3 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher). With a median age of 40.6 years, residents are typically in peak earning years, reinforcing the economic resilience of the Greater Houston metropolitan area. The presence of these corporate headquarters, coupled with The Woodlands Township’s proactive governance, ensures a diversified tax base and continued investment in infrastructure, making the community a premier destination for both businesses and families.
