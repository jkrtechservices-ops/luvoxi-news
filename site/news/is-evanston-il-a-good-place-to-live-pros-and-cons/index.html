Is Evanston, IL a Good Place to Live? Pros and Cons

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Nov 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Evanston, Illinois offers a mix of lakefront charm, a strong university presence, and progressive sustainability efforts. With a population of about 76,340, median incomes near $96,434 and a median home value of $481,200, the city balances affordability with access to Chicago. Its hot‑summer humid continental climate brings four distinct seasons, and public transit links residents to the city and O'Hare Airport. Below are the key pros and cons to help you determine if Evanston is a good place to live.

Cost of Living and Housing

Evanston’s median gross rent sits at $1,762 per month, while the median value of owner‑occupied homes is $481,200. About 56% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, indicating a stable homeowner base. The median household income of $96,434 helps offset these costs for many residents. Compared with nearby Chicago neighborhoods, Evanston’s housing market offers a blend of rentals and homes that can suit a range of budgets, though prices remain higher than some suburban alternatives.

Education and University Influence

Northwestern University, founded in 1851, is the intellectual engine of Evan ston and shapes nearly every facet of city life. With a student body of roughly 22,000 undergraduates and graduate scholars, the campus pumps a steady stream of young, educated residents into the community, helping keep the median age at a youthful 38.4 years. The university’s research budget—billions of dollars annually—spills over into local schools through formal partnerships, mentorship programs, and shared facilities, giving K‑12 teachers access to cutting‑edge science labs and guest lecturers. As a result, Evan ston’s public schools consistently rank above state averages, a draw for families who earn a median household income of $96,434 and are willing to invest in quality education.

Beyond the classroom, Northwestern’s cultural offerings are open to the public: free concerts at the Lakefill, art exhibitions at the Mary and Leigh Block Museum, and public lectures that attract residents of all ages. These events enrich a city already known for its vibrant lakefront parks and walkable streets. The university also fuels the local economy; roughly 56 % of housing units are owner‑occupied, with a median home value of $481,200, while the median gross rent of $1,762 supports a robust rental market for students and faculty alike. In short, Northwestern’s presence creates a dynamic, well‑educated community that underpins Evan ston’s reputation as a desirable place to live.

Transportation and Connectivity

Evanston’s transportation network is a major draw for professionals and families who value easy access to Chicago without the daily hassle of driving. The city sits just 12 miles north of the Loop, and the Metra Union Pacific/North Line delivers a fast, reliable commuter‑rail service that shuttles residents to downtown in roughly 25 minutes, with trains running at 15‑minute intervals during peak hours. Complementing the rail line, the Chicago Transit Authority operates several bus routes that not only weave through Evanston’s neighborhoods but also connect directly to O’Hare International Airport, making both domestic and international travel convenient for the city’s median‑age‑38.4 population.

For cyclists and pedestrians, the city’s extensive bike lanes and the popular Lakefront Trail provide safe, car‑free corridors that link residential areas to downtown, the university campus, and the lakefront parks. Because 56 % of housing units are owner‑occupied and the median household income sits at $96,434, many residents can afford the modest $1,762 average rent or the $481,200 median home price while still opting for public transit, which helps lower household transportation costs and reduces traffic congestion.

Evanston’s commitment to sustainability further enhances its connectivity. After adopting the Climate Action Plan in 2008 and winning the 2011 Mayors’ Climate Protection Award, the city has expanded electric‑vehicle charging stations and introduced “green” bus fleets, reinforcing a transit‑oriented lifestyle that appeals to environmentally conscious commuters.

Climate and Sustainability Initiatives

The city experiences a hot‑summer humid continental climate (Köppen Dfa) with four distinct seasons, offering warm lake breezes in summer and snowy winters. Evanston has been recognized for its climate action, signing the United States Conference of Mayors Climate Protection Agreement in October 2006. The 2008 Climate Action Plan introduced over 200 sustainability strategies, and in June 2011 the city won first place in the small‑city category of the Mayors' Climate Protection Awards, cutting emissions by 24,000 metric tons per year.

Community Diversity and Business Landscape

Evanston’s business community reflects growing diversity. In 2012, 12.3% of its 9,259 businesses were Black‑owned, and by 2017, women owned 24% of the city’s 2,041 employer firms. This mix of ownership contributes to a varied local economy, supporting everything from independent shops to larger retailers. The city’s progressive policies and emphasis on inclusion foster a welcoming atmosphere for entrepreneurs and residents alike.
