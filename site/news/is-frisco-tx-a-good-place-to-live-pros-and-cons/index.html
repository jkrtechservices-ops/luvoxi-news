Is Frisco, TX a Good Place to Live? Pros and Cons

Frisco, Texas blends rapid growth with high household incomes and a range of shopping, dining and sports venues. Its location near Dallas airports, a humid subtropical climate and a reputation for family‑friendly development make it attractive, yet tornado risk and pricey rent are factors to weigh.

Population Size and Growth Trends

Frisco’s population continues to surge, underscoring its appeal within the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex. The U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020‑2024 American Community Survey estimates the city at 219,304 residents, up from the 200,509 counted in the 2020 decennial census. This growth reflects a broader trend that began in the early 2000s—Frisco was the nation’s fastest‑growing city from 2000 to 2009 and again topped growth charts in 2017. The influx of new households has helped shape a housing market where 66 % of occupied units are owner‑occupied, with a median home value of $642,100 and a median gross rent of $2,014 per month.

The demographic profile is relatively young and affluent: the median age is 38.3 years, and households earn an average of $150,212 annually, well above the national median. Frisco’s expansion is not confined to city limits; it anchors the McKinney‑Frisco urban area, which recorded 504,803 people in the 2020 census, ranking 83rd among U.S. urban areas. Spanning 62.4 square miles (61.8 sq mi land, 0.58 sq mi water), the city’s density remains moderate, allowing for continued development of residential neighborhoods, schools, and amenities while preserving green space—a factor highlighted by its ongoing Tree City USA designation since 2003. This rapid yet managed growth positions Frisco as a dynamic, high‑quality place to call home.

Economic Strength and Housing Costs

A median household income of $150,212 places Frisco well above the national average, indicating strong earning power among residents. However, that prosperity translates into higher housing costs. The median gross rent stands at $2,014 per month, while the median value of owner‑occupied homes is $642,100. About 66% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, suggesting a stable homeowner base despite the price levels. These figures help prospective movers gauge affordability relative to income potential.

Climate, Weather Patterns and Tornado Risk

Frisco experiences a humid subtropical climate, receiving roughly 39 inches of precipitation annually and enjoying about 230 sunny days each year. July highs average 96 °F (36 °C) and January lows average 33 °F (1 °C), offering warm summers and mild winters. The city sits in Tornado Alley; an EF‑0 tornado touched down near the UNT Frisco Campus in 2024, underscoring the need for preparedness during storm season. Residents typically plan for both heat and occasional severe weather.

Retail, Entertainment and Lifestyle Options

Frisco’s shopping and entertainment scene includes the 165‑store Stonebriar Centre regional mall and a 310,000‑square‑foot IKEA store, providing a wide range of retail choices. The Star, home to the Dallas Cowboys headquarters, adds sports‑related attractions and event spaces. Preston Road runs north‑south through the city, lined with restaurants, boutiques and service businesses, creating convenient access to daily needs and leisure activities. These amenities contribute to a lifestyle that many families find appealing.

Transportation Access and Regional Connectivity

Located just 25 miles from both Dallas Love Field and Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport, Frisco’s proximity to two major hubs makes air travel remarkably convenient for its 219,304 residents. Business travelers can reach either airport in roughly 30‑45 minutes, while weekend getaways are equally hassle‑free.

The city sits at the crossroads of several key highways—U.S. 75 (Central Expressway), State Highway 121, and the Dallas North Tollway—providing swift north‑south and east‑west connections to downtown Dallas, Plano, and the wider DFW metroplex. For commuters, these thoroughfares help keep average rush‑hour travel times competitive, especially for the city’s median‑income households (median household income $150,212).

Public transit, though more limited than in central Dallas, is expanding. The DART commuter rail’s Trinity Railway Express (TRE) reaches the nearby Plano Station, and the Collin County Transit (CCT) offers bus routes that link Frisco’s commercial corridors—such as Preston Road and The Star—to neighboring suburbs and employment centers.

Freight and logistics also benefit from the city’s strategic location. The Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport’s cargo facilities and the extensive interstate network support the region’s thriving retail sector, which includes the 165‑store Stonebriar Centre and a 310,000‑square‑foot IKEA.

Overall, Frisco’s blend of highway access, growing transit options, and airport proximity creates a transportation ecosystem that supports both daily commuting and long‑distance travel, reinforcing its appeal for professionals, families, and visitors alike.

Frequently asked questions

Frisco’s estimated population is 219,304 according to the 2020‑2024 ACS 5‑year estimates, up from 200,509 recorded in the 2020 census.

Frisco lies about 25 miles from both Dallas Love Field and Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport, providing convenient access for travelers.

What is the climate like in Frisco, including tornado risk?

Frisco has a humid subtropical climate with 39 inches of annual precipitation and 230 sunny days. July highs average 96 °F, January lows 33 °F, and the city experienced an EF‑0 tornado near the UNT Frisco Campus in 2024.

The bottom line

Frisco, TX offers strong economic indicators, abundant amenities and convenient airport access, making it attractive for many families and professionals. Pros include high income levels and a wide range of retail and entertainment options; cons involve higher rent, home prices and the occasional tornado threat. Weigh these factors against your budget and lifestyle priorities to determine if Frisco fits your definition of a good place to live.
