Plano vs Arlington: Which City Fits Your Lifestyle?

By Luvoxi News Editorial Team · Oct 21, 2026 · 5 min read

Plano and Arlington are two of Texas' fastest‑growing cities, each offering a distinct mix of size, earnings, housing costs and lifestyle. Plano tops the list for higher incomes and larger homes, while Arlington provides a younger population and lower rent. Knowing these differences helps you decide which city matches your budget and life stage.

Key takeaways
  • Plano earns about 49% more than Arlington.
  • Arlington rent is roughly $371 cheaper per month.
  • Home values in Plano exceed Arlington by $161,200.
  • Arlington has a younger median age and larger population.

Population size and density

Arlington is the larger of the two, with an estimated population of 397,742, making it about 107,148 (roughly 37%) more residents than Plano's 290,594. Arlington ranks as the third‑largest city in the Dallas–Fort Worth metro area, while Plano sits ninth in Texas. Both cities spread over sizable land areas, but Arlington’s greater headcount translates to a busier urban feel, whereas Plano’s smaller population supports a more suburban atmosphere.

Median household income and what it means

Plano’s median household income stands at $112,253, which is $37,082 (about 49%) higher than Arlington’s $75,171. Higher earnings in Plano often reflect the presence of corporate headquarters such as Frito‑Lay, JCPenney, Pizza Hut and Toyota Motor North America. Arlington’s income level, while lower, aligns with its younger median age of 33.7 years, suggesting many residents are early‑career professionals or students at the University of Texas at Arlington.

Rent costs and affordability ratios

The median gross rent in Plano is $1,841 per month, about $371 (25%) higher than Arlington’s $1,470. When expressed as a share of household income, Plano renters spend roughly 20% of their earnings on rent, while Arlington renters allocate about 23%. Both percentages sit within a generally affordable range, but Arlington’s lower rent may appeal to those on tighter budgets or seeking more disposable income.

Home values and ownership rates

Owner‑occupied homes in Plano have a median value of $465,900, which is $161,200 (about 53 %) higher than Arlington’s median of $304,700. This price gap reflects the broader economic context: Plano’s median household income is $112,253, nearly $37,000 (49 %) above Arlington’s $75,171, and its median gross rent of $1,841 per month is $371 (25 %) higher than Arlington’s $1,470. Despite the higher cost of buying, the ownership rate in Plano remains only modestly higher—57 % of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied versus 54 % in Arlington. The slightly greater propensity to own in Plano suggests a market that attracts higher‑income families who can afford the premium, while still leaving room for renters and first‑time buyers.

Arlington’s lower home values and comparable ownership rate make it a more entry‑level market for prospective buyers. With a median age of 33.7 years—about six years younger than Plano’s 39.6—Arlington’s population is younger and often earlier in the home‑buying lifecycle. The city’s larger population (397,742 versus Plano’s 290,594) provides a broader pool of housing options, from modest starter homes to newer developments aimed at first‑time owners. In sum, Plano’s higher home values and modestly higher ownership percentage point to a more affluent, established homeowner base, while Arlington offers more affordable pathways to ownership for those beginning their real‑estate journey.

What each city is known for

Plano is best known as a thriving corporate hub within the Dallas‑Fort Worth metroplex. Home to the headquarters of Fortune‑500 giants such as Frito‑Lay, JCPenney, Pizza Hut and Toyota Motor North America, the city’s economy is anchored by high‑paying professional jobs, which is reflected in its median household income of $112,253—about 49 % higher than Arlington’s. With a population of 290,594, Plano feels more like an upscale suburb than a bustling city; the median age of 39.6 years points to a mature, family‑oriented community. Housing costs follow the income trend: the median gross rent is $1,841 per month (roughly 20 % of median income) and the median value of owner‑occupied homes sits at $465,900, about 53 % above Arlington’s. Approximately 57 % of occupied units are owner‑occupied, underscoring a stable, investment‑heavy market.

Arlington, by contrast, is the entertainment and sports powerhouse of the region. The city’s 397,742 residents enjoy a younger demographic (median age 33.7) and a more affordable cost of living, with a median household income of $75,171 and median rent of $1,470—about 23 % of income. Home values average $304,700, and 54 % of housing units are owner‑occupied. Arlington’s identity is shaped by its world‑class venues: Globe Life Field hosts the Texas Rangers, AT&T Stadium is home to the Dallas Cowboys, and the University of Texas at Arlington fuels a vibrant college‑town atmosphere. This mix of sports, concerts, and a sizable student population creates a lively, nightlife‑rich culture that stands in stark contrast to Plano’s corporate‑centric, suburban vibe.

Who each city suits best

Plano’s higher incomes, larger homes and family‑friendly suburbs make it ideal for established professionals, dual‑income families and those seeking a stable, upscale environment. Arlington’s younger median age, lower rent and vibrant entertainment scene attract early‑career individuals, students and active singles who value nightlife and sports events. Both cities have strong owner‑occupancy, but the cost gap tilts the decision toward budget considerations and lifestyle preferences.

Is rent higher in Plano or Arlington?

Which is better for families, Plano or Arlington?

Plano generally suits families better because of its higher incomes, larger median home values and a suburban setting that supports family life.

The bottom line

Plano offers higher earnings and larger homes for families willing to pay a premium, while Arlington delivers lower rent, a younger vibe and major sports attractions. Match your budget, career stage and lifestyle preferences to choose the city that feels like home.
