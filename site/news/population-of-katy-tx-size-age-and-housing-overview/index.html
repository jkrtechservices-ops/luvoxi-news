How many people live in Katy, TX?

The most recent Census Bureau estimate for Katy’s population comes from the 2020‑2024 American Community Survey (ACS) 5‑year data, which places the city at 25,184 residents. This figure captures the incorporated city that straddles the tripoint of Harris, Fort Bend, and Waller counties and reflects steady growth since the 2020 decennial census, which counted 21,894 people. Spread across 15.312 square miles (39.66 km²) of total area—virtually all land—Katy’s population density has risen alongside its expanding housing market.

Katy’s demographic profile is relatively young, with a median age of 37.6 years, indicating a sizable portion of families and working‑age adults. Economic indicators show a prosperous community: the median household income stands at $114,912, while the median gross rent is $1,602 per month. Homeownership is dominant, with about 81 % of occupied housing units owned by residents; the median value of owner‑occupied homes is $401,700.

Geographically, Katy lies 29 miles west of downtown Houston and 22 miles east of Sealy, positioning it as the hub of the Greater Katy urban area within the larger Greater Houston metropolitan region. Its location, combined with a humid subtropical climate (Köppen Cfa), makes it an attractive suburb for commuters and families seeking a blend of proximity to the city and a distinct, community‑focused lifestyle.

What is the physical size of Katy, TX?

Katy covers a total area of 15.312 square miles, according to the United States Census Bureau. Of that, 15.294 square miles is land and a tiny 0.018 square miles is water. The city’s compact footprint means most neighborhoods and commercial districts are within a short drive of each other, making it easy to navigate by car or bike.

What are the age and income characteristics of Katy residents?

The median age of Katy residents is 37.6 years, a figure that places the community squarely in the prime‑working‑age bracket. This relatively young demographic is reflected in the high proportion of families with school‑age children, which in turn drives demand for quality K‑12 schools, parks, and family‑oriented recreation. Coupled with the youthful profile is a robust earnings landscape: the 2020‑2024 American Community Survey reports a median household income of $114,912, well above the national median. Such income levels translate into solid purchasing power, encouraging a vibrant mix of upscale retailers, diverse dining options, and professional services throughout the city.

Housing data reinforce the picture of an affluent, owner‑focused community. Approximately 81 % of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, with the median value of those homes at $401,700. Renters face a median gross rent of $1,602 per month, indicating a competitive rental market that still remains accessible for many young professionals and families. The combination of high homeownership rates, substantial median home values, and strong household incomes suggests that Katy residents enjoy considerable financial stability, which supports ongoing investment in local infrastructure, schools, and community amenities. This economic vitality helps maintain Katy’s reputation as a desirable suburb within the Greater Houston metropolitan area, attracting new residents who seek both a family‑friendly environment and the convenience of proximity to Houston’s employment centers.

How does housing break down in Katy, TX?

Housing in Katy leans heavily toward ownership. About 81% of occupied housing units are owner‑occupied, while the remaining units are rented. The median value of owner‑occupied homes is $401,700, reflecting the city’s suburban appeal and proximity to Houston. For renters, the median gross rent stands at $1,602 per month, a figure that aligns with the area’s overall cost of living.

These numbers help prospective buyers and renters gauge what to expect financially when moving to Katy.

Where is Katy located and what are its historic boundaries?

Katy sits at the tripoint of Harris, Fort Bend and Waller counties, roughly 29 miles west of downtown Houston and 22 miles east of Sealy. The city’s historic core, known as Old Towne Katy, was incorporated in 1945. Its boundaries run just south of Kingsland Boulevard, cross Interstate 10 to Morton Road, and are flanked by Katy Fort Bend Road on the east and Cane Island Creek on the west. This area retains many of the original streetscapes and serves as a cultural anchor for the larger community.

Katy, TX blends modest size with strong economic indicators. With a growing population, a median age of 37.6, high home ownership and solid income levels, the city offers a stable environment for families and professionals alike.
